Where the AI is

A transparent rule in the cab, learning models behind it

Controller: linear headway rule

hold = ½ × (gap behind − gap ahead). Field-tested in Atlanta and Chicago. 30-second steps, 60–120 s cap, shown only while stationary.

Forecasts: LightGBM

Predicts when the bus behind will arrive from link times, hour and weekday. We show its error next to the timetable-based estimate.

Assignment: OR-Tools

Ranks add-a-bus options by empty drive time against passenger-minutes saved. The dispatcher decides.

Reinforcement learning stays in the appendix: it beats a tuned rule by seconds in simulation, and the one real pilot ran it inside rule-based guardrails.

12 · Bartholdi & Eisenstein 2012; Xuan, Argote & Daganzo 2011; CTA 2022–23 pilot (Rodriguez et al. 2025)

Judges will ask where the AI is, so here is the honest answer. The instruction the driver sees comes from a transparent linear rule, because that is what has worked in the field. The learning models sit behind it: a LightGBM model forecasts when the bus behind will arrive, and we show its error against the timetable estimate. OR-Tools ranks the add-a-bus options. Reinforcement learning is in our appendix as an offline experiment; it beats a tuned rule by seconds in simulation, and Chicago’s one real pilot ran it inside rule-based guardrails.
