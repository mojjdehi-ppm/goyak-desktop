What Baku already has

Baku tracks its buses and measures intervals. Nobody closes the loop.

Already in place

  • GPS and a Kentkart on-board unit in every route bus, mandatory since 1 Jan 2026
  • AYNA’s monitoring centre sees positions and intervals and warns carriers
  • BakuBus dispatchers release buses from terminals on the timetable
  • Interval is a penalised KPI under the new gross-cost contracts

Done by no one, in any public source

  • Computing the gap to the bus ahead and the bus behind in real time
  • A timed instruction to the driver at the stop
  • A published regularity figure for a single route
  • Spotting a gap before it opens and filling it

05 · Sources: Kentkart product documentation; AYNA and BakuBus statements (2025–26); Qafqazinfo, Oct 2026; Cabinet Decision No. 250

Before we show the product: what already exists. Every route bus in Baku carries GPS and a Kentkart on-board unit; the driver’s screen shows deviation from the timetable. AYNA’s monitoring centre sees intervals and warns carriers. BakuBus dispatchers release buses from terminals. And since September, interval regularity is a penalised contract KPI. What no public source shows is anyone computing the gap between two buses in real time and turning it into a timed instruction for the driver, or publishing a regularity figure for any route. That loop is what Ritm adds.
