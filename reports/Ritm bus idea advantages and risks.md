# Keep Ritm's Headway Loop, Cut the Rest

*Advisor's memo to the Ritm team, "İnsanyönümlü şəhərlər" hackathon (Youth Foundation + National AI Centre, Nov–Dec 2026). Evidence tags: [field] = measured in real operations; [simulation] = model only; [vendor] = vendor-authored claim; [agency] = agency document; [press] = news report; [inference] = our reading of the evidence.*

## 1. Bottom line

Proceed, but not as pitched. Ritm's core, a headway-based "earliest departure" instruction shown after boarding is complete, attacks a problem Baku measures but does not control: BakuBus's own monitoring centre recorded **17-minute intervals on route 2 against a planned 7–8 and 22 minutes on route 7A (35 buses) against a planned 4–5** in October 2026 ([Qafqazinfo](https://qafqazinfo.az/news/detail/insanlarin-gunu-yollarda-kecir-30-40-deqiqe-avtobus-gozleyirik-video-524436)), and no public source shows any Baku back end computing inter-bus headway or pushing hold advice to drivers; every documented correction is planning-level (more buses, re-routing), terminal-level (a dispatcher releasing departures) or after the fact (AYNA warning the carrier) ([Report.az, 2021](https://report.az/infrastruktur/bna-sernisinler-avtobuslarin-hereketini-izleye-bilecekler); [Bakues.az, Sep 2026](https://bakues.az/bakubus-10-nomreli-avtobusla-bagli-naraziliga-aydinliq-getirdi-video-yenilenib/)). The method is field-proven at modest magnitudes: **26% less excess waiting at WMATA** [field] ([Soza-Parra et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0361198119845369)), **5.7–8.7% less average waiting on two Chicago CTA routes, 19.9% in one evening peak, with only 35–57% driver compliance and 15–25% of trips cancelled** [field] ([Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)), and the elimination of sub-2.5-minute headways at Georgia Tech, where the line re-equalised by itself after a bus was removed [field] ([Bartholdi & Eisenstein 2012](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/papers/Bartholdi-Eisenstein-2011.pdf)). Three parts of the current pitch will not survive an informed judge. First, Baku drivers already have a Kentkart on-board unit whose stated purpose is "trip runtime information" for "timekeeping", so the "+1:54" on the route-172 screen is almost certainly schedule deviation, and no third party can write to that screen without K Group, the single fare-system gateway ([Kentkart](https://www.kentkart.com/on-board-unit/)). Second, "Crowd Forecast" without tap-in or passenger-counter data is a static profile, not a forecast, and AYNA is buying exactly that analytics layer in a **3,046,381.90-AZN "AI Platform and Public Transport Network Diagnostics" tender that closes on 24 November 2026** ([Fins.az](https://fins.az/az/biznes/ayna-dan-3-milyon-manatliq-tender); [BidDetail](https://www.biddetail.com/latest-tenders/119738634$0e0f32e7-e292-43d5-8f5f-5ce32855126c)). Third, a central pool of ~20 reserve buses does not exist in any source, is paid for on every day it is not used, and collides with a driver shortage and a gross-cost regime that would have to buy standby capacity explicitly ([SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)). Commercially, no Azerbaijani startup has a documented paid pilot with AYNA, BakuBus or Baku Metro; hackathon rewards top out at 5,000 AZN and laptops; and only **3.55% of hackathon projects show any activity five months after the event** ([Nolte et al. 2020](https://alexandernolte.github.io/download/Nolte%20et%20al.%20(2020)%20What%20Happens%20to%20All%20These%20Hackathon%20Projects%20-%20Identifying%20Factors%20to%20Promote%20Hackathon%20Project%20Continuation.pdf)). The recommendation is to shrink Ritm to a terminal-first headway controller for corridors with headways of ten minutes or less, running on a commodity tablet alongside the Kentkart unit, with a headway-derived crowding index and an "add-a-bus" advisor as supporting modules; to claim **5–15% excess-wait reduction at realistic compliance** instead of the 50–77% that idealised simulations produce; to position it as a module for AYNA's new platform and a compliance aid under gross-cost contracts rather than a rival platform; and to ask the judges for one route, one terminus tablet, eight weeks and a data agreement.

## 2. Baku already tracks buses and measures intervals; nobody closes the loop

Three layers of control exist today, and Ritm must be described against all three. At the top, AYNA's Monitoring Centre (the former BNA "Vahid Dispetçer Mərkəzi") watches GPS positions and intervals on every BakıKart-connected route; BNA said in October 2021 that once a route is integrated "we obtain the number of buses that went out on the line that day, their real-time movement status, where they are, at what interval and speed they move" ([Report.az, Oct 2021](https://report.az/infrastruktur/bna-sernisinler-avtobuslarin-hereketini-izleye-bilecekler)). That layer acts on carriers, not drivers: the standard response to an interval complaint is an instruction or warning to the company, as on route 49 in October 2025, routes 202/45 in November 2025 and the stop-skipping cases on routes 14/3/17 in February 2026 ([Oxu.az](https://oxu.az/cemiyyet/49-sayli-marsrutdan-sikayet-surucu-qapilari-baglaya-bilmir); [Icma.az](https://icma.az/news/ayna-bezi-avtobuslarin-dasiyicilarina-xeberdarliq-edecek-1255230); [Avtosfer](https://avtosfer.az/az/gundem/bu-dayanacaqda-saxlamayan-avtobus-suruculerine-xeberdarliq-edilib-ayna-dan-aciqlama-318962)), and AYNA's chairman admitted in September 2025 that the agency "has no mechanism to discipline carriers for quality" until gross-cost contracts spread ([Report.az, Sep 2025](https://report.az/infrastruktur/anar-rzayev-ayna-nin-elinde-dasiyicilari-tenbeh-elemek-mexanizmi-yoxdur)). In the middle sits BakuBus, the only operator with a demonstrated closed loop: its "Onlayn Monitorinq Mərkəzi" reports measured against planned intervals per route and day, and during the September 2026 Formula 1 closures "the departure of buses at the terminal stop was managed by the dispatcher based on the regulation of the Online Monitoring Centre" ([Bakues.az](https://bakues.az/bakubus-10-nomreli-avtobusla-bagli-naraziliga-aydinliq-getirdi-video-yenilenib/)); its vacancy notices describe a "dəstə rəhbəri" (team leader) who keeps "the movement schedule and interval of buses on the line under control" and "coordinates regularly with drivers" ([FED.az](https://fed.az/az/vakansiyalar/quotbaku-busquot-coxsayli-isciler-axtarir-vakansi%CC%87yalar-230475)). At the bottom, private carriers run in-house terminal dispatchers and, when asked about delays, cite bus counts and traffic rather than any intervention ([Bakupost](https://www.bakupost.az/29-ve-65-sayli-avtobuslar-niye-yubanir-ayna-dan-reaksiya)).

The hardware on the bus is more capable than the team's pitch assumes. The validator and driver screen are one Kentkart-family system operated locally by K Group MMC, the BakıKart operator that Mastercard calls "the local system integrator and transit technology provider" for ~60 million journeys a month ([Mastercard, Mar 2026](https://www.mastercard.com/news/eemea/en/newsroom/press-releases/en/2026/february/mastercard-and-bakikart-s-open-loop-transit-ecosystem-shortlisted-at-transport-ticketing-global-awards-2026/)). Kentkart's on-board unit "provides drivers with trip runtime information and enables the communication between the vehicle and the dispatching center. With its GPS and GPRS connectivity as well as adjustable voice reminders, Kentkart On-Board Computer facilitates timekeeping for the drivers" [vendor] ([Kentkart On-Board Unit](https://www.kentkart.com/on-board-unit/)), and its application datasheet promises "visual and aural driver warnings" and "a seamless communication interface between drivers and dispatchers" [vendor] ([Kentkart OBU app PDF](https://www.kentkart.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/obu-app.pdf)). The initial Baku scope was 300 OBUs on BakuBus buses around 2015, integrated with LED panels, speakers and the existing cameras ([Kentkart Baku presentation](https://prezi.com/2y0zvzwsv2cw/kentkart-baku/)), and from **1 January 2026 every bus on a Baku regular route must carry GPS, an e-payment device, an on-board unit streaming movement and fare data to a "central dispatcher system", and an automatic passenger counter, all integrated with the AYNA information system in real time** ([Banker.az, Jan 2026](https://banker.az/az%C9%99rbaycanda-avtobuslarla-bagli-yeni-t%C9%99l%C9%99bl%C9%99r/)). Behind it, AYNA pays NEC Telekomünikasyon (Turkey) for the bus AVL "Full SLA" (**2,595,000 AZN in March 2025, concluded outside the procurement portal, and 2.1 million AZN in February 2026**) ([Pravda.az](https://pravda.az/news/178798); [ABC.az](https://www.abc.az/en/news/197121/ayna-signs-a-contract-for-21-million-manats)), has given one local integrator, Az Software System, roughly **31 million AZN of information-system work since November 2023, including a 13.4-million-AZN support award in October 2026 and an existing "Analysis and Reporting System"** ([ABC.az, Oct 2026](https://www.abc.az/en/news/218308/ayna-to-spend-134-million-manats-on-these-systems); [ABC.az, Feb 2026](https://www.abc.az/en/news/196683/ayna-to-spend-45-million-manats-on-software)), announced a Data Warehouse in July 2026 as the base for "AI decision support, predictive analytics and Big Data" ([Report.az](https://report.az/infrastruktur/azerbaycanda-neqliyyat-sahesinde-melumat-anbari-yaradilir)), and hosts everything in AzInTelecom's Government Cloud since December 2025 ([Tech.az](https://tech.az/en/posts/ayna-has-migrated-its-information-systems-to-the-quot-government-cloud-quot-6285)). The AI tender's "Phase 0" scope reads like Ritm's analytics half: "analysis of movement time and slow zones, utilization and occupancy analysis, demand peaks (concentration), route diagnostics and transfer load, determining boarding/alighting points (recovery of O/D matrix)", plus text-to-SQL, RBAC and an immutable audit log ([BidDetail](https://www.biddetail.com/latest-tenders/119738634$0e0f32e7-e292-43d5-8f5f-5ce32855126c)). The gross-cost rules, finally, say that "the number of buses per route, movement interval, distance and other service indicators are set in advance" and that "through specially created software the quality of carriers' service will be measured", with penalties deducted from the monthly payment, on routes 130, 205, 211, 500 and 503 first ([AYNA](https://www.ayna.gov.az/az/news/brutto-muqavileye-kecidle-ictimai-neqliyyat-sahesinde-yeni-dovr-baslayir-1207); [Cabinet Decision 250](https://nk.gov.az/uploads/doc/docs/6a6c9fda82388.pdf)). Even a local telematics reseller already advertises interval alerts: GPS.az's Wialon NimBus page describes comparing stop times with the timetable and leaving the dispatcher to "call the driver, adjust the headway, or put a spare vehicle out" [vendor] ([GPS.az](https://gps.az/en/software/wialon-nimbus/)).

What is genuinely new, then, is narrow and valuable: the closed loop from measured headway to a timed instruction at the right control point, delivered to the person who can act, and a regularity KPI that AYNA has never published. What is not new is the data, the monitoring, the interval language, the practice of adding buses, or crowding analytics. The pitch should say so in the first minute, because the judges' transport mentors will know it.

## 3. Advantages: a measured problem, a proven method, and a buyer that just acquired a reason to care

The problem is documented in the operators' own numbers, and it is a regularity problem rather than a frequency problem. Planned headways on Baku trunk routes are 4–8 minutes, yet observed peak intervals of 15–22 minutes recur in the record: BakuBus's monitoring centre logged **route 46 at 7–15 minutes against a 7–8-minute passport** in June 2025 ([Baku.ws](https://baku.ws/social/bakubus-dan-istifade-edenlerin-nezerine-avtobus-20-25-deqiqeden-gec-gelerse)) and route 2, 7A and E1 at roughly double or triple plan in October 2026 ([Qafqazinfo](https://qafqazinfo.az/news/detail/insanlarin-gunu-yollarda-kecir-30-40-deqiqe-avtobus-gozleyirik-video-524436)); BNA's peak bulletins counted **154 buses on 48 routes with 10–18-minute inter-bus delays** on a single January 2023 evening ([APA](https://apa.az/social/sixliga-gore-187-avtobus-unvana-gecikir-741490)); route 49 riders described buses that "come rarely, and after a long gap 2–3 buses arrive one after another" while AYNA confirmed 26 buses at a 7-minute interval ([Oxu.az, Oct 2024](https://oxu.az/cemiyyet/bakida-avtobus-intervallari-sernisinleri-narazi-saldi-muvafiq-tapsiriq-verildi)). Bunching, not low frequency, is the complaint pattern on the big routes, and that is the one failure mode headway control exists to fix.

The method has a field record that most "AI for transit" ideas lack. WMATA's headway-control experiment on routes 70/79 produced "a reduction of 26% in passenger excess waiting time ... annual time savings that translate into US$1 million" even though "the field experiment implementation was far from ideal" [field] ([Soza-Parra et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0361198119845369)). Chicago CTA's 2022–23 pilots are the closest analogue to Baku because they ran under a shortage: on route 81, with 15–25% of trips cancelled for absenteeism, terminal holding cut average waits 8.7% in the morning and from 8.28 to 6.63 minutes (1.6 minutes per rider) in the evening peak at **35% driver compliance**; on route 66, en-route holding at three rail-transfer stops cut waits 5.7% and the 90th-percentile load from 60.0 to 56.7 riders while running 1.6 fewer trips a day, at **56.9% compliance** [field] ([Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)). Georgia Tech's self-equalising rule, which needs only the estimated arrival time of the following bus, kept the smallest headway at 2 minutes 28 seconds against 1 second under the schedule and the largest at 14:59 against 19:45, and after one of three buses was removed "headways of successive arrivals reequalized at about 17 minutes" with no management action; drivers "liked not having to check the clock constantly" [field] ([Bartholdi & Eisenstein 2012](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/papers/Bartholdi-Eisenstein-2011.pdf)). Stockholm's 2011–12 in-bus display trials with ~100 drivers cut daily waiting 10% and left drivers "less stressed", and the regularity approach is now written into SL's operator contracts [field] ([KTH impact case](https://www.kth.se/en/forskning/sarskilda-forskningssatsningar/strategiska-forskningsomraden/impactcases/regularity-based-bus-services-stockholm-1.935863)); Santiago's line-210 pilot cut regularity fines 40–50% at a 25% compliance rate [field, slides] ([Giesen et al., CASPT 2015](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/talk49.pdf)). The theory explains why these gains survive bad compliance: a one-parameter linear holding law "requires about 40% less slack than the conventional schedule-based method" and needs only "the arrival times of the current bus and the preceding bus relative to the virtual schedule" ([Xuan, Argote & Daganzo 2011](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0jp7c8k8/qt0jp7c8k8.pdf)), and even-headway cooperative holding is "highly robust to the impacts of imperfect driver compliance ... as well as imprecise communication with the driver display" ([Gkiotsalitis & Cats 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tre.2020.102176)). This robustness to missing buses is the single property that makes the idea fit Baku, where AYNA's chairman says **~1,000 buses still need replacing** ([Operativmm.az](https://operativmm.az/en/post/baku-bus-fleet-to-be-modernized-by-2030/66473)) and the agency deflects driver-shortage questions to carriers while BakuBus advertises 1,350 AZN gross for drivers ([Baku.ws](https://baku.ws/social/avtobus-suruculerinin-maaslari-artirilacaq-resmi-cavab); [Bakucity.az](https://bakucity.az/bakubus-driver-vacancy-october-2026/)).

The regulatory language already favours Ritm's metric. AYNA describes urban service by interval ("hərəkət intervalı"), the route passport must state "the movement interval by season, day of week and time of day" ([Rules on passenger carriage, AYNA PDF](https://ayna.gov.az/uploads/documents/vQ3Nfs2PELQhuRuUQLwPDMS7RNOXCpah39NP9iDH.pdf)), and the gross-cost KPIs are bus count and interval with penalties deducted from the carrier's monthly payment ([Cabinet Decision 250](https://nk.gov.az/uploads/doc/docs/6a6c9fda82388.pdf)). Decision 250 also lists "costs of organising the dispatcher service" (2.3.9.21) and "software used on computer equipment" (2.3.9.23) as reimbursable "other justified costs" inside the per-kilometre rate, so a carrier can book a Ritm licence and recover it through the state payment with a 10% margin on top ([Cabinet Decision 250](https://nk.gov.az/uploads/doc/docs/6a6c9fda82388.pdf)). Holding at termini is already an operator practice that BakuBus defends publicly as regulation "according to the set movement schedule" ([Bakues.az](https://bakues.az/bakubus-10-nomreli-avtobusla-bagli-naraziliga-aydinliq-getirdi-video-yenilenib/)), and the model contract gives carriers the right, with AYNA's consent, to "temporarily revise bus timetables and the number of trips" when flows change ([Cabinet Decision 52, model contract](https://mincom.gov.az/storage/pages/653/43ae63e8bf49c3dccdbeeabbbdcc4282.pdf)). Ritm's own design rule, hold only after boarding is complete, never skip a stop, never shorten boarding, is not only humane but legally prudent, since AYNA warns carriers precisely for stop-skipping and scheme changes ([Avtosfer, Feb 2026](https://avtosfer.az/az/gundem/bu-dayanacaqda-saxlamayan-avtobus-suruculerine-xeberdarliq-edilib-ayna-dan-aciqlama-318962)).

The institutional window is real, and it is this quarter. AYNA has made interval a penalised contract term and said it lacks the tooling; it has tendered an AI platform and started a Data Warehouse; BakuBus, which runs **1,337 buses on 83 routes, roughly half of Baku's 2,530 route buses**, has been folded into Baku Metro CJSC, a commercial entity that just received an **ADB loan of $302.38 million for signalling modernisation and operations digitalisation** ([AZCON](https://azcon.gov.az/az/portfolio/bakubus-4); [Banker.az, Sep 2026](https://banker.az/bakida-marsrut-avtobuslarinin-sayi-rekord-h%C9%99dd%C9%99-catdirilir-2-530-avtobus/); [Trend](https://www.trend.az/azerbaijan/politics/4183615.html); [ADB](https://www.adb.org/news/adb-approves-302-million-loan-modernize-baku-metro-signaling-and-digital-operations)). İRİA's Openwave programme, which ran the AYNA "TransportTech" hackathon on AYNA's own data in October 2026, states that "selected solutions are tested at prototype level in a real working environment and the results analysed by the agencies" and that local startups get first preference for state-company challenges ([Musavat.biz](https://www.musavat.biz/news/baku-id-cercivesinde-startap-ve-texnologiya-sirketleri-ile-innovasiya-ekosisteminde-imkanlar-ve-cagirislar-muzakire-edilib_1290155.html); [APA, Sep 2026](https://apa.az/ikt/iria-dovlet-sirketlerinin-problemlerinin-hellinde-yerli-startaplara-ustunluk-verilecek-993123); [Techzone](https://techzone.az/en/transporttech-hackathon-ayna-iria-2026/)).

The build is within a student team's reach if the scope is right. Control-point holding does not need high-rate telemetry: Georgia Tech ran on 15-second NextBus positions, and 60-second AVL "when processed appropriately, can provide ample information", with a model-based resampling giving a mean absolute error of 4.83 seconds on stop duration against 7.7 seconds for linear interpolation [field data] ([Bartholdi & Eisenstein](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/papers/Bartholdi-Eisenstein-2011.pdf); [Yang et al., TRR](https://humnetlab.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/TRRecordsBuses.pdf)). The driver interface does not need the fare vendor: Swiftly's Onboard App "requires no integrations with other onboard systems", runs on a commodity tablet "about one-tenth the price of a typical MDT" and is used by 50-plus US agencies [vendor] ([Swiftly, 2020](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/introducing-swiftlys-onboard-app); [Swiftly, 2026](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/onboard-app-reliable-service)); MDOT MTA's 15-day pilot of that model found buses "25% more likely to be on time" [agency figures via press] ([Mass Transit, Mar 2026](https://www.masstransitmag.com/technology/news/55362287/mdot-mta-aims-to-improve-on-time-performance-by-empowering-bus-operators-with-real-time-information)). And Baku's recent juries rewarded exactly this shape of product: the May 2026 Metro Hackathon winner, EscaFlow, was a decision-support system on the metro's real data in which "the Baku Metro personnel always holds decision authority", with separate analyst and dispatcher dashboards built in about 48 hours ([metro.gov.az](https://metro.gov.az/az/news/4734/baki-metropoliteni-tarixinde-ilk-metro-hackathon-layihesinin-qalibleri-belli-oldu); [team account](https://linkedin.com/in/telmanhaji)). A final, often overlooked advantage is that the mandatory passenger counters arriving from January 2026 will, within a year or two, create the load data that makes a real crowding model possible later ([Banker.az](https://banker.az/az%C9%99rbaycanda-avtobuslarla-bagli-yeni-t%C9%99l%C9%99bl%C9%99r/)); Ritm can be built so that module slots in when the data exists.

## 4. Disadvantages and risks: twelve objections the team must pre-empt

### The Kentkart screen already shows "+1:54", and Ritm cannot write to it

The driver in the team's route-172 photo is almost certainly looking at a Kentkart "Kent Tablet"/OBU run by K Group: BakuBus has been on Kentkart OBUs since 2015 and route 172 passed to BakuBus in 2023 ([Kentkart Baku presentation](https://prezi.com/2y0zvzwsv2cw/kentkart-baku/); [BakuBus 2023 review](https://bakubus.az/news/view/586)). The green "+1:54" and the "5 min" are consistent with Kentkart's advertised "trip runtime information ... facilitates timekeeping", that is, early/late against the trip's timing points and time to the next point [inference; no Baku UI documentation exists] ([Kentkart](https://www.kentkart.com/on-board-unit/)). Kentkart's deputy manager said at the 2015 Baku launch that "if the driver will go too fast or slow, out of time to get off a stop ... this information will be transmitted through the system at the center" ([Report.az, 2015](https://report.az/en/infrastructure/planned-to-use-two-kinds-of-cards-in-the-public-transport-of-capital-photos)). This creates three distinct problems. The first is competitive: a judge or mentor will say drivers already have a number on the screen. The honest reply is that a schedule-deviation number is the wrong signal on a congested 5-minute route, because schedule-based control "usually require[s] too much slack to be effective" ([Daganzo & Pilachowski 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261510000858)), and the agencies that run headway control treat the display differently: TfL's MDT shows "a schematic ladder" of the bus relative to the one in front, "is not used as a target reporting system", never asks a driver to speed up and "is only active when the vehicle is stationary" [agency] ([London Assembly MQ, 2024](https://www.london.gov.uk/who-we-are/what-london-assembly-does/questions-mayor/find-an-answer/dangerous-bus-contract-incentives-ibus-monitor-displays)), while MBTA's operator displays "do not show headways" at all [agency] ([MBTA deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent)). The second is technical and contractual: K Group is a single vendor gateway, the OBU is operationally mandatory (after K Group "loaded new software onto the payment devices in all buses overnight" in December 2022, most Xaliq Faiqoğlu buses "could not go out on the line" the next morning) ([Yeniçağ](https://yenicag.az/bakida-buna-gore-minlerle-sernisin-yollarda-qaldi-video)), and even a small cross-agency display change elsewhere required a Clever Devices scope of work with vendor development, testing and a confidential quote ([Clever Devices SOW](https://norta.legistar.com/View.ashx?GUID=939DD31E-AB29-4044-A0D7-017D5D5A3483&ID=12281911&M=F)). "We integrate with the driver console" is therefore a claim the team cannot back; "we run on a commodity tablet alongside the existing unit and can later map to the ITxPT MADT driver-terminal service" is defensible ([ITxPT specifications](https://itxpt.org/specifications/)). The third is a documented failure mode: two screens with two different numbers. Santiago drivers using an onboard headway tool were confused "about which instructions to follow (i.e., the instructions in the tablet or the existing schedule)" [field survey] ([Martínez-Estupiñán et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/23249935.2022.2025950)), and London drivers told the Assembly that the headway system "maintains the headways but sometimes it means bus drivers are forced to finish an hour late" [driver testimony] ([London Assembly transcript, 2017](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/18186/download)). Any Ritm instruction will coexist with a Kentkart schedule number and a passport timetable; the design has to say which wins and when.

### On 5-bus routes like 52 and 96, holding adds waiting

The team's own complaint examples cut against its product. Routes 52 and 96 run **5 and 6 buses at a standard 15–20-minute interval**, with waits of nearly an hour and full buses ([1news.az, Sep 2026](https://1news.az/az/news/20260903104422853-Bakida-avtobus-problemi-52-ve-96-nomreli-marshrutlari-bir-saata-yaxin-gozleyirler)). Headway-based service "is best suited for high-frequency bus routes, defined as those with a headway of 10 minutes or less" ([TCRP Synthesis 155](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/26163/chapter/3)); the random-arrival assumption that justifies even-headway holding "for headways of 12 min or less should be modified to 10 min or less" on Los Angeles data ([Abkowitz & Engelstein 1984](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/trr/1984/961/961-001.pdf)); and above that threshold riders time their arrival to the timetable, so holding to even out spacing delays people who arrived on purpose. A former UK transport minister's 2026 critique of London puts the practitioner view bluntly: regulation belongs at the terminus, is "pointless" on a 5-minute frequency and acceptable only at 20-minute-plus frequencies or first and last buses [comment] ([Passenger Transport](https://www.passengertransport.co.uk/?p=21707)). On a 5-bus route the gap problem is fleet size, not control; the right tools are realistic running times (van Oort found the 35th–50th percentile of historical trip time minimised wait on routes with holding points, and a 60-second maximum hold made schedule- and headway-based holding perform the same in The Hague simulations) [simulation] ([SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf); [Van Oort 2010](https://nielsvanoort.weblog.tudelft.nl/files/2014/01/8-TRR-2010-Van-Oort-Reliability-improvement-by-holding.pdf)) and accurate real-time information, which cut measured waits by almost two minutes for OneBusAway users [field] ([Watkins et al. 2011](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/where-is-my-bus-impact-of-mobile-real-time-information-on-the-perceived-and-actual-wait-time-of-transit-riders/10009292600)). The addressable set is still large: of 75 Baku routes tendered between February 2025 and July 2026, **49 (65%) require 12 or more buses and 14 (19%) require 20 or more**, with trunk routes like 49 at 30 buses, 3 at 26, 2 at 24, 18 at 23 and 7A at 20 ([AYNA announcement 63](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-63); [announcement 64](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-64); [announcement 66](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-66); [announcement 67](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-67)); network-wide, with ~209 route codes and ~2,530 buses, roughly 50–60% of routes have 12 or more buses and 15–20% are "big" routes where bunching dominates [inference from tender sample] ([map.ayna.gov.az](https://map.ayna.gov.az/); [Banker.az](https://banker.az/bakida-marsrut-avtobuslarinin-sayi-rekord-h%C9%99dd%C9%99-catdirilir-2-530-avtobus/)). The pitch should name those 30–40 trunk routes as the market and explicitly exclude the 52/96 class, or offer them a different module. Switching control modes by time of day, which many Baku routes would need, is "exactly the transition King County and VIA found hard operationally" ([SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)).

### Drivers comply 35–57% of the time, and the most experienced comply least

Every field pilot that measured compliance found it low to middling and dependent on physical supervision. At CTA's route-81 terminal only 28 of 80 adjusted departures were followed (35%), compliance fell the further the instruction was from the schedule, and route 66's 56.9% was attributed partly to "a supervisor physically enforcing the hold at the stop"; recorded reasons were schedule culture, fear that late departures "cut into their break time", leaving early at stops just before a signal to avoid a whole cycle, and reluctance to block traffic, with higher-seniority drivers over-represented among non-compliers [field] ([Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)). MBTA's one-day route-1 pilot cut bunching 38–40% but "roughly one-third of adjustments were not implemented exactly as intended" [agency] ([MBTA deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent)). In Donostia, the median driver violated holding orders 13.8% of the time after training, rising to 42.1% when the control parameter was tightened without retraining [field] ([Argote-Cabanero, Daganzo & Lynn](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6j16889k/qt6j16889k_noSplash_c53214284cd5f0a420be45fe2676deca.pdf)). Experienced Santiago drivers "appear to be more reluctant to use onboard headway control tools" [field survey] ([Martínez-Estupiñán et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/23249935.2022.2025950)). The institutional reactions are the real warning: Dublin's unions told drivers in November 2024, "for safety reasons, not to operate their radios while in service" after controllers kept calling early-running drivers to hold ([Irish Independent](https://www.independent.ie/regionals/dublin/dublin-news/dublin-bus-drivers-in-dispute-over-claims-of-distracting-calls-to-maintain-route-times/a1620070543.html)); London garages discussed industrial action over holds on empty roads that were "paving the way for drivers to be abused and assaulted" [activist] ([Socialist Party, 2016](https://www.socialistparty.org.uk/articles/22290/24-02-2016/stress-abuse-and-long-hours-for-london-bus-drivers/)); Toronto covered up a dashboard on-time indicator as a safety pilot ([CBC, 2015](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/ttc-wants-technology-to-boost-bus-safety-1.2927975)); Honolulu agreed to stop sending alerts to moving buses ([Hawaii News Now, 2014](https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/story/26671885/exclusive-city-bus-drivers-complain-text-alerts-compromise-safety/)). US agencies told TransitCenter that "even the best technology cannot substitute for staff presence" ([SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)). For Baku, with a shortage of drivers earning 1,200–1,350 AZN, a mixed state and private pool, and an enforcement chain that runs AYNA → carrier → driver rather than to the cab ([Qafqazinfo](https://qafqazinfo.az/news/detail/xaliq-faiqoglu-avtobus-suruculerini-ise-devet-edir-441426); [Oxu.az](https://oxu.az/cemiyyet/49-sayli-marsrutdan-sikayet-surucu-qapilari-baglaya-bilmir)), a product that assumes 80–90% compliance over-estimates by about a factor of two relative to Chicago and Boston [inference]. There is a Baku-specific twist: drivers already hold at termini for a different reason, waiting until the bus fills, which passengers on routes 46 and 79 complain about ([Baku.ws](https://baku.ws/social/bakubus-dan-istifade-edenlerin-nezerine-avtobus-20-25-deqiqeden-gec-gelerse); [Cebheinfo](https://cebheinfo.az/sosial/79-sayli-avtobusdan-naraziliq-interval-8-deqiqedirse-sernisinler-niye-yarim-saat-gozleyir_194)) and which AYNA's chairman links to a fare model that pushes carriers to "carry more passengers with minimum resources" ([Banker.az, Sep 2025](https://banker.az/ayna-paytaxtda-avtobuslarin-orta-yasi-7-12-il-arasinda-d%c9%99yisir/)). A "hold" instruction could become cover for the revenue hold, so Ritm's log of instructed versus actual departures matters as much as the instruction.

### Passengers see a bus idling, not a network evening out

Holding is a redistribution: downstream riders wait less, riders on the held bus and at upstream stops wait longer. The only field study that separated effects found that "the time passengers spent in vehicles that were holding increased by 20 seconds" and that "headway based holding did not have a significant effect on average travel time", with roughly half of the regularity gain attributable to holding and the rest to bus lanes, stop removal and third-door boarding [field] ([West & Cats 2015](http://resolver.tudelft.nl/uuid:5454500a-1e84-4a3c-9759-e18d725da02e)). Agencies admit that "without clear and consistent on-board announcements, customers question why a bus is idling" ([SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)), and London, the longest-running large-scale headway regime, generates persistent complaints about buses held "up to three minutes" on clear roads ([Passenger Transport, 2026](https://www.passengertransport.co.uk/?p=21707)). Barcelona's ombudsman documented a rider who recorded 13–15-minute gaps followed by paired buses while TMB's aggregate data showed 8–9-minute averages and compliance, which is to say that aggregate regularity KPIs can hide exactly the bunching riders feel ([Sindicatura de Greuges de Barcelona, 2024](https://www.sindicaturabarcelona.cat/wp-content/uploads/202405_Dret-mobilitat_linia-V23-dautobus-incompleix-sistematicament-la-frequencia-de-pas.pdf)). Models show net passenger time loss when slack is large or demand is near capacity; in a Chengdu case the controlled system's advantage reverses at a demand ratio of 1.2 because fewer passengers can be served per unit time [simulation] ([Journal of Advanced Transportation 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/9714046)). In Baku the risk is sharpened by culture: passengers already read a stationary bus as "not moving until full", and AYNA warns carriers whose buses "change their movement scheme at peak hours" ([Icma.az](https://icma.az/news/ayna-bezi-avtobuslarin-dasiyicilarina-xeberdarliq-edecek-1255230)). A mid-route hold without a visible reason on the interior monitor, which the January 2026 rules now mandate on every bus, will be reported as the old abuse ([Banker.az](https://banker.az/az%C9%99rbaycanda-avtobuslarla-bagli-yeni-t%C9%99l%C9%99bl%C9%99r/)).

### Holding spends slack Baku does not have

Agencies call headway management "a resource hog" because it needs dedicated dispatchers, terminal layover space, relief at terminals rather than mid-route, flexible rosters and often overtime; MBTA had "as few as six dispatchers for the system's almost 180 bus routes" and found that "lack of layover space at terminals limits terminal holds" ([SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf); [MBTA deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent)). Holding lengthens cycle time, and with a fixed fleet that means either longer effective headways, a capacity loss on crowded corridors, or overtime: the real-time method in Atlanta "dispatched buses with slightly longer headways, 622 seconds on average, compared to 584 seconds for the schedule method" [field] ([CASPT 2018](https://caspt.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Papers/CASPT_2018_paper_93.pdf)), and London drivers describe recovery time "being absorbed into running time" ([London Assembly transcript](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/18186/download)). Adoption is correspondingly rare: a 2021 survey found that "the nine transit agencies that reported having fully or partially implemented HBS reported a total of 15 routes with HBS" ([TCRP Synthesis 155](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/26163/chapter/5)); LA Metro's early-2000s Metro Rapid effort "was later discontinued" ([LAist](https://laist.com/news/transportation/can-buses-run-more-efficiently-without-a-schedule-metro-wants-to-test-it-out)); Cap Metro's terminal-dispatch pilot found that "headways did not improve throughout the rest of the route" because traffic variability "negated much of the dispatching effort" ([SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)), with spacing breaking down "after about three or four miles" [vendor-hosted agency quote] ([Swiftly, 2019](https://goswift.ly/blog/mind-the-gap-best-practices-in-headway-management)). Baku's long, congested trunk routes therefore need mid-route control points, which carry the passenger and driver costs above, and under gross-cost contracts the authority pays either way. There is also a legal edge: the Administrative Offences Code (Art. 322.0.1) fines "non-compliance with the established movement timetable and movement scheme" of regular-route buses, with the sanction set at 50 AZN by the August 2023 amendment according to AYNA's own guidance, though sources on the current amount conflict ([President.az, Aug 2023](https://president.az/az/articles/view/60868); [AYNA, Mar 2025](https://ayna.gov.az/az/news/sifarisli-sernisin-dasinmasi-ucun-formulyarlarin-istifadesi-ile-bagli-maariflendirici-tedbirler-kecirilir-695)), and the model contract lets AYNA terminate for "repeated or gross violation ... of the movement scheme, timetable, work regime" ([Cabinet Decision 52](https://mincom.gov.az/storage/pages/653/43ae63e8bf49c3dccdbeeabbbdcc4282.pdf)). Holds must be issued under the operator's dispatch authority, be short, and not make buses measurably late at passport timing points [inference].

### Untracked buses turn a hold into a guess

A backward-looking holding rule needs the follower's position. AYNA's MaaS app has, since February 2026, told users explicitly when an arrival time is "based not on real-time (GPS) data but on the route's interval", an official admission that a share of Baku buses is not tracked in real time ([AYNA, Feb 2026](https://www.ayna.gov.az/az/news/ayna-maas-tetbiqi-bir-qeder-de-tekmillesdirilib-1021)); passengers report buses that are invisible in the app or never arrive ([Redaktor.az, Oct 2026](https://redaktor.az/news/country/347569-ayna-maas-da-melumatlar-niye-yenilenmir)); about 2,000 of ~2,160 daily buses were cashless-connected in 2024 ([Baku.ws](https://baku.ws/social/ayna-bu-il-ozel-dasiyicilar-bakiya-100-den-cox-avtobus-getirecek)); no published figure gives the GPS ping interval or positional accuracy of AYNA's telematics. The failure catalogue elsewhere is long: the Georgia Tech route trial lasted "77 minutes ... due to defective GPS units" and the software coped with lag but "not when it lost AVL feed for extended periods" [field] ([CASPT 2018](https://caspt.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Papers/CASPT_2018_paper_93.pdf)); WMATA's reliability KPI was "unavailable" for all of FY2019 because of "older, defective software ... on the on-board equipment of approximately 10 percent of the Metrobus fleet" ([NVTC 2019](https://novatransit.org/uploads/studiesarchive/2019%20WMATA%20Full%20Report.pdf)); MBTA lists "ghost buses" and a "distorted/untrusted" OTP metric ([MBTA deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent)); the UK RTIG guidance lists more than 30 causes of phantom or missing vehicles, most of them logon, data and latency problems such as "poor quality logons" and "radio / GPRS black hole gaps" ([RTIG T054](https://www.rtig.org.uk/system/files/documents/RTIGT054-1.0%20Ghost%20Buses.pdf)). With 27 operators serving Baku, five of them owning 58% of the fleet ([Banker.az, Sep 2025](https://banker.az/ayna-paytaxtda-avtobuslarin-orta-yasi-7-12-il-arasinda-d%c9%99yisir/)), logon discipline across private carriers is the most likely data failure. A bus that drops off the feed looks like a gap, so the algorithm may hold the next bus for a phantom gap or send a reserve into a corridor that is fine; Ritm needs a dropout-aware "do nothing" rule and a forward-headway or schedule fallback whenever the follower is untracked, and the dispatcher view must show data-quality state [inference]. The data-access side is equally hard: AYNA MaaS declares "No data shared with third parties" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.aynamaas.app)), no GTFS or GTFS-RT feed or developer API exists, hackathon access to AYNA data was event-scoped ([Techzone](https://techzone.az/en/transporttech-hackathon-ayna-iria-2026/)), and the public map API has no published licence ([route_efficiency](https://github.com/ismatsamadov/route_efficiency)), so self-collected positions are a research snapshot in a legal grey zone and must be labelled as such.

### Crowd Forecast without tap data is a static profile wearing an AI label

Published stop-level demand prediction works only with historical tap-in or passenger-counter data, and even then it is fragile: a Seoul stop-level deep network matched 94.76% in training and at most 52.3% in test ([Baek & Sohn 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08839514.2016.1277291)); Beijing's XGBoost with point-of-interest features improved boarding RMSE only from 8.96 to 7.84 over the historical average ([Lv et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/app12030940)); Trondheim's APC-based stacking reached R² of just 0.575 for boardings per stop-visit ([Yusuf, Rasheed & Lindseth 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11116-025-10689-4)). Where real-time APC exists, crowding-class accuracy reaches 75–80% with an 8–11-passenger error, but APC covered only "about 20% of all buses" in Stockholm and historical patterns "cannot explain well why some buses are highly crowded"; worse, historical data "tend to underestimate the load on crowded runs" and real-time AVL "reduces the variance of the prediction but does not improve the bias" ([KTH/DiVA](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1786851/FULLTEXT01.pdf)). Baku's 89% cashless share means tap data exists, but it is not public, taps are tagged to vehicle and route rather than stop unless fused with GPS, and the 11% cash trips are invisible ([Report.az, Sep 2026](https://report.az/en/infrastructure/baku-bus-passenger-trips-reach-335-6-million-in-jan-aug-2026-ayna-says)). No published study uses only POI, time and weather with no historical counts, so any MAPE the team quotes for such a model would be unsupported [inference]. A model trained on daily route-level boardings and metro exits will under-trigger reserve dispatch on exactly the crowded trips it is sold to fix, and it competes head-on with the "occupancy analysis" and "demand peaks" lines of AYNA's tender ([BidDetail](https://www.biddetail.com/latest-tenders/119738634$0e0f32e7-e292-43d5-8f5f-5ce32855126c)).

### A 20-bus reserve pool is a fixed cost nobody in Baku has budgeted

Standby-bus insertion wins in simulations because the bus and driver are assumed to exist and be free: Petit and Ouyang found it outperformed schedule-based recovery with minimal rider disruption, but the strategy "can generate unbudgeted labor and fleet costs ... operators may only be available on overtime, and the peak fleet may already be on the road" [simulation, as summarised] ([SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf); [Petit, Ouyang & Lei 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:transb:v:115:y:2018:i:c:p:1-16)). Agencies that use it do so ad hoc for incidents and severe gaps, not routine bunching; Sound Transit states flatly that "for cost-saving measures, we do not have a reserve fleet of buses and drivers waiting for backup needs" ([Sound Transit](https://www.soundtransit.org/q-when-train-service-goes-down-why-aren-t-back-buses-available-immediately)); New York's MTA said it "is trying to make sure it isn't paying drivers when they are not needed" ([Planetizen, 2022](https://planetizen.com/news/2022/09/118574-new-york-mta-cuts-bus-service)); Minneapolis research found "reserve drivers were found to be less efficient than overtime or regular drivers" ([Minnesota Daily, 2016](https://mndaily.com/237198/uncategorized/u-researchers-design-scheduling-algorithm)). No utilisation or benefit-cost figures for any gap-bus programme were found anywhere, and no field evaluation of a reserve-dispatch optimiser against dispatcher practice exists. In Baku the reserves that do exist are per route and contractual, "+1 reserve" in most tender lots and "+2" on route 49 ([AYNA announcement 66](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-66)), not a central pool of 20 at hubs; the project plan's hub list is an assumption with no source. With a driver shortage, every standby driver is a scheduled trip not run, so "gap buses" compete directly with base service, and under gross-cost contracts the authority would have to buy standby capacity as an explicit line item, which Decision 250 does not contain ([Cabinet Decision 250](https://nk.gov.az/uploads/doc/docs/6a6c9fda82388.pdf)). The scale is also wrong for the pitch: 20 buses are 0.8% of a 2,530-bus fleet, while AYNA's planning-level response to interval problems was to add 112 buses on 31 routes in one decision ([AYNA, Sep 2023](https://www.ayna.gov.az/az/news/bakida-marsrut-x-tl-rin-lav-112-avtobus-c-lb-edilib)). The vehicle-rescheduling formulation the team would implement is well known ([Li, Mirchandani & Borenstein 2007](https://doi.org/10.1002/net.20199)), but it needs depot locations, shift rules and breakdown rates that no Baku source provides, so anything shown must be labelled scenario analysis with synthetic disruptions calibrated to published failure rates such as SFMTA's 12,000-mile mean-distance-between-failures target ([SFMTA](https://www.sfmta.com/reports/muni-mean-distance-between-failure)).

### AYNA is buying the analytics half of Ritm this month

The tender is a direct overlap and a timing problem: bids and envelope opening are on **24 November 2026**, so by Demo Day a winner may already hold a contractual claim on occupancy, demand-peak, slow-zone and O/D analytics inside AYNA ([Fins.az](https://fins.az/az/biznes/ayna-dan-3-milyon-manatliq-tender); [Trend](https://az.trend.az/business/tender/4238737.html)). An Azerbaijani ICT expert reads it as a "mini" intelligent transport management system with an AI agent for AYNA staff, serving the agency's analysis rather than citizens, and notes that AYNA has not said how it integrates with existing systems or whether the ministry's expediency opinion was obtained ([Tezadlar](https://tezadlar.az/mini-niis-hazirlanir-ayna-iflasa-ugramis-evvelki-110-milyonluq-layiheden-ibret-goturecekmi/)). Likely bidders are Az Software System (incumbent on every AYNA information-system lot), SINAM (historic ITS integrator), NEC Telekomünikasyon (owner of the AVL pipe) and AzInTelecom-aligned integrators; the scope's identity integration, RBAC and audit-log requirements favour whoever already has AYNA system access, and a startup is plausible only as a subcontractor or algorithm provider [inference]. What the tender does not buy is real-time control delivered to drivers or reserve-bus insertion, which remains white space, but AYNA may well regard that as "Phase 1" of the same platform, so Ritm must present itself as a module that plugs into the platform being procured, not a competitor to it [inference].

### Three incumbents own the data pipe, the platform and the screen

NEC holds the AVL service contract, Az Software System holds the information-system estate, and K Group holds the validator and driver screen, with AzInTelecom hosting all of it ([Pravda.az](https://pravda.az/news/178798); [ABC.az](https://www.abc.az/en/news/218308/ayna-to-spend-134-million-manats-on-these-systems); [Tech.az](https://tech.az/en/posts/ayna-has-migrated-its-information-systems-to-the-quot-government-cloud-quot-6285)). Lock-in is the norm in this industry: "AVL hardware providers often impose proprietary constraints on the data produced by their systems" ([World Bank Connections note](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/pt/979511471004982975/pdf/107701-BRI-PUBLIC-conn-27-MARK-VC-KNOWLEDGENOTES.pdf)); Fresno's technology "is proprietary and only accessible to its original vendor, Trapeze" ([City of Fresno](https://agendas.fresno.gov/en/legislation/id19-11218-10280)); even County Connection, which bought Swiftly as an overlay because "obtaining useful and timely data from CCCTA's Clever Devices CAD/AVL system ... has been an ongoing challenge", took from December 2022 to 2026 to grow from two modules to five ([CCCTA, Aug 2026](https://countyconnection.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/4.c.-Swiftly-Contract.pdf)). Regional full-stack platforms such as AzRy's PTS in Georgia and Avtobys in Kazakhstan can bundle a basic "interval monitoring" screen cheaply [vendor] ([AzRy](https://www.azry.com/transport); [Avtobys](https://avtobys.kz/for-partners)), and a Baku reseller already sells headway alerts on Wialon [vendor] ([GPS.az](https://gps.az/en/software/wialon-nimbus/)). None of the Western headway-management vendors has a documented bus deployment in Azerbaijan, Georgia, Armenia, Kazakhstan or Uzbekistan, which is both an opening and a signal that the market has not paid for this capability.

### No startup has yet been paid by AYNA, and the price ceiling is small

No example was found, across 2023–2026, of an Azerbaijani startup with a signed, paid pilot or SaaS contract with AYNA, BakuBus or Baku Metro. The documented ceiling is a MacBook Air per winning team and a 50-AZN BakıKart per member at TransportTech ([İRİA](https://tr.linkedin.com/posts/iddaazerbaijan_iria-ayna-openwave-activity-7519304073095958528-RgC2)), 5,000/3,000/1,000 AZN plus internships and a promise that "the best projects may be piloted" at the Metro Hackathon, with no pilot confirmed ([Tech.az](https://tech.az/en/posts/a-competition-with-a-prize-of-5-000-azn-begins-in-the-baku-metro-6684)), and Youth Foundation grants capped at 5,000 AZN per individual project with commercial projects excluded ([Youth Foundation](https://youthfoundation.az/192976/)). The paid path exists but is narrow: purchases under 100,000 AZN can go by request for quotations with 10 working days' notice and the lowest compliant price winning, out-of-plan purchases up to 10,000–20,000 AZN a year fall outside the law, single-source awards need the supervisory authority's consent, and local-origin bids get up to a 20% price preference ([e-qanun](https://e-qanun.az/framework/54966); [Prosecutor General's Office](https://genprosecutor.gov.az/az/post/7582); [Caspian Legal Center](https://caspianlegalcenter.az/news/more/procurement-law)); the KOBİA "Startup" certificate gives a three-year profit-tax holiday once revenue exists ([Report.az](https://report.az/biznes-xeberleri/startap-sehadetnamesini-kimler-ve-nece-elde-ede-biler/)). The revenue arithmetic is sobering: Swiftly's realised pricing clusters at roughly **$1,500–2,500 per bus per year** for a four-to-five-module stack (LAVTA's year-one licence of $101,940 for 68 buses; County Connection's $246,563 for 125 buses), with driver guidance a $600-list add-on ([LAVTA](https://www.wheelsbus.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/7.1_SR_Swiftly-SaaS-Agreement.pdf); [CCCTA, Jun 2025](https://countyconnection.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/5.FINAL_Swiftly-Modules.pdf); [Beaumont order form](https://mccmeetingspublic.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/beaumntca-meet-ada51768922143e7aa781ac02b57d63d/ITEM-Attachment-001-d951d98eeee9456cac722302bfdc07cb.pdf)), so at a locally plausible 500–1,000 AZN per bus the whole Baku ceiling is **1.15–2.3 million AZN a year**, about one NEC service contract and far below the 31 million AZN AYNA has given one integrator [estimate]. The practical prize is a slice of the integrator budget, a subcontract or module licence, not per-bus SaaS. The buyers themselves are fragile: Xaliq Faiqoğlu, the largest private carrier with ~400 buses, reports audited net losses of 3.1–5.3 million AZN in each year from 2021 to 2024 ([ENA.az](https://ena.az/az/news/id-66172)); the gross-cost model was still "at the final stage" in May 2026 ([Report.az](https://report.az/en/infrastructure/ayna-gross-contracts-in-public-transport-will-develop-fare-payment-system)); BakuBus changed owners twice in 18 months; and institutional memory includes a >$100 million ITS bought "without proper adaptation to the local context" that ended "at a crossroads of improvement or complete disrepair" ([World Bank 2018](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/963521580136216965-0080022020/original/BakuUrbanMobilityPolicyNoteJune2018.pdf)). Donor money found is metro- or corridor-specific; no World Bank or EBRD programme for Baku bus operations software exists.

### Most hackathon projects are dead within a week

Of Devpost projects with repositories, **35.30% had at least one commit after the event, 17.06% by day six, and 3.55% after five months**; winning predicts short-term continuation, while skill diversity and stated intention predict long-term continuation ([Nolte, Chounta & Herbsleb 2020](https://alexandernolte.github.io/download/Nolte%20et%20al.%20(2020)%20What%20Happens%20to%20All%20These%20Hackathon%20Projects%20-%20Identifying%20Factors%20to%20Promote%20Hackathon%20Project%20Continuation.pdf)). The comparable real systems took years: Georgia Tech's tablet system ran "live trials for over one year" before its 2012 demo and entered full-time use in August 2013 ([GT project page](https://www.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/buses/buses.html)); CTA's two pilots spanned 2022–2023 with a university team running a web app on supervisors' phones ([Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)). Judges are also getting sharper: at a 2026 government AI hackathon the panel asked whether the tool works on records "as they are", "Where is the AI, and can you name it?", and whether it changes a number the agency reports ([Gotchaa Lab, Sep 2026](https://gotchaa-lab.com/blog/2026-09-10-selangor-ai-hackathon-2026-top-10)); published rubrics treat "a simple wrapper around an existing model" as lower-scoring ([DNHacks](https://portal.dnhacks.org/e/2026/judging)) and warn that "admitting something is faked costs a little; hiding it and being found out costs a lot" ([ITKAN](https://www.itkan.one/I-Hackathon/judging)). No rubric has been published for this hackathon; the organisers confirm only Stage I with 150 participants, Stage II with ten finalists in Startup Demo Day format, three awards and a link to the AI Strategy 2025–2028 ([Edumap](https://edumap.az/i-nsanyonumlu-seherler-hakatonu-kecirilecek)).

### Privacy law makes driver scoring a liability

Driver GPS traces and compliance logs are employee personal data under Law No. 998-IIIQ, which requires written consent for processing and for any transfer to third parties, purpose limitation and state registration of personal-data systems, and restricts cross-border transfer to countries with equivalent protection ([Law on Personal Data, Mincom PDF](https://mincom.gov.az/storage/pages/376/998-iiiq-ferdi-melumatlar-haqqinda.pdf); [idp.gov.az](https://idp.gov.az/az/law/143/parent/15)); Azerbaijani legal commentary holds that employee consent "in principle cannot be considered as freely given", so monitoring must rest on the employer's duties and cannot be covert ([LHRI](https://www.lhri.az/en/library-download/2172)). The cautionary case is Rochester, where a union called an AI telematics system "invasive and incapable of perceiving the nuances of the decision-making required while operating city buses" and the city moved to progressive discipline after disputes ([Workday Magazine, 2026](https://workdaymagazine.org/foia-documents-show-intrusive-ai-system-is-monitoring-rochester-bus-drivers/)). Ritm should process driver data only as the operator's contracted processor, publish no per-driver scores, host in Azerbaijan and use only aggregated passenger counts [inference].

## 6. Recommended changes: shrink the scope, fix the numbers, ask three questions

Change the product in six ways. First, scope Headway Guardian to corridors with scheduled headways of ten minutes or less, which in Baku means the 30–40 trunk routes with 20 or more buses (49, 3, 2, 18, 11, 6, 5, 14, 20, 21, 88, 104, 7A, 125) and the 12–19-bus tier where headways stay under ten minutes most of the day ([AYNA announcement 63](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-63); [announcement 64](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-64); [announcement 67](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-67)), and make the first control point the terminus, because "irregular departure headway at the initial stop is the key cause of bus bunching rather than downstream traffic conditions and passenger demand uncertainty" and a 0–1-minute departure headway gave a ~70% probability of downstream bunching against under 10% for 4–5 minutes on TriMet data [field] ([Feng & Figliozzi](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1328&context=cengin_fac)), with two or three mid-route control points only at high-dwell transfer stops such as metro stations, since the optimal point sits "just before a group of stops at which many passengers are boarding" ([Abkowitz & Engelstein 1984](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/trr/1984/961/961-001.pdf)). Use the parameters the literature will accept: a backward-looking rule with α≈0.5 or a two-way rule with α below 0.5 ([Bartholdi & Eisenstein](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/papers/Bartholdi-Eisenstein-2011.pdf); [Argote, Xuan & Gayah 2012](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~xuanyg/doc/Argote_Xuan_Gayah_2012_CASPT12.pdf)), holds in 30-second steps with a 5-minute lateness guard as CTA used ([Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)), a hold cap of 60–120 seconds (a synthesis across CTA, Wang & Sun's 180-second cap and van Oort's 60 seconds, not a single-source number), no holds under 30 seconds, a display locked while moving as TfL and Honolulu require, and an explicit fallback to forward-headway or schedule-based holding whenever the follower is untracked.

Second, drop every claim of console integration. Say that Ritm runs on a commodity Android tablet or phone beside the Kentkart unit in a handful of buses, or as a dispatcher-relayed instruction through BakuBus's monitoring centre, exactly as CTA had to do because "the supervisors at CTA do not have a direct communication line with bus drivers" ([Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)), and put "map to ITxPT MADT or a K Group change order" on the roadmap slide ([ITxPT](https://itxpt.org/specifications/)). Third, rename Crowd Forecast as a crowding index: derive expected load from the headway model plus a static arrival-rate profile per stop and hour, because headway is the best real-time proxy for load, validate it against a few days of the team's own manual counts, present it as low/medium/high, quote no MAPE, and state the exact data request that would turn it into a forecast, anonymised BakıKart boardings by stop, 15-minute slot and day type for one or two routes, plus any APC loads, citing the Metro Hackathon's release of the metro's real database and DataRace's de-identified data as local precedents ([metro.gov.az](https://metro.gov.az/az/news/4734/baki-metropoliteni-tarixinde-ilk-metro-hackathon-layihesinin-qalibleri-belli-oldu); [Trend, Aug 2026](https://az.trend.az/business/it/4218556.html)). Fourth, demote Reserve Dispatch to an "add-a-bus advisor": a small OR-Tools assignment over the per-route reserve buses that tenders already require, with deadhead time and a hard shift-end constraint, that outputs the top three options with costs for a dispatcher to accept or decline, validated only by replaying synthetic breakdowns at roughly one per 19,000 km and trip cancellations at an assumed 5–20% through the calibrated simulator and comparing against a "nearest available bus" heuristic, all labelled scenario analysis ([SFMTA](https://www.sfmta.com/reports/muni-mean-distance-between-failure); [Li, Mirchandani & Borenstein 2007](https://doi.org/10.1002/net.20199)). Fifth, give the 12–20-minute routes their own honest module: a running-time and timetable tool that fits the 35th–50th-percentile trip time from observed positions and feeds accurate arrival information, since schedule rewrites repeatedly deliver 5–15-point on-time gains (WMATA 67.0% to 73.2% [vendor]; Donostia cut delayed trips 59–72% by lengthening running times without new investment [press]) ([Swiftly](https://goswift.ly/blog/wmata-improves-on-time-performance-by-6-2-systemwide-with-swiftlys-run-times); [El Diario Vasco](https://www.diariovasco.com/san-sebastian/dbus-buenos-cambios-seis-lineas-20240428065709-nt.html)). Sixth, lead with measurement: compute and publish, for one route, the headway coefficient of variation on the TCQSM scale (below 0.21 "like clockwork", 0.40–0.52 "irregular headways, with some bunching", 0.75 and above "most vehicles bunched"), excess wait time as AWT − SWT with AWT = 0.5·E[H]·(1+CV²), and the shares of intervals under 25% and over 150% of scheduled ([UMN Metro Transit report](https://cts-d10resmod-prd.oit.umn.edu/pdf/contract-14i072.pdf); [Furth & Muller 2006](http://www1.coe.neu.edu/~pfurth/Furth%20papers/2006%20Service%20reliability%20&%20hidden%20waiting%20time,%20Furth%20&%20Muller%20TRR.pdf)), because nobody, including AYNA, has released such a figure for Baku, and insist on excess wait measured at stops rather than vendor tolerance bands, since a 6-minute service alternating 3- and 9-minute gaps can still "meet the standard" on a wide band ([Steve Munro, 2021](https://stevemunro.ca/2021/05/13/headway-quality-measurement-a-proposal/)).

Change the pitch and its numbers. Replace the registration text's promise that "excess waiting time falls by at least 15%" with a claimed range of **5–15% excess-wait reduction on one controlled route at realistic compliance**, anchored on CTA's 5.7–8.7% average-wait reductions and Stockholm's 5–10% EWT reduction (up to 45% at individual stops), with WMATA's 26% presented as the upper bound achieved under conditions "far from ideal" [field] ([Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1); [Cats, RETT3](https://trid.trb.org/View/1375940); [Soza-Parra et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0361198119845369)); keep "bunched share below 5%" as a target, not a promise, noting CTA's systemwide 2.8–3.9% as what a mature system achieves ([UMN report](https://cts-d10resmod-prd.oit.umn.edu/pdf/contract-14i072.pdf)). Never show a single idealised bar: the 50–77% waiting-time savings in the literature are simulations under ideal conditions ("in the majority of the cases the benefits of these models have been estimated assuming ideal operational conditions while only few of them have been tested in real conditions") ([Phillips et al. 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856415001639)), and a "−70% wait" chart is the single most attackable slide. Show three bars per metric instead: ideal (100% compliance, 15-second GPS, all buses tracked), CTA-like (50% compliance, 60-second GPS, 10% of buses untracked, 5-minute lateness cap) and pessimistic (35% compliance concentrated in a few drivers, which Phillips shows "deters the level of service more than if it is spread homogeneously"), and before any control result show that the baseline simulator reproduces observed Baku headway distributions with Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests by stop, as the Brisbane and Boston calibrations did ([Kieu et al. 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/151530/1/Bus_simulation_paper__Transportmetrica_B___2019_%20%281%29.pdf); [Yang et al.](https://humnetlab.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/TRRecordsBuses.pdf)). Name every model: a linear holding rule (Xuan/Bartholdi) as the controller, LightGBM for follower arrival-time and headway forecasts with its error shown against the schedule-based ETA, OR-Tools assignment for the add-a-bus advisor, and reinforcement learning only as an offline experiment, because RL beats tuned rules by tens of seconds in simulation and the one field deployment wrapped it in rule-based guardrails ([Wang & Sun 2021](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0060.pdf); [Xu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/tits.2025.3545642); [Zhang et al. 2026](https://academic.oup.com/tse/advance-article/doi/10.1093/tse/tdag059/8812242)). Do not cite Dubai's ">68%" early-departure reduction or Shenzhen's "−33% waiting", which have no published baseline or method ([RTA](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/news-and-media/all-news/NewsDetails/ai-contributes-68percent-to-reducing-early-bus-departures)), and do not present vendor on-time figures as headway results. Add a data-provenance slide that says what is real AYNA data, what is self-collected from the public app, and what is synthetic and why. Position the business case as a compliance aid for carriers under gross-cost contracts, booked under Decision 250's dispatcher-service and software cost lines, and as a module for AYNA's platform; name BakuBus/Baku Metro CJSC as the first pilot host (half the fleet, a closed dispatch loop, ADB-funded digitalisation) and Xaliq Faiqoğlu as the second, and name İRİA's Openwave and an RFQ under 100,000 AZN as the path from prototype to payment. Add a privacy slide: operator as data controller, no driver leaderboards, in-country hosting, aggregated passenger data.

Before Stage II, the team should sit with one BakuBus driver and one team leader or dispatcher and ask three questions, because the answers change the design and a quoted stakeholder conversation is the strongest antidote to the "synthetic project" critique.

## 7. Verdict: proceed with the changes above, and say so to the judges

Proceed with specific changes; do not proceed as-is, and do not switch. The alternative of switching to pure analytics collides with a 3-million-AZN tender that will have a winner by Demo Day; the alternative of switching to a different domain throws away the one quadrant where the evidence, the data and the policy window line up, a fleet- and driver-short city with heavy traffic that has just made interval regularity a penalised contract term and has no documented control loop. The changes that are not optional are these: Headway Guardian narrowed to ten-minute-or-better corridors, terminal-first, on a commodity tablet or through the dispatcher, with guardrails and an untracked-follower fallback; Crowd Forecast demoted to a headway-derived crowding index until tap or counter data are shared; Reserve Dispatch demoted to an add-a-bus advisor over the reserves that already exist per route, presented as scenario analysis; a timetable module for the 5-bus routes the team has been citing; a published headway CV and excess wait for one Baku route; claimed benefits of 5–15% at 35–57% compliance with three-bar ranges; named models with reinforcement learning kept offline; and positioning as a module and compliance aid rather than a platform. Keep the human-centred rule that no stop is skipped and no boarding is shortened; it is the part of the pitch that is simultaneously humane, legally safe and operationally honest.

What to say to the judges, in roughly these words: "Baku already collects the data and already measures intervals; AYNA's monitoring centre and BakuBus's dispatchers see 17-minute gaps on routes planned at seven. What no one in Baku does is close the loop from a measured headway to a timed instruction at the point where it matters, and no one has published a regularity figure for a single route. We built that loop with a control rule that has worked in the field in Washington, Chicago, Stockholm and Atlanta, and we ran it on Baku's own bus positions. In the field such rules cut excess waiting by 5–15% at the compliance rates real drivers show, 35–57%, and they stop the worst gaps even when buses are missing, which is Baku's situation. The driver already has a Kentkart screen that shows schedule deviation; our instruction runs on a separate tablet, appears only after boarding is complete and only while the bus is stationary, and never asks anyone to skip a stop or hurry a passenger. Our crowding layer is an index derived from headways until AYNA shares stop-level tap counts; our reserve module advises dispatchers on the reserve buses carriers already hold, and it is validated only in simulation. The AI is a named forecast model whose error we show, feeding a transparent rule; reinforcement learning is in our appendix, not in the cab. We know most hackathon projects stop within a week, so we are asking for the things that keep this one alive: one route, one terminus tablet, eight weeks, and a data agreement with BakuBus or AYNA, through Openwave or a quotation request under 100,000 manats." A team that says this will have conceded nothing that an informed judge could not discover, and will have the most defensible AI claim in the room.

The deeper lesson from the evidence is that headway control succeeds where it is cheap and fails where it is ambitious. Singapore and London moved city-scale regularity through contract incentives, more service controllers and driver training, with algorithms as the labour-saving layer, and Singapore's excess wait fell 15–25% once operators were paid per tenth of a minute ([Singapore MOT](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/written-reply-by-minister-for-transport-khaw-boon-wan-to-parliamentary-question-on-bus-regularity-following-implementation-of-bus-service-reliability-framework/); [Straits Times, Sep 2017](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/transport/bus-services-more-reliable-and-less-crowded-now-lta)). Baku has just adopted the contract half. Ritm's realistic destiny is not to be the platform AYNA buys but the module that shows, on one route and in minutes returned to riders, what the new contracts should be measuring; if the team treats Demo Day as the start of that eight-week pilot rather than the end of a hackathon, it will be in the 3.55%.
