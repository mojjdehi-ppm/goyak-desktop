# Pitch Baku's Bus Headway Tool, Not Smarter Crossings

The team should pitch a sharpened version of idea 2: an AI headway-management ("anti-bunching") tool for Baku's buses, built on self-collected vehicle positions from AYNA's own public map and MaaS app, demonstrated as a dispatcher/driver decision tool and positioned as the monitoring layer that AYNA's new gross-cost bus contracts now legally require. It beats idea 1 on every axis the research supports: it addresses the dominant daily pain of ~1.3 million bus riders rather than a niche of mid-block crossings; its core method (headway-based holding) has repeated field validation of 5–26% waiting-time reductions and is explicitly robust to missing buses, which is Baku's exact fleet-shortage regime; it has an identifiable buyer with a live budget (AYNA is tendering a 3 million AZN "mini" intelligent transport system with an AI agent and building a data warehouse this year); and the data to make the demo "real" is obtainable in two weeks without any agency cooperation. The pedestrian-crossing idea is humane and locally relevant (pedestrians are 40% of Azerbaijan's crash victims), but every feature in it is already in production in Vienna, London, Shenzhen and Singapore, Baku's own traffic-police centre launched a pilot in December 2025 that already includes a pedestrian signal and the "hold green on an empty road at night" logic, the controlling agency (the Interior Ministry's NİİM) publishes no data and no controller specs, and hackathon juries rarely reward smart traffic lights. Keep idea 1 in reserve as a one-slide "human-centred signal" extension, and use the bus-lane violation-detection concept, which has the best evidence-to-cost ratio of all the alternatives, as the product's phase-two module rather than a competing pitch.

## Baku's bus riders lose more time to bunching than any crossing can save

Baku's congestion is officially acknowledged and worsening: President Aliyev told a January 2025 transport meeting that jams are "becoming more frequent and severe each year" ([president.az](https://president.az/en/articles/view/68151/print)), and AYNA's chairman put the daily inbound flow at **630,000 people in roughly 300,000 cars** ([APA](https://en.apa.az/infrastructure/plan-to-prevent-traffic-jams-in-center-of-baku-revealed-459213)). The only public commute benchmark is Numbeo's crowd-sourced **37.9-minute average one-way trip** with 34.9% of respondents commuting by car and 23.8% by bus ([Numbeo](https://www.numbeo.com/traffic/in/Baku)); Baku appears in neither the TomTom nor the INRIX index, so there is no independent congestion score to quote. The bus, however, is the city's workhorse: AYNA reports **~2,300 buses operating daily carrying ~494 million passengers a year** ([APA, WUF13](https://en.apa.az/wuf13/planned-railway-metro-and-tram-projects-for-passenger-transportation-in-baku-announced-507647)), roughly double the metro's 627,000 daily riders ([Wikipedia, Baku Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Metro)), and the active fleet reached a record **2,530 buses in October 2026**, up ~42% in four years ([1news.az](https://1news.az/az/news/20261002150642203-Bakida-sinaga-chevrilen-avtobus-seferi-AYNA-sixliq-kondisioner-ve-davranish-qaydalari-ile-bagli-neleri-deyir)). A scraped snapshot of AYNA's public map counts **201 routes, 39 carriers and 3,456 stops**, and the accompanying preprint found only 29 of 201 routes run within 1.4× the straight line between their ends ([Samadov, engrXiv 2026](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/view/8632)).

Reliability, not capacity, is the binding constraint. Average passengers per bus per day fell from 630 (2023) to 560 (2025) even as ridership rose, so frequency is being added faster than demand ([Report.az](https://report.az/en/infrastructure/anar-rzayev-nearly-400-green-buses-to-be-launched-in-baku-by-end-of-2025)), yet bunching complaints are constant and specific. Route 49 passengers in October 2024: buses "come rarely, and after a long gap 2–3 buses arrive one after another and you can't board any"; AYNA's reply was that the carrier runs 26 buses at a 7-minute interval and that peak congestion "causes interval growth" ([Oxu.az](https://oxu.az/cemiyyet/bakida-avtobus-intervallari-sernisinleri-narazi-saldi-muvafiq-tapsiriq-verildi)). Routes 52 and 96 saw waits of nearly an hour in September 2026 with buses arriving full on 5- and 6-bus fleets ([1news.az](https://1news.az/az/news/20260903104422853-Bakida-avtobus-problemi-52-ve-96-nomreli-marshrutlari-bir-saata-yaxin-gozleyirler)). AYNA's own historic peak bulletins recorded **185 buses on 43 routes delayed with 15–18 minutes of extra interval** on the Baku–Sumgayit corridor ([Report.az, Nov 2022](https://report.az/infrastruktur/bakida-185-avtobusun-hereketi-gecikir)), and in October 2026 the agency conceded that "traffic density, especially where there are no dedicated bus lanes, causes delays and broken intervals" ([1news.az](https://1news.az/az/news/20261002150642203-Bakida-sinaga-chevrilen-avtobus-seferi-AYNA-sixliq-kondisioner-ve-davranish-qaydalari-ile-bagli-neleri-deyir)). The fleet-shortage half of the team's diagnosis is also on record: AYNA says **~1,000 buses still need replacing** ([operativmm.az](https://operativmm.az/en/post/baku-bus-fleet-to-be-modernized-by-2030/66473)), and it deflects driver-shortage questions to carriers while BakuBus advertises 1,350 AZN gross for drivers ([baku.ws](https://baku.ws/social/avtobus-suruculerinin-maaslari-artirilacaq-resmi-cavab); [bakucity.az](https://bakucity.az/bakubus-driver-vacancy-october-2026/)).

Three 2026 institutional shifts turn this chronic complaint into a procurement window. First, from September 2026 AYNA began paying carriers under **gross-cost ("brutto") contracts** on routes 130, 205, 211, 500 and 503: operators are paid per kilometre and service quality rather than per passenger, intervals are fixed per route, breaches carry financial penalties, and "a dedicated software tool" is supposed to monitor quality ([marja.az](https://marja.az/139391/dasiyicilara-mesafeye-gore-pul-odenilecek-bakida-avtobuslarla-bagli-yeni-sistem-ise-dusdu)). For the first time, headway adherence is a contractual KPI, and AYNA has never published an on-time or headway-regularity figure. Second, AYNA announced a **Data Warehouse** in July 2026 to unify public-transport, passenger-flow and vehicle-movement data with planned AI decision support and predictive analytics ([1news.az](https://1news.az/az/news/20260723132639398-Azerbaycanda-neqliyyat-sahesinde-Melumat-Anbari-yaradilir)). Third, in October 2026 AYNA tendered a **3 million AZN "mini" intelligent transport system** that analyses bus movements, fare payments and routes to detect delays, congestion and problem corridors, with an AI agent staff can query, explicitly framed against the earlier 110 million AZN police-run ITS centre that "largely failed to meet its goals" ([tezadlar.az](https://tezadlar.az/mini-niis-hazirlanir-ayna-iflasa-ugramis-evvelki-110-milyonluq-layiheden-ibret-goturecekmi/)). A hackathon prototype that computes and predicts headway regularity is, almost word for word, the thing AYNA has just put out to tender.

## The crossing idea is already built, including by Baku's own traffic police

Idea 1 is humane and the underlying safety problem is real. Nationally **39.9% of 2025 road accidents involved pedestrians** ([stat.gov.az](https://stat.gov.az/special_version/news/index.php?id=6619)), and in Baku **21 pedestrians were killed in Q1 2026 alone**, with 43 of 68 recorded accidents involving a pedestrian being struck ([Operativmm](https://operativmm.az/en/post/21-pedestrians-killed-in-baku-traffic-accidents-in-q1/71044)). Baku's engineering reflex has been grade separation, with 163 under- and overpasses built nationally in 20 years ([ereforms.gov.az](https://ereforms.gov.az/en/ekspert-yazilari/baki-ve-etraf-erazilerde-neqliyyat-infrastrukturunun-tekmillesdirilmesi-qlobal-cagirislar-ve-dovlet-proqraminin-rolu-149)), and residents have protested a 10-metre overpass that replaced a zebra crossing ([JAM News](https://jam-news.net/az/boyuk-baki-səddi-10-metrlik-piyada-kecidi-azərbaycan-paytaxtinin-sakinlərini-qəzəbləndirib/)). A pedestrian-first signal fits the hackathon's "human-centred cities" theme perfectly on paper.

The problem is novelty and ownership. Every feature in the team's list is in production somewhere. Demand-cancel and slow-walker extension have been standard on UK Puffin crossings since the 1990s ([DfT TAL 1/02](https://tsrgd.co.uk/pdf/tal/2002/tal-1-02.pdf)). Vienna has run **21 camera-based intent-detecting pedestrian signals since 2018**, with a 2024 second generation that recognises wheelchairs, rollators and strollers and extends green, processes images locally and deletes them within 50 ms, and reaches 99% intent accuracy; its stated goal is to replace ~200 push-button lights ([EurekAlert / TU Graz](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1066452); [BR24](https://www.br.de/nachrichten/wissen/warten-ade-fussgaengerampeln-schalten-automatisch-auf-gruen,UWBJpFY)). London's Green Person Authority shows continuous pedestrian green until a vehicle approaches, exactly the "immediate green on an empty street" feature, and measured **+13% pedestrian compliance, 1.3 pedestrian-hours saved per day per site and at most 11 s added vehicle journey time** ([TfL](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/media/press-releases/2022/february/new-tfl-data-shows-success-of-innovative-pedestrian-priority-traffic-signals)). Shenzhen's 2024 intersection that sets pedestrian green by the number of people waiting cut **average pedestrian wait 22.1% (65.7 to 53.8 s) and wasted vehicle green 70.8%** ([CNR Shenzhen](https://sz.cnr.cn/rdzt/xzsclsgcjt/zyjtkjcxxty/20240926/t20240926_526918285.shtml)). Singapore's Green Man+ gives seniors 3–13 s extra at more than 1,000 crossings ([Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/transport/seniors-to-get-longer-green-man-time-at-half-the-crossings-in-all-housing-estates-by-2027)). Judges who know the field will recognise the pitch as a repackaging, and the only defensible angle, a low-cost edge retrofit for pedestrian-only mid-block signals, is a hardware-and-procurement story that a two-week prototype cannot prove.

Worse for the pitch, Baku's own agency is already there. In December 2025 the Interior Ministry's NİİM installed its first adaptive signals in Binəqədi with specialists from two foreign countries, and the pilot sites explicitly include **the pedestrian signal in front of School No. 115** ([1news.az](https://1news.az/az/news/20251218100458812-Bakida-ilk-defe-agilli-svetoforlar-qurashdirilib-Tixacin-qarshisi-alinacaq-VIDEO); [hit.az](https://hit.az/az/hadise/292965/paytaxtda-ilk-ldquoailli-isiqforlarrdquo-gelir-qezalar-azalacaq/)). In February 2026 NİİM said the system holds main-direction green when the road is empty, specifically addressing night-time "long red on an empty road" complaints ([ENA.az](https://ena.az/az/news/id-70645)), and by March 2026 it reported speeds up 5–10 km/h at pilot sites with expansion contingent on results ([1news.az](https://1news.az/az/news/20260319015254246-NIIM-resmisi-Bakida-agilli-ishiqforlar-in-tetbiq-oldugu-yerlerde-neqliyyatin-sureti-artib-ACHIQLAMA)). NİİM centrally manages controller software at ~152 intersections ([Baku.ws](https://baku.ws/social/din-13-cu-xezer-beynelxalq-yol-infrastrukturu-ve-ictimai-neqliyyat-road-and-traffic-sergisinde-istirak-edir)), but no source names its controller models or protocols, its camera feeds are closed, and it sits under the Interior Ministry rather than the transport ministry that co-sponsors the youth ecosystem. A signal-control pitch therefore has no data, no interface spec and a buyer who has already chosen a foreign vendor.

The engineering realities also shrink the "AI" claim. Standards bound every lever the idea pulls: a vehicle minimum green of normally 7 s that "cannot be overridden by demands from vehicles, manual control devices, central computers, or linked controllers" ([DfT TSM Ch. 6](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/851465/dft-traffic-signs-manual-chapter-6.pdf)), a mandatory 3 s yellow and 3 s flashing green on pedestrian signals under GOST R 52289, the CIS framework Baku practice most resembles ([ConsultantPlus](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_348566/1a86b3cbce7898eb0ce04fa13a3df96493aaa6e1/)), and clearance speeds of 1.07–1.2 m/s. "Immediate green" is lawful only after minimum green plus the full change interval. Vienna's team found that rejecting passers-by without intent to cross "proved to be the biggest challenge" ([EU Urban Mobility Observatory](https://urban-mobility-observatory.transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/vienna-introduce-smart-traffic-lights-2019-06-27_en)), TfL found detector faults at 4 of 5 Puffin sites ([TfL Research Summary 8](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/research-summary-no-8-puffin-crossings.pdf)), and Boise scrapped a $600k camera-based adaptive system because glare and fog defeated detection ([KTVB](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/achd-scraps-adaptive-traffic-light-program/277-22736840)). A Raspberry Pi/YOLOv8 pedestrian counter reaches ~92.7% count accuracy ([ICISS 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3700706.3700717)), which is enough for group-size logic but not for safety-critical clearance decisions, so the honest controller is rule-based with a fail-safe. That is a sound design, but it leaves the "quality of AI use" rubric line, worth 15–25% in published hackathon rubrics, nearly empty.

## Headway control is field-proven, shortage-robust, and has a buyer

Idea 2 rests on the oldest result in transit operations: Newell and Potts showed in 1964 that bus motion contains a positive feedback loop in which a lagging bus collects more passengers, dwells longer and falls further behind, so "if buses are left to their own devices, they cannot stay on schedule," and the conventional remedy, timetable slack, "is sometimes worse than the illness" because it lowers commercial speed ([Daganzo & Pilachowski 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261510000858)). Traffic variability and missing trips are the two disturbance sources that seed the loop ([Delgado, Muñoz & Giesen 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261512000653); [Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231)). Baku, with heavy traffic and a fleet short by ~1,000 buses, sits in exactly the quadrant where schedules fail and headway-based control extracts the most value from whatever fleet is on the road.

The field record is strong and consistent, which matters because judges penalise simulation-only claims. Washington DC's WMATA headway-control experiment on routes 70/79 cut **passenger excess waiting time 26%, worth about US$1 million a year**, even though implementation "was far from ideal" ([Soza-Parra, Cats et al. 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0361198119845369)). Stockholm's in-bus headway display trials cut daily waiting time 10% and left drivers "less stressed," and the regularity-based approach is now written into SL's long-term operator contracts ([KTH impact case](https://www.kth.se/en/forskning/sarskilda-forskningssatsningar/strategiska-forskningsomraden/impactcases/regularity-based-bus-services-stockholm-1.935863)). Chicago CTA's 2022–23 pilots are the closest analogue to Baku: on route 81, where **15–25% of scheduled trips were cancelled** for lack of drivers, terminal holding cut waits 8.7% in the AM and 19.9% in the PM peak with only 35% driver compliance, and route 66 improved 5.8% while running 1.6 fewer trips a day ([Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231)). Georgia Tech's self-equalising rule "entirely avoided severe bunching" and, when one of three buses was removed, headways re-equalised at about 17 minutes ([Bartholdi & Eisenstein 2012](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/papers/Bartholdi-Eisenstein-2011.pdf)). Santiago's pilot cut regularity fines 40–50% at 25% compliance and lifted fare validations 20% ([Giesen et al., CASPT 2015](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/talk49.pdf)). At city scale, Singapore's EWT-based incentive framework reduced average additional wait **15% across 108 services and 25% across 292 services** within a year of rollout ([Straits Times, 2017](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/transport/sbs-transit-and-smrt-earn-high-marks-travel-time-and-safety-improvements)); the lesson there and in London is that contract incentives plus more service controllers moved the needle, with algorithms as the labour-saving layer. Baku has just adopted the contract half; the controller half is missing.

The evidence also dictates the architecture, and it is not reinforcement learning. A 2025 systematic RL evaluation found that simple state representations (forward and backward spacing) can outperform complex ones and that multi-agent methods add cost for marginal gain ([Xu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/tits.2025.3545642)); cooperative MARL holding beat a plain even-headway rule by only 1.5–3.2% on mean wait ([OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2577182)). The big gain, 20–60% headway-variance reduction, comes from any headway control versus none. CTA's deployed design wrapped its RL model in guardrails (limits on lateness and hold time) with a classical even-headway rule as fallback ([Rodriguez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231)), and that is the credible template: a two-way-looking headway rule as the controller, with machine learning doing the job it demonstrably does well, predicting link travel and arrival times. A GNN+LSTM model on 246 million Belo Horizonte records cut arrival-time MAE to ~13 s against ~26 s for mean-delay baselines ([ACM AI-ML Systems 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3703412.3703417)); gradient boosting on link times by time-of-day, updated with live positions, is enough to beat schedule baselines roughly twofold and gives the team a model it can name, cost, validate and show failing.

The data story, the usual weakness of a Baku transit pitch, is now solvable. AYNA publishes no GTFS and no real-time API, but its public map API exposes routes, stops and shapes for the full network, already scraped and re-released with a reproducible pipeline and GTFS exports on GitHub and Zenodo ([ismatsamadov/route_efficiency](https://github.com/ismatsamadov/route_efficiency)), and the **AYNA MaaS app, launched 5 December 2025, shows live vehicle positions per stop**, flagging when an ETA is schedule-based rather than GPS-based ([ayna.gov.az](https://ayna.gov.az/az/news/ayna-maas-neqliyyatda-mobillik-helli-tetbiqi-istifadeye-verilib-952); [ayna.gov.az update](https://ayna.gov.az/az/news/ayna-maas-tetbiqi-bir-qeder-de-tekmillesdirilib-1021)). Polling that backend for one to two weeks on a handful of routes yields the team its own AVL history: stop-arrival events from 30–50 m geofences, observed headways, link-time means and coefficients of variation, all calibrated to Baku. BakuBus daily boardings per route and metro station entries are on opendata.az under CC BY ([Samadov, engrXiv](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/8632/14011/12213)). The gap flag in AYNA's own app, and press reports of buses invisible in it ([redaktor.az](https://redaktor.az/news/country/347569-ayna-maas-da-melumatlar-niye-yenilenmir)), mean the model must handle missing vehicles, which is a feature to show, not a weakness to hide. The remaining caveat is legal: the map API has no published licence, so the team should describe the collection as a research snapshot with attribution and request an official feed through the organisers.

## Judges reward operator tools with buyers, and the best alternative is a module, not a rival

No rubric has been published for this hackathon; the organisers confirm only a Stage I of 150 participants, a Stage II of 10 finalist teams in Startup Demo Day format with AI mentoring, three awards, and an explicit link to the AI Strategy 2025–2028 ([Edumap](https://edumap.az/i-nsanyonumlu-seherler-hakatonu-kecirilecek)). Proxy rubrics converge: working live demo 20–30%, quality and necessity of the AI 15–25%, problem and measurable impact 20–25%, feasibility or business model 15–25%, presentation 10–15%. ITKAN's rubric defines excellent AI use as work "that would be hard without it," where the team "can explain which model, why, roughly what it costs, where it fails, how they check its output," and warns that hiding a faked component "costs a lot" ([ITKAN](https://www.itkan.one/I-Hackathon/judging)); DNHacks scores a "simple wrapper around an existing model" lower ([DNHacks](https://portal.dnhacks.org/e/2026/judging)). Azerbaijani demo days add team capability, MVP readiness, market potential and commercialisation ([startup.az](https://startup.az/menu/telebler-142); [StartupSchool.az](https://startupschool.az/en/xeber/9/Jury-Panel-announced-for-the-Startup-School-3-finale)). Winners confirm the pattern: TransitHack 2024 in Baku rewarded a carpooling algorithm, an all-in-one mobility app, a flood early-warning system and a congestion-pricing bottleneck study ([LinkedIn, Sofi-zada](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rafasofizada_after-6-months-of-preparation-and-work-with-activity-7260237475036516353-0ZdR)); the May 2026 Baku Metro hackathon, the first on an operator's own data, rewarded passenger-flow forecasting and digital-twin concepts and moved winners into internships and a pilot pipeline ([SUP.VC](https://sup.vc/metro-hackathon)); Istanbul's İETT hackathon rewarded a real-time stop-tracking app showing occupancy of the following vehicle ([Tech Istanbul](https://tech.istanbul/v2/en/inovasyon-maratonunda-kazananlar-belli-oldu/)). Smart traffic lights do not appear among winners anywhere in the 2022–2026 record the research covered.

Among the "better ideas," one stands out and should be absorbed rather than fought. Bus-mounted computer vision that detects cars blocking bus lanes and stops has the strongest evidence-to-cost ratio: New York's ACE programme reports **bus speeds +5% on average and up to +30%, collisions −20% and blocked stops −40%** across more than 1,600 equipped buses ([MTA](https://www.mta.info/press-release/mta-announces-bus-lane-camera-enforcement-expanded-include-new-violations)), Dubai reports −83% violations and +20% on-time arrival ([RTA Al Masar](https://www.rta.ae/links/magazine/masar/Al_Masar_132_Eng.pdf)), and Barcelona paid Hayden AI €100,000 for a four-bus pilot through a venture-client challenge ([Barcelona Digital City](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/digital/en/news/news/turning-bus-stops-into-data-centres-and-cameras-with-ai-to-improve-bus-lane-circulation-the-two-winning-projects-by-barcelona-innova-mobility-lab-1415817)). Baku already has **~117 km of bus lanes on 43 streets** with 100 AZN camera fines ([1news.az](https://1news.az/az/news/20261002150642203-Bakida-sinaga-chevrilen-avtobus-seferi-AYNA-sixliq-kondisioner-ve-davranish-qaydalari-ile-bagli-neleri-deyir)) and buses equipped with cameras whose footage police can request. The natural product logic is that the headway tool diagnoses where run-time variance originates (Swiftly markets its headway module precisely to decide "where would Transit Signal Priority have the biggest impact" ([Swiftly](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/think-like-your-riders-introducing-the-swiftly-headways-module))), and lane-blocking detection is the phase-two fix at those hotspots. Pitch it as a roadmap slide. Caveats to carry: an independent analysis found no observable speed gain on 15–16 NYC routes two months after citations began ([How Much Faster Are We Moving](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/87d8897e-0526-4b64-8769-54eb32823548/How-Much-Faster-Are-We-Moving%2520-%2520Final.pdf)), and no before/after evaluation of Baku's lanes exists beyond a press figure of bus speeds rising from 18 to 22 km/h ([Mincom](https://mincom.gov.az/en/media-en/news/roundtable-on-the-2025-state-program-for-the-improvement-of-transport-infrastructure-in-baku-and-surrounding-areas)).

The other alternatives are weaker fits. Congestion pricing is already legislated for Baku from 1 January 2027 ([APA](https://en.apa.az/infrastructure/azerbaijan-to-create-paid-traffic-zones-in-baku-478240)) and already won a TransitHack prize, so a simulation entry would be derivative; demand-responsive microtransit has a 50% failure rate within two years for tech-era schemes and costs $8–19 a ride ([Currie & Fournier](https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/bitstream/handle/2123/27188/Why%20most%20DRT%3AMicro-Transits%20fail.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1); [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2019/on-demand-transit-can-unlock-urban-mobility)); and parking guidance built on the "30% of traffic is cruising" meme is indefensible, since Shoup himself calls the generalisation apocryphal ([Shoup](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0xc27671/qt0xc27671.pdf)). A network-redesign research entry is credible, the 2026 preprint shows public data alone can yield a fleet-neutral redesign saving ~12% of buses ([Samadov, engrXiv](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/8632/14011/12213)), but AYNA is mid-rollout of its own "new network" and the Demo Day format favours a product with an operator in the room.

| Criterion | Idea 1: AI pedestrian-priority crossing | Idea 2 (sharpened): AI headway management for AYNA |
|---|---|---|
| Problem scale in Baku | Real (21 pedestrian deaths Q1 2026) but mid-block signals are a small share of exposure | ~1.3M daily riders; documented 20–60 min gaps on named routes |
| Novelty vs state of the art | Every feature already in production (Vienna, TfL, Shenzhen, Singapore, Puffin) | Method is classic, but no Baku headway KPI or control layer exists; AYNA is tendering for one |
| Field evidence to cite | TfL GPA +13% compliance; Shenzhen −22% ped wait | WMATA −26% EWT; CTA −8.7/−19.9% with 15–25% trips cancelled; Singapore −15–25% |
| Robust to Baku's fleet shortage | Not relevant | Yes, the core property of headway control |
| Obtainable data for a live demo | None public (NİİM feeds closed); own video only | Public map API + MaaS live positions; opendata.az boardings; GTFS exports exist |
| "Real AI" story | Pedestrian counting (~93%), rule-based controller | Link-time/arrival prediction with measurable error vs baseline, guard-railed controller |
| Buyer and pilot path | Interior Ministry NİİM; vendor already selected; safety-critical integration | AYNA/BakuBus; gross-cost contracts need monitoring; 3M AZN mini-ITS and DWH tenders; metro hackathon pilot precedent |
| Hackathon-winner precedent | None found 2022–2026 | Real-time info, forecasting and operator tools dominate winner lists |

## What to build, demo, cite, and never claim

Build the demo as a pipeline the judges can watch run. Ingest positions polled from AYNA's MaaS or map backend for two or three routes chosen for salience, either the complaint routes 49, 52 and 96 or the five gross-cost routes 130, 205, 211, 500 and 503, and snap them to the route shape to produce stop-arrival events. From those compute, per route, direction and hour, the metrics the industry actually uses: headway coefficient of variation graded on the TCQSM scale (below 0.21 "like clockwork," 0.40–0.52 "irregular with some bunching," above 0.75 "most vehicles bunched"), excess waiting time via AWT = 0.5·E[H]·(1+CV²) minus half the scheduled headway, and the share of intervals bunched (below 25% of scheduled) or gapped (above 150% or scheduled plus 5 minutes), following the NYCT, SFMTA and CTA conventions ([UMN Metro Transit report](https://cts-d10resmod-prd.oit.umn.edu/pdf/contract-14i072.pdf); [Furth & Muller 2006](http://www1.coe.neu.edu/~pfurth/Furth%20papers/2006%20Service%20reliability%20&%20hidden%20waiting%20time,%20Furth%20&%20Muller%20TRR.pdf)). Show the time–space trajectory plot, the one figure every paper in this field uses, with real Baku buses visibly converging. Then run an event-based single-line simulator, calibrated with the observed link-time means, CVs and boarding rates, and compare no control, terminal even-headway dispatch, and a two-way-looking en-route holding rule with CTA-style guardrails; open-source testbeds such as EXACT and RL-BusBunching already contain these controllers ([EXACT](https://github.com/chaojing-li/exact-experimental-adaptive-control-testbed-for-transit); [RL-BusBunching](https://github.com/zhandongxu/RL-BusBunching)). The two stress tests to perform live are removing a bus mid-run and injecting a traffic delay, demonstrating re-equalisation, which is the Georgia Tech result reproduced on Baku parameters. Front it with two thin interfaces: a dispatcher tablet view for the terminus showing a single instruction ("hold 90 s" / "depart"), because terminal dispatch regularity drove downstream regularity with R² of 0.87 in Bogotá and needs no en-route driver compliance ([Giesen et al.](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/talk49.pdf)), and a passenger view of "next two buses plus crowding," which lets riders self-sort and which real-time information studies show cuts measured waits by nearly 2 minutes ([Watkins et al. 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856411001030)). Use an LLM only as the natural-language query layer over computed KPIs, mirroring the "AI agent AYNA staff can query" in the agency's own tender.

Frame the human-centred story in minutes rather than algorithms. Excess waiting time is literally human time: Singapore pays operators per 0.1 minute of EWT, WMATA valued a 26% cut at a million dollars a year, and under Baku's new gross-cost contracts evening and off-peak service regularity is a stated objective ([marja.az](https://marja.az/139391/dasiyicilara-mesafeye-gore-pul-odenilecek-bakida-avtobuslarla-bagli-yeni-sistem-ise-dusdu)), so the pitch is that the same fleet, held smartly, gives a grandmother at a Yasamal stop a predictable 10-minute wait instead of a 40-minute gap followed by three full buses. The "who pays" slide names AYNA and BakuBus as venture clients under the State Program 2025–2030, cites the Baku Metro hackathon-to-pilot pipeline as precedent, and points to Tashkent's and Tbilisi's reforms as the regional pattern where fleet, cashless fares and apps arrived before headway management ([Gazeta.uz](https://www.gazeta.uz/en/2025/01/14/tashkent-buses/); [CEE Bankwatch](https://bankwatch.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2022-09-29_Tbilisi-urban-public-transit-reform.pdf)).

The claims to avoid are as important as the ones to make. Do not pitch reinforcement learning as the controller: reviews through 2025 note no multi-agent RL has been deployed in signal control and RL holding gains over a tuned headway rule are single-digit. Do not quote Shenzhen's "waiting time −33%" or Dubai's "punctuality +50%," whose metric definitions and baselines are unpublished ([Shenzhen Transport Bureau](https://www.sz.gov.cn/szzt2010/jjhlwzwfw/cgzs/content/post_11085848.html); [RTA](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/news-and-media/all-news/NewsDetails/50-percent-improvement-in-punctuality-index-of-buses-in-dubai)), nor vendor OTP figures from Swiftly or Optibus as if they were headway results. Do not claim access to AYNA GPS data or fare taps; say the positions were collected from the public app and the model is designed for the official feed. Do not claim bunching is purely a traffic problem, which is AYNA's own line, nor that software replaces bus lanes: the Stockholm line 4 package that cut irregularity-related waiting 25% combined holding with stop consolidation, third-door boarding and 3 km of new bus lanes ([Cats et al., CASPT 2015](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/paper11.pdf)). Do not promise compliance: state the 35–57% driver-compliance range from Chicago and the Donostia finding that aggressive holds without retraining tripled violations ([Argote-Cabanero et al.](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6j16889k/qt6j16889k_noSplash_c53214284cd5f0a420be45fe2676deca.pdf)), and present the terminal-first design as the response. If the team keeps idea 1 as a reserve slide, cite only the conservative comparables (TfL GPA, Shenzhen −22%), never Surtrac's 25–40% or Google Green Light's "up to 30%," which were measured against stale baselines ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/googles-project-green-light-uses-ai-to-take-on-city-traffic/)), and never describe "immediate green" without the minimum-green and change-interval constraints.

## Conclusion

The decisive asymmetry is not technical merit but timing and ownership. Idea 1 arrives after both the global field and Baku's own traffic police have shipped the concept, into an agency that shares no data and has already chosen a foreign partner; idea 2 arrives in the exact quarter when AYNA has made headway adherence a penalised contract term, admitted it lacks the tooling, and put money on the table for an AI analytics layer. A youth team cannot out-engineer Vienna's second-generation crossing camera in two weeks, but it can plausibly be the first party to publish a headway coefficient of variation for a Baku bus route, because nobody, including AYNA, has done so.

The deeper implication for the pitch is that "human-centred" and "AI" are best reconciled through measurement rather than automation. The humane output is minutes returned to a million riders a day; the AI is the prediction model that makes those minutes recoverable from a fleet that is not getting bigger fast enough; and the honest architecture, a classical rule guarded by learned predictions and a human dispatcher, is precisely what every successful deployment from Stockholm to Chicago actually used. Teams that frame AI as replacing judgement lose to the rubric line on failure modes; a team that shows its model's error, its fallback rule, and a live Baku bus re-equalising after a simulated breakdown will have the most "real" AI in the room.
