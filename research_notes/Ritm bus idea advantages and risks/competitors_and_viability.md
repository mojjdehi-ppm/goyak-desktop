# Competitive landscape and commercial viability: AI headway management / anti-bunching, stop-level crowd forecasting and reserve-bus dispatch software for Baku (AYNA, BakuBus, private carriers), 2024–2026

Research date: 2 November 2026. Currency: 1 USD ≈ 1.70 AZN (Trend converted AYNA's 3.046M AZN tender to "about $1.8M"). "Vendor" = vendor-published claim; "est." = my estimate.

## Key Question 1 — Who already sells this, at what price, and do they operate in Azerbaijan or nearby markets?

### Takeaway
Real-time headway control delivered to drivers is a niche inside two established product families: (a) US cloud overlays (Swiftly) priced per vehicle per year (~$300–$730 list per module, ~$1,500–$2,500 per bus/yr for a full stack) and (b) heavy CAD/AVL/ITCS suites (Clever Devices, INIT, Trapeze, NEC) sold as multi-million, multi-year hardware+software contracts. None of the Western vendors has a documented bus deployment in Azerbaijan; the Baku incumbents are a Turkish NEC subsidiary (AVL) and a local integrator (Az Software System), while Georgia's and Kazakhstan's bus systems run on local full-stack platforms (AzRy PTS, Avtobys).

### Inferences
- The "$100M Series B/C" entries for "Swiftly" on aggregator sites almost certainly belong to the unrelated retail-tech company Swiftly Systems; the transit Swiftly's documented equity is ~$12.5M plus two undisclosed growth-equity rounds (JMI 2020, Cove Hill 2025). Treat transit Swiftly as a PE-backed, revenue-stage company, not a venture unicorn.
- Realised Swiftly pricing clusters at ~$1,500–$2,500 per bus per year for a 4–5-module stack (LAVTA ≈ $101,940/68 ≈ $1,500; CCCTA ≈ $246,563/125 ≈ $1,970; Beaumont full stack $2,464 list-less-discount). Headway/driver guidance (Onboard App) is a $600-list add-on. Heavy CAD/AVL replacements cost $5–55k per vehicle (Kitsap, LA Metro) and take 3–4 years.
- No Western headway-management vendor has a bus reference in the Caucasus or Central Asia; the region is served by local/Turkish/Chinese full-stack platforms where "dispatch" means GPS monitoring and schedule-adherence reporting, not algorithmic headway control. The functional gap a startup would fill is real, but incumbents (AzRy, Avtobys, NEC, Yutong LINK+) can bundle a basic "interval monitoring" screen cheaply.

## Key Question 2 — Azerbaijan-specific incumbents and tenders (AYNA, BakuBus, Baku Metro): who is contracted, what is the AI-platform tender, who will bid?

### Takeaway
AYNA's software estate is dominated by one local integrator, Az Software System (≈31M AZN of AYNA contracts since Nov 2023, including 13.4M AZN in Oct 2026), with NEC Telekomünikasyon (Turkey) holding the bus AVL service contract and AzInTelecom hosting everything in the Government Cloud. The 3.046M AZN "AI Platform and Public Transport Network Diagnostics System" tender (bids due 24 Nov 2026) covers delay/slow-zone analysis, occupancy, demand peaks and O/D recovery — i.e., the analytics half of a headway/crowding product — but is an analyst-facing tool, not real-time driver control.

## Key Question 3 — How could a young local startup realistically get a pilot and get paid in Azerbaijan? Precedents, procurement rules, programmes

### Takeaway
The only live, AYNA-specific on-ramp is İRİA's "Openwave" open-innovation programme (TransportTech hackathon, Oct 2026, run on AYNA's real data; prizes are laptops, not contracts), plus Baku Metro's May 2026 hackathon that promised pilots. Paid work below 100,000 AZN can be bought by request for quotations (10 working days' notice; lowest price wins) and small out-of-plan purchases up to 10,000–20,000 AZN per year fall outside the procurement law entirely; single-source awards need the supervisory authority's consent. Grants are small (Youth Foundation ≤5,000 AZN per individual), but the KOBİA "Startup" certificate gives a 3-year profit-tax holiday.

**Public procurement rules (Law No. 988-VIQ, in force 1 Jan 2024)**
- Methods: open tender, two-stage tender, closed tender, request for quotations (kotirovka sorğusu), single-source — [e-qanun.az text](https://e-qanun.az/framework/54966). Art. 44.4: estimated price ≥100,000 AZN → open or two-stage tender; <100,000 AZN → request for quotations may be used; closed tender, single-source and framework agreements require the supervisory authority's consent regardless of value — [Prosecutor General's Office explainer](https://genprosecutor.gov.az/az/post/7582).

### Inferences

## Key Question 4 — Business-model options and evidence: SaaS per bus; performance-based under gross-cost contracts (Cabinet Decision No. 250 of 31 Jul 2026); selling to private carriers vs AYNA; donor funding

### Takeaway
Decision No. 250 creates a per-kilometre gross-cost payment (operating cost + capital cost + 10% margin) with fare revenue swept into AYNA's account, a monthly subsidy from the new "İctimai nəqliyyat" targeted budget fund, and contractual penalties (dəbbə pulu) deducted from the carrier's monthly payment — but the KPI/penalty schedule itself lives in each AYNA–carrier contract, not in the published rule. Crucially, dispatch and software costs are explicitly reimbursable cost items, so a carrier can pass a SaaS fee through the per-km rate. Private carriers (Xaliq Faiqoğlu: ~400 buses, losses of 3–5M AZN/yr) are cash-constrained and eager for gross contracts; donors fund metro, not bus software.

### Cited Findings

**Cabinet Decision No. 250 (31 July 2026) — "Rules for calculating and paying the subsidy for distance-based road-transport service by carriers performing regular passenger transport"** — [nk.gov.az (PDF, 11 pp.)](https://nk.gov.az/uploads/doc/docs/6a6c9fda82388.pdf)
- Scope (1.1): Baku, Sumqayıt, Gəncə, Naxçıvan, Xankəndi cities and Abşeron, Ağdam, Şuşa, Laçın districts; applies to carriers meeting requirements set by the Ministry under Presidential Decree No. 497 of 19 Sept 2025 and selected through the route competition rules (Cabinet Decision No. 52 of 2009) (1.4).
- Service value per km for bus class i: T_i = operating cost + capital cost + profit (2.2); operating cost = driver wages + social contributions + fuel/energy + maintenance + casco insurance + "other justified costs" (2.3). Driver wage benchmark = State Statistics Committee average for transport/warehousing × class coefficient (0.6/0.8/1.0) × regional coefficient 1.4 for Baku/Sumqayıt/Abşeron, 1.0 elsewhere (2.3.2). Profit M_ti = operating cost × R, with R = 0.1 (2.5).
- "Other justified costs" (2.3.9) explicitly include 2.3.9.21 "costs of organising the dispatcher service", 2.3.9.23 "software used on computer equipment", 2.3.9.26 "preparation and submission of statistical data", 2.3.9.27 "improvement of the carrier's production and management activities"; these are costed as a coefficient k_dx (0.755 down to 0.375 depending on total contracted km) × (fuel + maintenance) (2.3.10).
- Capital cost per km = new-bus price (incl. transport, customs) ÷ service-life mileage cap + leasing/credit interest per km (2.4); buses bought with state funds get no capital-cost reimbursement (3.10); buses beyond the mileage/age caps of Decree 497 may not be used (3.9).
- Monthly subsidy S_a = service value XD_a − fare revenue GH_a (3.1); XD_a = Σ(actual monthly km of class i × T_i) (3.2); **payment to carrier DÖ_a = XD_a − C_a, where C_a is "the total amount of contractual penalties (dəbbə pulları) applied under the contract"** (3.3).
- Fares are paid into the Agency's account; the service is paid from fare receipts, subsidies from the "İctimai nəqliyyat" Targeted Budget Fund and other lawful sources; subsidy paid monthly on invoice plus a signed acceptance act; surplus fare revenue on a route stays with the Agency and can cross-subsidise other carriers' routes (3.4); advances possible (3.5); annual reconciliation within 30 working days (3.7); rates revised yearly and on fuel-price changes, >10% repair-cost rises, devaluation or >10% inflation (3.8).
- The decision does not define the KPIs, penalty amounts or bonus terms; the penalty schedule is left to the contract ("müqavilə üzrə tətbiq olunan dəbbə pulları").

**Gross-contract rollout and eligibility**
- Presidential decree (Sept 2025) created the "İctimai nəqliyyat" targeted budget fund as the base for gross contracts; only carriers running new electric or CNG buses with cashless payment qualify; carriers "will be obliged to strictly comply with service-quality requirements and to fulfil the contract's daily kilometrage regardless of passengers carried"; target fleet for Baku and suburbs is 2,800 buses "to reduce intervals and keep optimal loading"; private orders in transit included Xaliq Faiqoğlu 54, Vətən-AzTrans 18, General Auto Company 10, Menlogg 16, Ayşən LTD 10, Fidan 6, Kənan 2000 and Çinar-Trans 20 — [Haqqin via Icma.az, 4 Nov 2025](https://icma.az/ru/news/ukaz-prezidenta-v-dejstvii-chastnye-perevozchiki-uskorenno-obnovlyayut-avtobusnyj-park-1195291).
- AYNA's Head of Strategic Planning Hüseyn Abdullayev (WUF13, 21 May 2026): "we are now at the final stage of developing a gross contract model for buses" and a new fare-payment system — [Report.az](https://report.az/en/infrastructure/ayna-gross-contracts-in-public-transport-will-develop-fare-payment-system).
- Xaliq Faiqoğlu MMC (largest private carrier; SAFA Holding): ~300,000 passengers/day; audited net losses 5.26M AZN (2021), 3.10M (2022), 3.57M (2023), 3.89M (2024), higher expected 2025; PASHA Bank opened 50M AZN credit lines over four years; 114 new buses contracted for 2025 (64 CNG, 50 electric); pays BakuBus "more than double" the tariff for charging; says gross contracts (pay per km plus quality/Euro-standard factors) have been discussed for over two years and asks for subsidy or per-passenger payment — [ENA.az interview, 31 Jul 2025](https://ena.az/az/news/id-66172); fleet ~400 buses, 3 depots — [Qafqazinfo, Sept 2025](https://qafqazinfo.az/news/detail/geceler-isini-bitiren-avtobuslar-hara-gedir-xaliq-faiqoglunun-qarajindan-reportaj-482246); won routes 71 and 206 (10 new 2025 buses on 71) and route 170 (15 buses, 8-minute interval) in 2025 — [Icma.az](https://icma.az/news/bu-marsrut-avtobuslari-xaliq-faiqoglu-na-verildi-1180593); [Qafqazinfo](https://qafqazinfo.az/news/detail/bu-marsrut-xetti-uzre-faiqoglu-xidmet-gosterecek-476712). EBRD lent it up to €6M for 35 buses in 2018 when it provided >20% of Baku bus services — [EBRD](https://www.ebrd.com/home/news-and-events/news/2018/ebrd-supports-modernisation-of-private-bus-operator-in-azerbaijan.html).
- Report.az publishes AYNA monitoring of bunching/delays route-by-route ("Bakıda 43/122/152/185 avtobusun hərəkət intervalında gecikmələr var") — [Report.az example](https://report.az/infrastruktur/bakida-185-avtobusun-hereketi-gecikir) — i.e., interval deviation is already a tracked and publicised metric.

**Donor funding relevant to a pilot**
- ADB: $302.38M Baku Metro signalling and digital operations loan (Oct 2026) — [ADB](https://www.adb.org/news/adb-approves-302-million-loan-modernize-baku-metro-signaling-and-digital-operations); up to $1B readiness for metro development within a $2B programme — [Report.az](https://report.az/en/finance/adb-ready-to-provide-azerbaijan-with-up-to-1b-loan-for-metro-development-exclusive); TA "Decarbonizing Baku Metro" $700,000 — [EWS/ADB 59165-001](https://ewsdata.rightsindevelopment.org/projects/ADB-59165-001/); TA "Supporting Baku Green Corridor Analysis" $0.17M (approved 28 Nov 2024) to pre-assess Azadliq Avenue (14.7 km) for LRT or BRT with battery-electric buses — [EWS/ADB 58414-001](https://ewsdata.rightsindevelopment.org/projects/58414-001-supporting-baku-green-corridor-analysis).

- State budget is the main bus funder: 33,449,000 AZN allocated to BakuBus for 60 e-buses in Nakhchivan — [APA](https://en.apa.az/official-news/president-ilham-aliyev-orders-transition-to-electric-buses-in-nakhchivan-city-461999); >600 new buses nationally in 2025 — [Mincom 2025 review](https://mincom.gov.az/en/ministry/history/2025en).

### Inferences
- Performance-based pricing is legally possible but must be contracted carrier-side: the published rule only guarantees that penalties (C_a) are deducted from the carrier's monthly payment; a startup could sell "share of avoided dəbbə" to a carrier once AYNA's contract template with interval/missed-trip penalties is public. Until then, the safer construct is a fixed SaaS fee that the carrier books under 2.3.9.21/2.3.9.23 and recovers through T_i (the per-km rate is rebased yearly, so the fee is passed through to the state with a 10% margin on top).
- Selling to AYNA directly aligns with who holds the data and the subsidy budget, but the AI tender shows AYNA buys analytics as integrator-built systems; selling to BakuBus (now inside Baku Metro CJSC, a commercial entity with ADB-funded digitalisation) or to Xaliq Faiqoğlu (loss-making, debt-financed, but motivated by gross-contract eligibility and interval compliance) gives a smaller but faster buyer.
- Donor TA budgets found are metro- or corridor-specific (ADB) with no bus-software line; an EBRD e-bus loan to a private operator could carry a technical-cooperation component (as EBRD did for Khaliq Faiqoglu in 2018), which is the most plausible donor route for a pilot.

### Gaps
- The AYNA gross-contract template (KPIs, penalty per missed trip/interval breach, bonus) is not public; the Decision only references "dəbbə pulları" generically. Presidential Decree No. 497 (19 Sept 2025) requirements (bus age/mileage caps) were not retrieved.
- Which routes/carriers have actually signed gross contracts as of Nov 2026 is unconfirmed (a Sept 2025 "pilot" announcement appeared only in a search snippet).
- No per-km rates (T_i values) have been published.
- No World Bank or EBRD technical-assistance programme for Baku bus operations in 2024–2026 was found.

## Key Question 5 — Size of the opportunity: buses, routes and cities; revenue at plausible per-bus prices; comparables

### Takeaway
Baku runs ~2,300 buses/day on ~209 listed route codes, targeting 2,800; Gəncə has 280 buses on 22 routes and Sumqayıt ~200+; the national addressable fleet is roughly 2,800–3,300 buses. At Swiftly-like prices ($1,500–2,500/bus/yr) the theoretical Baku ceiling is $3.5–5.8M/yr; at a locally plausible 500–1,000 AZN/bus/yr it is ~1.2–2.3M AZN/yr — comparable to a single NEC AVL service contract (2.1–2.6M AZN) and well below Az Software System's 13.4M AZN award.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Revenue math (est.): Baku 2,300 buses × $1,500–2,500 = $3.5–5.8M/yr at US prices; × 500–1,000 AZN = 1.15–2.3M AZN/yr at local prices; adding Gəncə (280) and Sumqayıt (~200–300) lifts the national ceiling ~20%. A single-carrier entry (Xaliq Faiqoğlu, 400 buses × 500–1,000 AZN) is 200–400k AZN/yr — within RFQ (<100k AZN per lot) territory only if split or phased, otherwise an open tender.
- AYNA's demonstrated willingness to pay (≈31M AZN to one integrator over three years; 2–2.6M AZN/yr for AVL SLA) is far larger than the whole per-bus SaaS ceiling, implying that the practical commercial prize is a slice of AYNA's integrator budget (subcontract or module licence) rather than a per-bus SaaS sold bus-by-bus.
- Investor comparables show exits are driven by multi-country scale (Optibus, Via, Moovit); a Baku-only product is a services/licensing business unless it exports to Gəncə/Sumqayıt-type regional rollouts and then to Tbilisi/Almaty-type markets where local full-stack vendors (AzRy, Avtobys) could be channel partners.

- No published per-bus budget line for ITS/software exists on the Azerbaijani side to calibrate willingness to pay per vehicle.

## Key Question 6 — Risks: single-customer dependence, long sales cycles, data access, incumbents bundling, political/organisational change

### Takeaway
The dominant risks are structural: one data owner (AYNA/AzInTelecom) and two entrenched vendors (Az Software System, NEC) whose contracts already cover analytics and AVL; a 3M AZN AI tender that bundles occupancy/demand/delay analytics into AYNA's own platform; a loss-making private-carrier base; and a sector in mid-reorganisation (BakuBus absorbed by Baku Metro in May 2026, gross contracts "at the final stage", AYNA's own systems being rebuilt). Baku's $100M+ ITS failure (2011) is institutional memory that cuts both ways.

### Cited Findings
- Incumbent bundling: AYNA's Oct 2026 AI tender buys delay/slow-zone, occupancy, demand-peak and O/D analytics with 1-year support — [BidDetail](https://www.biddetail.com/latest-tenders/119738634$0e0f32e7-e292-43d5-8f5f-5ce32855126c); Az Software System already built AYNA's "Analysis and Reporting System" and BRMS (Feb 2026) and holds the 13.4M AZN support contract (Oct 2026) — [ABC.az](https://www.abc.az/en/news/196683/ayna-to-spend-45-million-manats-on-software); [ABC.az](https://www.abc.az/en/news/218308/ayna-to-spend-134-million-manats-on-these-systems); NEC's AVL SLA includes "AVL Route Planning Update Professional Service (License-Free)" — [Pravda.az](https://pravda.az/news/178798).
- Data access: AYNA MaaS still shows interval-based (non-GPS) ETAs for some routes as of Feb 2026 — [AYNA](https://ayna.gov.az/az/news/ayna-maas-tetbiqi-bir-qeder-de-tekmillesdirilib-1021); no public GTFS/GTFS-RT feed or developer API for Baku was found (searches in EN/AZ returned nothing); hackathon access to AYNA data was event-scoped — [Techzone](https://techzone.az/en/transporttech-hackathon-ayna-iria-2026/).
- Organisational change: BakuBus and Baku Taxi merged into Baku Metro CJSC (May 2026) — [Trend](https://www.trend.az/azerbaijan/politics/4183615.html); AYNA is subordinate to the Ministry of Digital Development and Transport (RİNN) and a critic notes no published RİNN expediency opinion for the AI project — [Tezadlar](https://tezadlar.az/mini-niis-hazirlanir-ayna-iflasa-ugramis-evvelki-110-milyonluq-layiheden-ibret-goturecekmi/); the gross-contract model was still "at the final stage" in May 2026 — [Report.az](https://report.az/en/infrastructure/ayna-gross-contracts-in-public-transport-will-develop-fare-payment-system).
- Customer solvency: Xaliq Faiqoğlu reports four consecutive years of multi-million-manat losses and creditor arrears "making continued cooperation with those companies impossible" — [ENA.az](https://ena.az/az/news/id-66172).
- Procurement/transparency risk: AYNA's 6.78M AZN information-system contract went to a company in the tax-risk group — [Turan](https://turan.az/en/economics/creation-of-the-electronic-system-of-ayna-entrusted-to-company-listed-in-tax-risk-group); the 2.595M AZN AVL contract bypassed the procurement portal — [Pravda.az](https://pravda.az/news/178798). Under the procurement law, persons who were employees of a bidder within the prior 3 years cannot sit on the commission (6.2), and the law excludes procurements funded by international organisations where the agreement sets its own rules — [e-qanun](https://e-qanun.az/framework/54966); [Caspian Legal Center](https://caspianlegalcenter.az/news/more/procurement-law).
- Institutional memory: the 2011 Baku ITMS (SK C&C, >$100M) fell into disrepair for lack of maintenance and local adaptation — [World Bank 2018 note](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/963521580136216965-0080022020/original/BakuUrbanMobilityPolicyNoteJune2018.pdf); stop displays installed at ~$10k each never showed arrival times — [AzadMedia](https://azadmedia.az/siyaset/12552-ziya-memmedovun-100-milyonluq-intellektual-korrupsiyasi-ttham.html) (opposition outlet; treat as critical commentary).
- Sales-cycle evidence: Swiftly's own customers took 2–3 years to grow from two modules to five (CCCTA Dec 2022 → Aug 2024 → 2026) — [CCCTA board item](https://countyconnection.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/4.c.-Swiftly-Contract.pdf); LAVTA justified Swiftly as sole-source because modules "must be procured directly from Swiftly to integrate with existing modules" — [LAVTA](https://www.wheelsbus.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/7.1_SR_Swiftly-SaaS-Agreement.pdf), illustrating lock-in that benefits incumbents.

### Inferences
- Single-customer dependence is near-total: AYNA sets routes, holds fare revenue (Decision 250 §3.4), owns AVL/validator data and will own the DWH and AI platform. A carrier-side product only works if AYNA grants (or the carrier independently collects) vehicle and boarding data; the TransportTech precedent shows AYNA will share data for prototypes but nothing shows it will share for a commercial third-party service.
- The AI tender's winner will have a contractual claim on "occupancy analysis" and "demand peaks" inside AYNA; a startup pitching crowd forecasting to AYNA after Nov 2026 competes with a just-procured system — the differentiated wedge is real-time control (driver holding/speed guidance, reserve-bus insertion), which neither the tender nor NEC's AVL SLA covers.
- The merger of BakuBus into Baku Metro, and ADB's $302M digital-operations loan, make Baku Metro CJSC a second, better-funded buyer whose procurement will follow ADB rules (international competitive bidding), which cuts against local-preference advantages but opens a donor-funded path.

### Gaps
- No public statement on AYNA's data-sharing policy for third parties or on whether the AI tender winner receives exclusivity.
- Post-merger governance of bus dispatch (Baku Metro vs AYNA) is undefined in available sources.
- The outcome of the Nov 2026 AI tender and any 2027 AYNA restructuring are unknown at research date.
