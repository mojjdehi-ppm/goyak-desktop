# Disadvantages, failure modes and hidden costs of headway-based bus control, AI dispatch tools and reserve-bus dispatching (2010–2026)

*Scope note for the report writer.* This brief deliberately collects the negative evidence. Tags used throughout: **[FIELD]** = measured in real operations; **[SIM]** = simulation/analytical model only; **[VENDOR]** = vendor-authored; **[AGENCY]** = agency document/presentation; **[UNION/ACTIVIST]** = union or activist source; **[PRESS]** = news report; **[FORUM/BLOG]** = anecdotal. "Holding" means delaying a bus at a terminal or stop to even out spacing. Baku context supplied by the coordinator: ~2,500 buses, heavy traffic, fleet/driver shortage, state operator plus private carriers, gross-cost contracts since Sept 2026, 89% cashless, GPS on most buses with known gaps; Baku-specific implications are flagged as *inferences*, since no Baku-specific headway-control evidence was found.

## Key Question 1 — Driver acceptance: compliance rates, objections, in-cab distraction, gaming, and what agencies did

### Takeaway
Measured driver compliance with holding instructions in real pilots is low to middling (35% at a Chicago terminal in 2022; 57% en-route in 2023 even with a supervisor physically present; roughly one-third of Boston adjustments "not implemented as intended" in 2024), and the most senior drivers are the least compliant; the recurring objections are schedule culture, lost break/recovery time, distrust of inaccurate displays, and controller "harassment" by radio, which has produced formal union instructions not to answer radios (Dublin 2024), threats of industrial action (London 2016), a covered-up dashboard pilot (Toronto 2015) and alert redesigns (Honolulu 2014).

### Cited Findings
- **CTA Chicago, Route 81 terminal holding (17–21 Oct 2022, AM/PM peaks) [FIELD, preprint]:** 158 trips observed, 80 (50.6%) received adjusted departures, only 28 of 80 instructions complied with (35%); compliance fell the further the instructed departure was from the schedule, and non-compliant trips tended to leave closer to schedule, often later than instructed. — [arXiv 2509.08231 (Rodriguez et al., 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)
- **CTA Route 66 en-route holding (6–17 Nov 2023, PM rush, three rail-transfer control stops) [FIELD, preprint]:** 174 holding instructions logged, 99 compliant (56.9%); the higher rate is attributed partly to "a supervisor physically enforcing the hold at the stop". Short-turning was used only 8 times. — [arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)
- **Reasons for non-compliance recorded by CTA supervisors:** schedule-punctuality culture persisting even after drivers were told punctuality enforcement was paused; fear that late departures cut into end-of-trip break time; at stops just before a signal, drivers left early to avoid waiting a whole cycle; reluctance to hold where the bus blocks traffic. Non-compliant trips had higher-seniority drivers in both pilots (authors note possible selection bias). Route 66 supervisors had no direct radio line to drivers and had to meet them at stops. — [arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)
- **CTA recommendations (what the agency was told to do):** train drivers on benefits, "consider relaxing schedule start/end times to guarantee breaks", use incentives for senior drivers, pick holding stops that are existing timepoints or transfer stops to reduce retraining, give supervisors better field tools, and for routes without supervisors use on-board digital instructions or radio. — [arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)
- **MBTA Boston, Route 1 pilot (Wednesday 4 Sept 2024, 6am–6pm, one dedicated dispatcher) [AGENCY/FIELD]:** bunching fell 38–40% versus comparison Wednesdays, but "roughly one-third of adjustments were not implemented exactly as intended"; adjustments were radioed to operators or relayed via the Nubian inspector; operator displays (MDTs) "do not show headways". — [MBTA, "Making Headway" deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent)
- **Transantiago/Santiago driver survey (two routes equipped with an onboard headway-control tool) [FIELD survey, 2022]:** "experienced drivers (over 50 years) perceive being faster and maintaining more regular headways than younger ones. They also appear to be more reluctant to use onboard headway control tools. Less experienced drivers recognize the accuracy of the information delivered and assure that it has improved their driving performance." — [Martínez-Estupiñán, Delgado, Muñoz & Watkins, Transportmetrica A, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/23249935.2022.2025950)
- **Santiago follow-up [SIM, 2023]:** holding tools assume identical drivers; driver speed heterogeneity raises headway variability, and "an easy way of improving headway regularity is to stratify drivers by line according to historical driving speed, regardless of whether or not a control action is applied". — [Martínez-Estupiñán et al., Transportation Research Part A 174, 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transa/v174y2023ics0965856423001817.html)
- **Santiago pilot of a real-time holding tool (Lizana, Muñoz, Giesen, Delgado 2014) [FIELD, secondary]:** a BRT Centre of Excellence slide deck reports regularity fines on the pilot day dropped "around 50%" and claims "time savings of over 50%"; these figures are from slides, not the peer-reviewed paper, and sample sizes/durations were not found. — [BRT CoE slides, 2012](https://es.slideshare.net/BRTCoE/2012-1214-bus-bunching)
- **Stockholm RETT3 field experiment (autumn 2012) [FIELD]:** "The project dealt with many challenges such as radio communication problems which limited its success... The recurrent technical problems have made it very difficult for drivers to follow and trust the system." Excess waiting time still fell 5–10% overall (up to 45% at some stops). — [Cats, RETT3 final report (TRID)](https://trid.trb.org/View/1375940)
- **Berrebi et al. field implementations (Atlanta Streetcar, Georgia Tech Red Stinger, VIA Primo 100 San Antonio, spring 2017) [FIELD]:** operators at the Atlanta control point "often missed their permissive phase and had to wait for the entire 120-second cycle. On average, vehicles held for 107 seconds longer than recommended"; "indirect lines of communication can introduce layers of error"; "Knowing they were monitored, operators had a good reason to follow instructions with more diligence than usual"; authors conclude operators "are the best positioned to make the final judgment call, and should therefore be provided holding instructions directly" (tablets/kiosks). — [CASPT 2018 paper](https://caspt.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Papers/CASPT_2018_paper_93.pdf); [Mass Transit, Oct 2017](https://www.masstransitmag.com/technology/article/12358944/from-research-to-practice-implementing-real-time-control-to-avoid-bus-bunching); [Transportation Research Part A 111 (2018) 213–226](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transa/v111y2018icp213-226.html)
- **VIA San Antonio Primo 100 terminal-holding pilot (spring 2017) [AGENCY case study]:** "Operators (and the union) were used to the traditional system and not always eager to adjust. The agency felt they would need in-person dispatchers in order to explain and enforce the terminal dispatching, which would be untenable in the long-term." Dispatchers also "had to sort out issues when operations did not align with the established relief schedule" and the agency "found it difficult to transition back to the non-headway based terminal dispatching when service levels decreased in the evenings". — [Sam Schwartz Engineering for TransitCenter, "Elevated Standards for Bus Operations – Scheduling and Dispatching", 30 Aug 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- **Agency attitudes (SSE/TransitCenter 2019 case studies of 8 US agencies) [AGENCY]:** Maryland MTA listed "Increasing on-street staff, as headway management is viewed as needing more supervision to ensure that operators are accountable"; MTA, MBTA and NYCT "conveyed that even the best technology cannot substitute for staff presence, and that operators take performance more seriously when they have in-person supervision"; "Agencies may be reluctant to have operators making service adjustments or feel that they would be distracted by onboard headway management." — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- **Cap Metro Austin MetroRapid (six-month headway pilot, reported 2019) [VENDOR]:** operators "trained for years to follow a schedule had difficulty shifting to a mindset where schedules matter less"; retraining "took longer than expected". — [Swiftly blog, 15 June 2019](https://goswift.ly/blog/mind-the-gap-best-practices-in-headway-management)
- **London iBus headway displays — driver experience [FIELD qualitative, 2014]:** Newcastle University interviews found drivers felt controller instructions "deskilled them"; drivers admitted "forgoing their usual considerate nature" if the bus behind got too close; passengers "got frustrated with them for driving too slowly"; recommendation that "future systems should grant drivers more autonomy". — [Newcastle University press release on CHI 2014 paper](https://www.ncl.ac.uk/press/articles/archive/2014/05/digitallydriven.html)
- **London Assembly private roundtable with 15 bus drivers (9 Feb 2017) [UNION/driver testimony]:** "Nowadays there is a system that runs off of the iBus. Yes, it maintains the headways but sometimes it means bus drivers are forced to finish an hour late."; a controller: "the pressure on the controller to turn the buses is immense. Sometimes it is out of date. I could be looking at you moving but you are two bus stops behind"; "The recovery time during the day at the end of each trip is essential. It is being absorbed into running time now because of the QICs message"; Quality Incentive Contract bonuses mean "To maximise their profit through QICs they have to push and push and push." — [London Assembly transcript (PDF)](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/18186/download)
- **TfL's official position on in-cab headway displays (Mayor's Question answer, 23 Jan 2024) [AGENCY]:** the MDT shows "a schematic ladder" of the bus relative to the one in front; "the MDT is not used as a target reporting system"; controller interventions "will not include asking a driver to increase their speed and the MDT display is only active when the vehicle is stationary." The question itself compared the display to DPD's discontinued "traffic light" delivery-target system. — [London Assembly MQ](https://www.london.gov.uk/who-we-are/what-london-assembly-does/questions-mayor/find-an-answer/dangerous-bus-contract-incentives-ibus-monitor-displays)
- **London drivers' grievances (2016) [ACTIVIST]:** "if a bus is held up, then every bus behind it has to slow down, sometimes to a complete stop to maintain headway. Passengers complain... the bus is at a standstill, sometimes on an empty road or at a bus stop for up to five minutes. This is paving the way for drivers to be abused and assaulted"; "The headway system is also inaccurate and drivers could, according to the meter, be ten minutes behind the bus in front but turn a corner to find they are right behind it"; garages discussing grievances and industrial action. — [Socialist Party, 24 Feb 2016](https://www.socialistparty.org.uk/articles/22290/24-02-2016/stress-abuse-and-long-hours-for-london-bus-drivers/)
- **Dublin Bus dispute (Nov 2024) [PRESS/UNION]:** SIPTU and NBRU "issued a notice advising drivers, 'for safety reasons, not to operate their radios while in service'" after controllers called drivers running early to hold at stops; drivers said the in-cab PressIt boxes "can be 'inaccurate' and 'frustrating', while some routes lack 'reasonable places' to pull over"; standard is no more than 5 min 59 s late and no more than 1 min early; PressIt boxes standard since 2010. — [Irish Independent, 8 Nov 2024](https://www.independent.ie/regionals/dublin/dublin-news/dublin-bus-drivers-in-dispute-over-claims-of-distracting-calls-to-maintain-route-times/a1620070543.html)
- **Honolulu TheBus text alerts (Oct 2014) [PRESS]:** drivers said MDT beeps for "running ahead of schedule" messages were startling and dangerous; management agreed to "reduce the frequency of the alerts" and asked staff "to see if we can modify our system so messages are not sent if the bus is moving". — [Hawaii News Now, 1 Oct 2014](https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/story/26671885/exclusive-city-bus-drivers-complain-text-alerts-compromise-safety/)
- **Toronto TTC (Jan 2015) [PRESS]:** union head Bob Kinnear blamed "outdated route schedules"; as a pilot "the TTC will cover up the dashboard on-time indicator on the East Mall route to see how it affects safety and schedules"; CEO Byford: "Safety trumps on-time running every single time." — [CBC News, 22 Jan 2015](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/ttc-wants-technology-to-boost-bus-safety-1.2927975)
- **London iBus controllers (2021) [FIELD qualitative]:** an Applied Ergonomics study found "all participating controllers expressed being overloaded with work" and that drivers and controllers "often misunderstand the role of the other". — [World Transit Research record](https://worldtransitresearch.info/research/8792)
- **WMATA Route 79 "Headway Management by Operator" (from June 2018) [AGENCY]:** operators got a mounted smartphone (MetroHero app) showing distance to leader/follower; WMATA "moved the relief location... to the terminal (previously mid-route)" and "revised its on-time performance metric for headway-based routes"; "initial operator feedback has been positive". — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- **Barcelona TMB [AGENCY explainer]:** regulation is done mainly at terminals ("Les alteracions petites de la regularitat es compensen al final del recorregut"); severe irregularity is fixed by "una llarga" (bus runs empty, skipping stops); regulation is most needed on lines at 5–8 min intervals; network regularity ~85% on weekdays. No driver-compliance data published. — [GenTMB](https://gentmb.tmb.cat/seccio/mon-bus/com-funciona-regulacio-dels-intervals-entre-cotxes)
- **Donostia Dbus (2023–24) [PRESS]:** instead of real-time control, Dbus lengthened running times and headways (line 13: 6–7 → 7–8 min; line 26: 12 → 15 min) explicitly to meet timetables and "permitir los descansos estipulados de los trabajadores, sin llevar a cabo inversiones añadidas"; the works council unions (UGT, Kurpil, ELA) did not accept the management report; delayed trips then fell 59% (line 13) and 72% (line 26) with ridership up 9.5–10.4%. — [Noticias de Gipuzkoa, 26 Sept 2023](https://www.noticiasdegipuzkoa.eus/donostia/2023/09/26/dbus-estudia-cambiar-recorridos-frecuencias-alargar-autobuses-7301992.html); [Cadena SER, 18 Oct 2023](https://cadenaser.com/euskadi/2023/10/18/los-cambios-de-horarios-y-frecuencias-en-dbus-no-convencen-a-los-trabajadores-radio-san-sebastian/); [El Diario Vasco, 28 Apr 2024](https://www.diariovasco.com/san-sebastian/dbus-buenos-cambios-seis-lineas-20240428065709-nt.html)

### Inferences
- Across every field pilot found, compliance depended on physical supervision or direct in-cab instruction; remote instructions relayed through dispatchers or radio degrade quickly. For Baku, with a mixed state/private driver pool and a driver shortage, a product that assumes 80–90% compliance would be over-estimating by roughly a factor of two relative to the Chicago and Boston evidence.
- The strongest objections are not about technology but about pay and breaks: holding eats recovery time and extends shifts. Under gross-cost contracts the operator bears the overtime, so operators and drivers both have reasons to resist holds unless schedules build in guaranteed layover.
- Senior-driver resistance means the drivers with the most route knowledge are the least likely to follow algorithmic holds; the Santiago work suggests sorting drivers by speed profile is a cheaper lever than more control instructions.
- In-cab displays are safe only if locked while moving (TfL, Honolulu) — a product requirement that also reduces how often instructions can be delivered.

### Gaps
- No agency publishes routine compliance rates; the only quantified figures are from two short Chicago pilots and one Boston day. No TriMet, Barcelona or Donostia compliance data were found.
- No formal ATU or Unite position paper specifically on headway holding was found (union statements found concern cameras/telematics, fatigue and radio harassment).

## Key Question 2 — Passenger reaction to holding: complaints, on-board delay vs waiting-time gains, net time loss, communication

### Takeaway
Holding is a redistribution: riders downstream wait less, riders on the held bus and at upstream stops wait longer; agencies admit that "without clear and consistent on-board announcements, customers question why a bus is idling", and London — the longest-running large-scale headway regime — generates persistent public complaints about buses sitting for up to 3–5 minutes on clear roads. The one field study that separated effects (Stockholm 2014) found holding added ~20 s of in-vehicle time per rider and had "no significant effect on average travel time"; models show net passenger time *loss* when slack is large or fleets are tight.

### Cited Findings
- "Holding may benefit downstream passengers by equalizing intervals of buses that come after the bus being held, but it worsens service for passengers already on, or who will be picked up by the bus being held." Case-study agencies viewed holding at timepoints "as somewhat problematic, as without clear and consistent on-board announcements, customers question why a bus is idling." — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- **MBTA 2024 pilot lessons [AGENCY]:** "Some riders wait longer so that many others benefit"; "Most adjustments are trade-offs that inconvenience some riders or personnel"; the agency identifies "a misalignment between" the promise of walk-up frequent service and how it is delivered, and recommends adapting "real-time predictions and public screens". — [MBTA deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent)
- **CTA 2022–23 [FIELD]:** on Route 66 "stops before the holding points were equal or worse; savings were significant downstream"; average wait saving only 0.3 min/rider (5.13 → 4.83 min) versus 1.6 min/rider on Route 81 PM (8.28 → 6.63 min); in-vehicle delay from holds "not quantified"; on short-turned trips riders were asked to alight and wait. The authors claim riders perceive holding favourably "because more riders can board", but report no passenger survey. — [arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)
- **Stockholm trunk line 4 field experiment (17 Mar–19 Jun 2014, package of bus lanes, stop removals, third-door boarding and even-headway holding) [FIELD]:** "the time passengers spent in vehicles that were holding increased by 20 seconds due to the headway based holding strategy... Headway based holding did not have a significant effect on average travel time"; waiting time fell 35% with all measures combined; "Approximately half of the regularity improvements are attributed to the headway-based holding." — [West & Cats, TU Delft 2015](http://resolver.tudelft.nl/uuid:5454500a-1e84-4a3c-9759-e18d725da02e); excess waiting time -27–29%, trip time -10% (19.5 → 17.5 min), "82% of the time savings were obtained through shorter in-vehicle times" — [CASPT 2015 paper](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/paper11.pdf)
- **WMATA routes 70/79 (2018–19) [FIELD]:** 26% reduction in passenger excess waiting time, "annual time savings that translate into US$1 million", but "the field experiment implementation was far from ideal". — [Soza-Parra, Cats, Carney & Vanderwaart, TRR 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0361198119845369)
- **London complaints (2026) [COMMENT, former transport minister Norman Baker]:** buses held "up to three minutes" in light traffic while cars pass; a bus held "for some minutes" before Clapham Junction so he nearly missed a train; "Nothing is more likely to put people off using buses"; argues regulation belongs at the terminus, is "pointless" on a 5-minute frequency and acceptable only at 20-minute-plus frequencies or first/last buses. — [Passenger Transport, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.passengertransport.co.uk/?p=21707)
- **London rider account (Feb 2018) [BLOG]:** four "Please wait while we regulate the service" holds in a ~30-minute midnight journey a car does in 5–10 minutes; passengers "worried about missing connections to the last train", "banged their feet and asked what was happening"; the bus had left on time, so holds reflected a timetable padded for daytime traffic. — [From The Murky Depths](https://fromthemurkydepths.co.uk/2018/02/04/please-wait-while-we-regulate-the-service)
- **London rider/enthusiast forum (2024–25) [FORUM]:** "up to a dozen might be regulated due to one bus delayed"; one poster proposes headway holding only "when advertised headways are twenty minutes or more"; another: "good in principle, [but] doesn't work very well in practice". — [RailUK Forums](https://www.railforums.co.uk/threads/tfl-managed-decline.225110/post-5723743)
- **Drivers on passenger reaction (London 2017):** "The most common complaint a bus driver will get is, 'I have been waiting 20 minutes for you'. 'Well, no, you have not. You were waiting for the bus that was late. I am actually on time.'" — [London Assembly transcript 2017](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/18186/download); the 2016 activist account links holds on empty roads to abuse and assault of drivers — [Socialist Party 2016](https://www.socialistparty.org.uk/articles/22290/24-02-2016/stress-abuse-and-long-hours-for-london-bus-drivers/)
- **Barcelona [AGENCY]:** TMB acknowledges that a regulating express run ("una llarga") that passes a stop "pot generar decepció i incomprensió entre els usuaris". — [GenTMB](https://gentmb.tmb.cat/seccio/mon-bus/com-funciona-regulacio-dels-intervals-entre-cotxes). A 2024 Barcelona ombudsman case (line V23, stop 3371): rider documented 13–15 min gaps instead of 8, followed by two buses together, Sept–Dec 2023; TMB's aggregate data showed 8–9 min average and compliance, while admitting "valors d'intervals puntuals més elevats" — i.e., aggregate regularity KPIs hid the bunching the rider experienced. — [Sindicatura de Greuges de Barcelona, 2024](https://www.sindicaturabarcelona.cat/wp-content/uploads/202405_Dret-mobilitat_linia-V23-dautobus-incompleix-sistematicament-la-frequencia-de-pas.pdf)
- **Modelled net time loss [SIM/THEORY]:** "sometimes excessive holding increases passengers' total travel time" because slack raises dwell/in-vehicle time and, with a limited fleet, raises the dispatch headway; in a Chengdu case the controlled system's advantage reverses at a demand ratio of 1.2 because "less amount passengers can be served in a unit of time compared with uncontrolled case". — [Journal of Advanced Transportation 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/9714046). Schedule-based holding "require[s] too much slack, which slows buses. This delays on-board passengers and increases operating costs." — [Xuan, Argote & Daganzo, TR Part B 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transb/v45y2011i10p1831-1845.html). Static threshold holding means "passengers on board the bus are penalized by extra delay"; dynamic thresholds cut that penalty "at the expense of a slight increase in overall passenger system time". — [Transportation Research Part A, 1998](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856498000196)
- **Where to hold:** the optimal control point sits "just before a group of stops at which many passengers are boarding"; where many board at the origin "the best strategy is to ensure that vehicles depart on time from the route origin rather than detain them en route"; estimated wait reductions from holding on a Los Angeles route were only 11–15%. — [Abkowitz & Engelstein, TRR 961, 1984](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/trr/1984/961/961-001.pdf)
- **Literature reviews:** "most bunching solutions prioritize operators over public transport users" and hybrid methods "need real-world testing" — [Rezazada et al., Transport Reviews 2024](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/10327); "there is a large gap between the state-of-the-art and the state-of-practice" with "organisational, contractual and technological barriers" — [Tirachini et al., Transport Reviews 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/transr/v42y2022i3p337-361.html)

### Inferences
- The passenger case for holding rests on total wait-time arithmetic that riders cannot see; every documented rollout that lacked an explicit announcement and a visible reason generated "why are we sitting here" complaints. A Baku product should budget for on-board/app messaging and expect complaints to concentrate on held buses in light traffic (evenings, weekends).
- Net benefit shrinks when (a) holds happen mid-route with many through-riders, (b) the fleet is tight so slack lengthens headways, and (c) demand is near capacity — all three describe Baku trunk corridors at peak. Terminal holds (empty bus) dominate mid-route holds on both passenger and acceptance grounds.
- Aggregate regularity KPIs (TMB ~85%) can coexist with severe local bunching; a vendor dashboard showing improved average headway CoV is not evidence riders felt it.

### Gaps
- No agency-run passenger survey on holding was found; complaint statistics attributable to holding (e.g., London TravelWatch categories) were not located. London TravelWatch mentions "gaps in service and compulsory stops" as complaint themes but without counts.
- A widely cited London Countdown finding (perceived wait fell from 11.9 to 8.6 min even as reliability declined) appeared only via a search summary attributing it to TCRP Synthesis 48; primary URL not verified.

## Key Question 3 — Operating-cost side effects: cycle times, fleet, layover, overtime, capacity, and agencies that turned holding off

### Takeaway
Agencies describe headway management as "a resource hog": it needs dedicated dispatchers/supervisors, terminal layover space, relief at terminals, flexible rosters and often overtime; holding adds to cycle time and, with a fixed fleet, lengthens effective headways, so the capacity cost is real even where simulations say extra buses are not needed. Several agencies have quietly dropped or declined it (LA Metro Rapid in the early 2000s; Cap Metro's 2018 terminal pilot; VIA's 2017 pilot judged "untenable"; King County and Maryland MTA chose not to), and a 2021 TCRP survey found only nine agencies with headway-based service on 15 routes in total.

### Cited Findings
- "The primary challenge of headway management is its demands on resources compared to traditional timepoint scheduling – staff, communication systems, technology, etc. As one agency put it, headway management is 'a resource hog.' This is in part due to the need for flexibility in staff and vehicles, as well as costs associated with technology training and hardware/software." — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- Maryland MTA's list of practical obstacles: interlined routes; "Eliminating mid-route relief to only having relief at terminals, which could be more resource intensive"; "Ensuring breaks and relief that meet contractual obligations"; on-board systems and enhanced communications; more on-street staff. King County Metro: headway management "would require more flexible terminal operations, including creating or dedicating indoor space for operators and terminal managers" and a way "to transition between headway management and standard scheduled operations at different times of day". Four of eight case-study agencies had no plans to implement. — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- Dispatcher capacity: MBTA "reported having as few as six dispatchers for the system's almost 180 bus routes, and only one dedicated dispatcher for its 19 frequent routes" (2019); in 2024 MBTA said dispatchers and inspectors are "overloaded compared to peer agencies", was hiring toward 26 active dispatchers, and found "Lack of layover space at terminals limits terminal holds". — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf); [MBTA deck 2024](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent)
- Layover norms and cost: "Providing ten percent of the trip running time as layover/recovery is common practice, but it could be as high as 15 or 20 percent for particularly unreliable routes"; layover is paid time the operator "owns". Standby-bus insertion "can generate unbudgeted labor and fleet costs. If this strategy is employed at peak times, the cost may be disproportionately greater; operators may only be available on overtime, and the peak fleet may already be on the road." — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- CTA pilots added staff: a dedicated supervisor on Route 81 and two supervisors driving between stops on Route 66, plus a remote research assistant; the authors argue supervisor costs are "a fraction of adding buses and drivers" (no figures). — [arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)
- Overtime/fatigue as the hidden cost in London: drivers "forced to finish an hour late"; recovery time "absorbed into running time"; "They leave you to run and run and run." — [London Assembly transcript 2017](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/18186/download). Unite's survey of >2,000 drivers (late 2025) reports a third saying conditions are "much worse" since 2019 and links fatigue to schedules. — [SHP Online on Unite survey](https://www.shponline.co.uk/safety-management/clock-ticking-for-tfl-to-act-on-bus-workers-fatigue-says-unite/)
- Holding and cycle time [SIM]: "Holding vehicles lead to higher cycle times that could also lead to more buses. However, the effects of holding on the total cycle time do not lead, in general, to the need of more buses"; "Cycle times are important for the operator, because it determines the number of buses needed, in case the frequency of a line is predefined." — [van der Werff, TU Delft thesis 2017](http://resolver.tudelft.nl/uuid:d5d3d495-439c-49b1-bd57-f45c46041d12). Headway- and schedule-based strategies perform "generally equal in regular, undisrupted situations" and schedule-based "are currently easier to implement". — [van der Werff et al., TRR 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0361198119845893)
- Field evidence of longer holds/headways: the real-time method "required longer holds in some cases"; on the Atlanta Streetcar it "dispatched buses with slightly longer headways, 622 seconds on average, compared to 584 seconds for the schedule method". — [CASPT 2018](https://caspt.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Papers/CASPT_2018_paper_93.pdf)
- Fleet arithmetic: cycle time c = n·h; buying slack with fleet "does increase operational cost"; using slack en route for holding means "running time variability decreases at the expense of higher typical running times". — [Sánchez-Martínez et al., Research in Transportation Economics 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0739885915301049). Counter-evidence [SIM]: with fleet size co-optimised, headway-based holding lowers total passenger+operator cost 7.7–13.5% versus schedule-based holding. — [Liang, Ma & He, J. Adv. Transportation 2019](https://doi.org/10.1155/2019/2452348). Daganzo's adaptive scheme "requires less slack than the conventional, schedule-based approach". — [Daganzo, TR Part B 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transb/v43y2009i10p913-921.html)
- What buying reliability with buses costs (UK example): a Slough service specification estimated 5 extra peak buses (4 off-peak) at "£610,000 per annum", or alternatively cutting 16 minutes from the cycle to hold a regular 10-minute headway. — [Slough Borough Council bus service specification](https://slough.gov.uk/downloads/file/1300/bus-service-specification)
- **Cases where holding was dropped or failed to deliver:** LA Metro's early-2000s Metro Rapid headway-based effort "was later discontinued"; Metro's Joseph Forgiarini: "operators really did not have anything on board the bus to help them directly know how they were positioned." — [LAist, July 2023](https://laist.com/news/transportation/can-buses-run-more-efficiently-without-a-schedule-metro-wants-to-test-it-out). Cap Metro's 10-day Route 801 terminal-dispatch pilot (Sept 2018): "despite the in-person dispatching effort, headways did not improve throughout the rest of the route. Variability due to congestion and other on-route factors negated much of the dispatching effort." — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf); spacing "broke down after about three or four miles" and adding enough slack "would waste time and money". — [Swiftly 2019, VENDOR](https://goswift.ly/blog/mind-the-gap-best-practices-in-headway-management). VIA: in-person enforcement "untenable in the long-term". — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- **Adoption is rare:** TCRP Synthesis 155 (2021) survey — "The nine transit agencies that reported having fully or partially implemented HBS reported a total of 15 routes with HBS." — [TCRP Synthesis 155, ch. 3](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/26163/chapter/5)
- **WMATA data failure interrupted its KPI:** after introducing headway-based OTP for routes 70, 79, X2, 90, 92, 16Y and Metroway in July 2018, "Metrobus OTP for FY2019 is unavailable" because of "errors in timepoints and older, defective software that were installed on the on-board equipment of approximately 10 percent of the Metrobus fleet". — [NVTC 2019 Report on WMATA](https://novatransit.org/uploads/studiesarchive/2019%20WMATA%20Full%20Report.pdf)
- **Contract-side cost (Singapore BSRF, model for Baku's gross-cost regime):** operators earned S$3.4 million in incentives with no penalties for Dec 2015–Aug 2016 as excess wait time fell ~20% on 45 services; after rollout to 108 services from Sept 2016, EWT fell 15%. — [Singapore MOT written reply](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/written-reply-by-minister-for-transport-khaw-boon-wan-to-parliamentary-question-on-bus-regularity-following-implementation-of-bus-service-reliability-framework/); [Torque](https://www.torque.com.sg/news/sbs-transit-smrt-earn-3-4-million-incentives-improving-bus-reliability/); design evaluated in [Leong, Goh, Hess & Murphy, RETREC 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/106500/)
- **Transantiago regularity penalties:** operators accumulated fines of ~CLP 155 billion between March 2012 and March 2013 — [CNN Chile](https://www.cnnchile.com/pais/multas-a-operadoras-del-transantiago-superan-los-155-mil-millones-reclamos-de-los-usuarios-aumentaron/); the 2012 renegotiation "led to minor frequency gains but worsened regularity" and the 2013 one "reduced fines with no change in frequency compliance and lower regularity" — [Misguided quality incentives: the case of the Santiago bus system](https://researchers.uss.cl/es/publications/misguided-quality-incentives-the-case-of-the-santiago-bus-system-2); [The impact of compliance measures on the operation of a bus system](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/4471)

### Inferences
- For Baku's fleet- and driver-short system, the binding constraint is not software but slack: holding consumes recovery time that does not exist, so either headways lengthen (capacity loss on crowded corridors) or overtime rises. Under gross-cost contracts the authority ultimately pays either way.
- The organisational costs (dedicated dispatchers per corridor, terminal space, relief relocation, roster changes agreed with unions at both the state operator and private carriers) are the items vendors' business cases omit; the SSE case studies suggest these, not licences, are why agencies stop.
- Terminal-only control is cheaper but, per Cap Metro's pilot, its effect decays within a few kilometres in heavy traffic; Baku's long, congested trunk routes would need mid-route control points, which carry the passenger and driver-acceptance costs above.

### Gaps
- No agency publishes the full operating cost of a headway-management programme (staff, overtime, lost capacity) against measured benefits; the WMATA US$1 million/year benefit figure has no cost side.
- The TCRP Synthesis 155 survey denominator and the agencies' reasons for not adopting were not retrievable (OpenBook pages are image-only).

## Key Question 4 — Limits by frequency: where headway control pays off and what to do on 15–20 minute routes

### Takeaway
Guidance and empirical arrival-behaviour studies converge on a threshold of roughly 10 minutes (range 5–12 min across studies) below which riders arrive randomly and headway regularity matters; above ~12 minutes riders time their arrival to the timetable, so holding to even headways can *add* waiting time and the right tools are realistic schedules, schedule-based holding with capped hold times, and accurate real-time information (which cuts perceived waits by roughly 13–30% in field studies).

### Cited Findings
- "Headway-based service is best suited for high-frequency bus routes, defined as those with a headway of 10 minutes or less." — [TCRP Synthesis 155 (2021), ch. 1](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/26163/chapter/3)
- Headway-based holding "is most suitable for routes operating with short, uniform headways"; schedule-based holding "is considered suitable on routes that have long headways"; from Los Angeles field data the random-arrival assumption "for headways of 12 min or less should be modified to 10 min or less". — [Abkowitz & Engelstein, TRR 961, 1984](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/trr/1984/961/961-001.pdf)
- NYCT's Customer Journey Time Performance "assumes that passengers arrive randomly when headways are under 11 minutes and arrive based on schedules when headways are longer". — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- Berrebi's method "disregards the schedule entirely" because "passengers tend to arrive randomly at stops when buses come every 12 minutes or less". — [Mass Transit 2017](https://www.masstransitmag.com/technology/article/12358944/from-research-to-practice-implementing-real-time-control-to-avoid-bus-bunching)
- Arrival-behaviour thresholds "vary from study to study and ranges from 5 to 12 minutes (Luethi et al., 2007)"; Stuttgart found schedule-dependent arrival above 8 minutes, London above 12. — [Edinburgh Napier repository paper](https://napier-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/170379). Trondheim field counts (1,145 passengers, 24 stops): "The arrival distribution is far from uniform at 10-, 15-, and 20-minute headways" and most passengers plan arrival on the departure time. — [NTNU thesis](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2405551). Timetable-dependent passengers "reduce their mean scheduled waiting time by more than 3 min compared with random incidence". — [Frumin & Zhao, 2012 (MIT DSpace)](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/120845?show=full)
- Simulation on a Malaysian route [SIM]: "if the headway is less than 10 min (high-frequency), maintaining headway takes priority over strict adherence to the schedule"; headway-based terminal dispatch cut "big gaps" 90% and average waiting 51% versus 33% for schedule-based dispatch. — [Moosavi, Ismail & Yuen, PLoS ONE 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0232799&type=printable)
- Stockholm's regularity-driven scheme applies to trunk lines with "an average planned headway of 4–6 min during most of the day (7:00–19:00)". — [Cats, Transport Policy 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0967070X14001917)
- Practitioner view: holding is pointless at a 5-minute frequency and acceptable only on "a timetabled frequency of 20 minutes or more". — [Norman Baker, Passenger Transport 2026](https://www.passengertransport.co.uk/?p=21707)
- Schedule design for schedule-based control: van Oort found "using as low as the 35th percentile of the historical total trip time value minimized passenger wait time and travel time"; "The optimal value for routes with control/holding points was between the 30th and 50th percentile". — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf). With a 60-second maximum holding time, schedule- and headway-based holding performed the same in The Hague simulations. — [Van Oort, TRR 2010](https://nielsvanoort.weblog.tudelft.nl/files/2014/01/8-TRR-2010-Van-Oort-Reliability-improvement-by-holding.pdf)
- Real-time information as the lever for lower-frequency routes: OneBusAway users (Seattle, 2011) reported waits of 7.5 vs 9.9 min; modelled perceived wait fell 0.7 min (~13%) and actual waits were "almost 2min less". — [Watkins et al., 2011 (EconBiz record)](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/where-is-my-bus-impact-of-mobile-real-time-information-on-the-perceived-and-actual-wait-time-of-transit-riders/10009292600); [ITS JPO summary](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2011-b00737). Tampa controlled experiment (2014): benefits "pertain to waiting at the bus stop"; "no significant differences" in trip frequency or transfers. — [Brakewood, Barbeau & Watkins](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/5432)
- Schedule rewrites as the alternative: WMATA adjusted 34 routes and systemwide OTP rose 67.0% → 73.2% [VENDOR] — [Swiftly](https://goswift.ly/blog/wmata-improves-on-time-performance-by-6-2-systemwide-with-swiftlys-run-times); Valley Metro removed 20% of run-time slack on one route "saving one bus from service" [VENDOR] — [Swiftly case study](https://www.goswift.ly/case-study/phoenix-valley-metro); JTA route/timepoint changes lifted on-time arrival ~14% (2015) [PRESS] — [Jacksonville Daily Record](https://www.jaxdailyrecord.com/news/2015/aug/07/route-changes-increased-jtas-time-performance-14-percent); Canberra new timetable 73% → 79% on time [PRESS] — [CityNews](https://citynews.com.au/?p=110222); Donostia lengthened running times/headways and cut delayed trips 59–72% — [El Diario Vasco 2024](https://www.diariovasco.com/san-sebastian/dbus-buenos-cambios-seis-lineas-20240428065709-nt.html)
- Bunching definitions differ by agency (matters for any KPI the vendor proposes): MBTA bunched = <25% of scheduled headway, gapped = >175% — [MBTA deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent); Cap Metro on-time = 50–150% of scheduled headway — [Swiftly](https://goswift.ly/blog/mind-the-gap-best-practices-in-headway-management); TriMet research used an "arbitrary value of two minutes" — [Feng & Figliozzi](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1328&context=cengin_fac); a Toronto critique shows a 6-minute service alternating 3 and 9 minute gaps can still "meet the standard". — [Steve Munro, 2021](https://stevemunro.ca/2021/05/13/headway-quality-measurement-a-proposal/)

### Inferences
- In Baku, headway control should be scoped to corridors with ≤10-minute scheduled headways for most of the day; on 12–20 minute routes an "anti-bunching" hold will often delay riders who timed their arrival, so the product should default to schedule-based control (capped holds ~60 s, realistic 35th–50th percentile running times) and invest in prediction accuracy instead.
- Because many Baku routes may run 10–15 min at peak and sparser off-peak, the system must switch control modes by time of day — exactly the transition King County and VIA found hard operationally.
- Vendor KPIs defined on wide tolerance bands (50–150%) will show "improvement" that riders do not perceive; the advisor should insist on excess-wait-time measured at stops.

### Gaps
- No field study was found that directly measured net passenger-time change from holding on 12–20 minute routes; the threshold evidence is from arrival-behaviour studies and simulation.
- The TCQSM's exact frequency definitions were not retrieved.

## Key Question 5 — Data/technical failure modes: GPS dropouts, stale AVL, stop matching, terminal detection, latency, wrong holds, prediction under disruption

### Takeaway
Every field implementation reported technology failures as a first-order problem — lost GPS feeds that stopped the Georgia Tech trial after 77 minutes, radio failures that made Stockholm drivers distrust the system, defective on-board software on ~10% of WMATA's fleet that voided a year of reliability reporting, "ghost buses" at MBTA, and controllers in London watching positions "two bus stops behind" reality; the UK RTIG catalogue lists more than 30 distinct causes of phantom or missing vehicles, most of them logon, data and latency problems rather than hardware. No agency publishes a wrong-hold rate.

### Cited Findings
- "The DynamicTime software was able to function when Automatic Vehicle Location (AVL) data were lagged or had low polling frequency, but not when it lost AVL feed for extended periods of time"; the Georgia Tech route trial lasted only "77 minutes... due to defective GPS units"; "Losing GPS signal for several minutes at a time... caused substantial problems"; "the physical design of control points and the presence of adjacent traffic signal affected instruction adherence". — [CASPT 2018](https://caspt.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Papers/CASPT_2018_paper_93.pdf); [Mass Transit 2017](https://www.masstransitmag.com/technology/article/12358944/from-research-to-practice-implementing-real-time-control-to-avoid-bus-bunching). Adherence in these pilots was "affected by AVL data lag time, loss of data connection, judgment calls by operators, and the design of control points". — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- Stockholm RETT3 (2012): "radio communication problems", "several system functionality problems and persistent timetable constraints"; recurrent technical problems made it "very difficult for drivers to follow and trust the system". — [TRID record](https://trid.trb.org/View/1375940)
- WMATA FY2019: reliability KPI unavailable for a full year because of timepoint errors and "older, defective software... on the on-board equipment of approximately 10 percent of the Metrobus fleet" that "resulted in incorrect reporting of departure and arrival times". — [NVTC 2019](https://novatransit.org/uploads/studiesarchive/2019%20WMATA%20Full%20Report.pdf)
- MBTA (Dec 2024): "Tracking issues and 'ghost buses' exist"; dispatch tools and operator MDTs "do not show headways"; the OTP metric is "distorted/untrusted"; "Technology is 'not a silver bullet' and 'takes time to improve and implement'". — [MBTA deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent)
- UK RTIG "Ghost Buses" guidance (Dec 2022) lists causes including: bus "cancelled physically but not electronically"; "faulty hardware leading to no data updates, no GPS, stuck GPS, or no electronic ticket machine feed"; "radio / GPRS black hole gaps in coverage lead to jumps in real time passenger information"; "more than one ticket machine is signed in with the same Journey details"; "poor quality logons - the driver signs on to the ticket machine too early or late or when too far away from the start point of the journey"; off-route diversions removing predictions; "latency", "incorrect coordinates for a stop", "slow traffic" freezing or reversing predictions; the prediction system "cannot represent: 'bus bunching' effectively; service-run overlaps; and buses running the same service overtaking one-another"; third parties (e.g., Google) showing stale data; "rejected data - speeding buses, times running backwards". — [RTIG T054 v1.0, Ghost Buses](https://www.rtig.org.uk/system/files/documents/RTIGT054-1.0%20Ghost%20Buses.pdf)
- GPS in dense cores: location accuracy is "generally better than 10 m, but tall buildings cause multipath errors"; buses near downtown Chicago "appear to jump into Lake Michigan"; systems fall back on odometer dead reckoning. — [TCRP AVL synthesis (NAP 13907), ch. 4](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/13907/chapter/4). Older NextBus feeds polled every 120 s versus ≤30 s on newer systems; WMATA attributed vanishing buses to "lost GPS signals, logged-off buses, traffic, detours, or breakdowns" and said detours, traffic and weather "will always affect predictions". — [Greater Greater Washington](https://ggwash.org/view/29225/fewer-ghost-buses-haunt-nextbus)
- TMB Barcelona's SAE receives positions "aproximadament cada 30 segons" and predicts from rolling historical stop-to-stop times; forecast quality is checked only every 15 minutes and analysed monthly. — [Sindicatura de Greuges 2024](https://www.sindicaturabarcelona.cat/wp-content/uploads/202405_Dret-mobilitat_linia-V23-dautobus-incompleix-sistematicament-la-frequencia-de-pas.pdf)
- Human-reported display errors: London controller — "Sometimes it is out of date. I could be looking at you moving but you are two bus stops behind" — [London Assembly 2017](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/18186/download); London drivers — meter says "ten minutes behind the bus in front but turn a corner to find they are right behind it" — [Socialist Party 2016](https://www.socialistparty.org.uk/articles/22290/24-02-2016/stress-abuse-and-long-hours-for-london-bus-drivers/); Dublin drivers call PressIt boxes "inaccurate" — [Irish Independent 2024](https://www.independent.ie/regionals/dublin/dublin-news/dublin-bus-drivers-in-dispute-over-claims-of-distracting-calls-to-maintain-route-times/a1620070543.html)
- Demand data missing in the CTA "AI" pilots: "Real-time passenger demand is unavailable, so loads and boardings were estimated from historical averages"; the reinforcement-learning model's risks were "missing or noisy state data and unseen scenarios", mitigated by capping holding and lateness and falling back to even-headway rules; compliance was measured by "a dwell time deviation heuristic" and modelled in training as "a uniform random process"; electric buses sometimes needed charging at the terminal, delaying trip starts. — [arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)
- Where bunching originates (TriMet Route 15, 115 days of AVL/APC) [FIELD]: "irregular departure headway at the initial stop is the key cause of bus bunching rather than downstream traffic conditions and passenger demand uncertainty"; ~15% of westbound AM bunching trips formed at the first high-frequency stop; a 0–1 minute departure headway gave a ~70% probability of downstream bunching versus <10% for 4–5 minutes; "Time point bus stops can help reduce bus headway variability but with limited capability." — [Feng & Figliozzi, PDXScholar](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1328&context=cengin_fac)
- Crowding predictions degrade exactly where they matter: "Historical data tend to underestimate the load on crowded runs, but overestimate the load on the least crowded runs"; real-time AVL "reduces the variance of the prediction but does not improve the bias". — [KTH Stockholm crowding-prediction paper](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1786851/FULLTEXT01.pdf)

### Inferences
- Baku's known GPS gaps map directly onto the failure list above: a bus that drops off the feed looks like a gap, so the controller/algorithm may hold the following bus for a phantom gap or dispatch a reserve into a corridor that is actually fine. Berrebi's experience suggests latency and low polling are tolerable but multi-minute dropouts are not — the product needs a dropout-aware "do nothing" rule.
- With mixed state and private operators, logon discipline (correct trip/block sign-on) is the most likely data failure; RTIG lists it as a leading cause of phantom vehicles. Stop-matching and terminal-departure detection depend on accurate stop coordinates and dwell detection, both weak where buses lay over off-stop.
- Any crowd forecast built on tap-in totals plus historical averages will be biased low on the most crowded trips, i.e., it will under-trigger reserve dispatch on exactly the trips the product is sold to fix.

### Gaps
- No agency or vendor publishes a false-hold or wrong-instruction rate; the only proxies are compliance heuristics (CTA) and "one-third not implemented as intended" (MBTA).
- No quantitative study of prediction-model accuracy specifically under disruptions (detours, incidents) was found beyond qualitative statements from WMATA and RTIG.

## Key Question 6 — Reserve / "gap bus" dispatching in practice: who does it, cost, utilisation, evidence of benefit

### Takeaway
Standby-bus insertion looks best in simulations (minimal rider disruption) but in practice is done ad hoc by a few agencies (King County Metro, CUMTD, TMB for schedule-based lines) and avoided by many because reserve vehicles and drivers are paid whether used or not — "we do not have a reserve fleet of buses and drivers waiting for backup needs" (Sound Transit); the US evidence on reserve drivers finds them less efficient than regular or overtime drivers, and no published utilisation or benefit–cost figures for gap-bus programmes were found at MTA, TfL, KMB or Singapore.

### Cited Findings
- Simulation evidence [SIM]: "Petit and Ouyang found that standby bus insertion outperformed schedule-based recovery strategies... minimal impact to passengers - no extra dwell time or transferring. However, this strategy can generate unbudgeted labor and fleet costs... operators may only be available on overtime, and the peak fleet may already be on the road." — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- Practice: the approach "is currently implemented by some transit agencies in an ad-hoc manner", e.g., CUMTD (Champaign-Urbana) "dispatches a reserve bus when a running bus falls significantly behind schedule or bunching is forming"; the downside is "the extra resources (vehicles and drivers) needed in the standby fleet". — [Petit, Ouyang & Lei, TR Part B 115 (2018)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:transb:v:115:y:2018:i:c:p:1-16); [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10079018)
- Santiago model [SIM]: "reserving a bus to be injected can be better than operating the entire fleet continuously" for an unscheduled (headway-based) service. — [Morales, Muñoz & Gazmuri, 2020](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/7898)
- King County Metro: "Standby buses are strategically placed in the case of incidents or to inject into service gaps"; deadheading to an injection point and bypassing stops were the most common mid-route strategies among the eight case agencies. — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- Barcelona TMB: for the ~40 lines run to timetable, "es pot mobilitzar un vehicle de reserva, o bé d'una altra línia"; frequency-based lines are regulated at terminals or with express runs instead. — [GenTMB](https://gentmb.tmb.cat/seccio/mon-bus/com-funciona-regulacio-dels-intervals-entre-cotxes)
- Sound Transit: "for cost-saving measures, we do not have a reserve fleet of buses and drivers waiting for backup needs." — [Sound Transit FAQ](https://www.soundtransit.org/q-when-train-service-goes-down-why-aren-t-back-buses-available-immediately)
- NYC MTA bus (2022): a manager's email said depots would not staff extra drivers to cover absences; the MTA spokesman said the agency "is trying to make sure it isn't paying drivers when they are not needed" — [Planetizen, Sept 2022](https://planetizen.com/news/2022/09/118574-new-york-mta-cuts-bus-service); express trips cancelled for "a shortage of drivers" — [Patch](https://patch.com/new-york/new-york-city/mta-blames-canceled-express-bus-trips-driver-shortages)
- Reserve-driver economics (Metro Transit, Minneapolis, 2016): "Reserve drivers were found to be less efficient than overtime or regular drivers"; "About 25 percent of bus drivers across transit agencies have no regular job assignments"; Metro Transit used reserve drivers for substitute work about 80% of the time; "If you make them sit idle, you're still paying their salary." — [Minnesota Daily, 3 May 2016](https://mndaily.com/237198/uncategorized/u-researchers-design-scheduling-algorithm)
- Extraboard sizing: the average US operator misses about 12% of scheduled workdays excluding vacation/holidays; oversizing is costly, undersizing causes missed trips — [MTI via Mass Transit](https://www.masstransitmag.com/bus/press-release/11600520/mineta-transportation-institute-mti-ca-improved-model-helps-transit-agencies-predict-more-accurate-staffing-for-bus-routes); an MBTA teaching case modelled a part-time extraboard at 40% of the full-time workforce — [MIT OCW](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-259j-transit-management-fall-2006/8b4edc999da3a077f4bed688aa26deae_lect1920.pdf); FTA guidance caps spare vehicles at 20% of peak fleet for fleets of 50+ — [TCRP spare-ratio synthesis](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/22409/chapter/3)
- Rail analogue (NYC subway): the MTA's own explainer describes closing gaps by holding the train ahead so "the gap is split", not by inserting standby trains — [DNAinfo on MTA video, 2015](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20150609/midtown/mta-releases-retro-animation-video-explain-subway-delays); 24.0% of subway headways failed the wait standard Jun 2016–May 2017 — [NYC IBO, Oct 2017](https://ibo.nyc.ny.us/iboreports/we-are-being-held-momentarily-how-much-time-and-money-are-new-york-city-subway-riders-losing-to-delays-october-2017.html)
- Dubai RTA (June 2026) claims AI dashboards enable "deploying buses immediately during metro disruptions" — an agency press claim with no figures. — [RTA press release](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/news-and-media/all-news/NewsDetails/ai-contributes-68percent-to-reducing-early-bus-departures)

### Inferences
- A reserve pool is a fixed cost paid on every day it is not needed; with Baku's driver shortage, every standby driver is a scheduled trip not run, so "gap buses" compete directly with base service. The Minnesota finding that idle reserves are less efficient than overtime suggests paying overtime for planned extras beats a standing pool.
- The agencies that do use standby buses (King County, CUMTD) do so for incidents and severe gaps, not routine bunching; the simulation benefit comes from assuming the bus and driver already exist and are free.
- Under gross-cost contracts the authority would have to buy standby capacity explicitly from private carriers; without a contract line item, carriers have no incentive to hold buses back.

### Gaps
- No documentation of an NYC MTA "gap bus" programme, TfL spare-bus ratios, KMB standby fleets or Singapore LTA standby buses was found; no utilisation or benefit–cost figures for any gap-bus programme were located.

## Key Question 7 — Crowd/demand forecasting at stop level: typical accuracy, data needs, failures with aggregate-only data

### Takeaway
The best published stop-level crowding predictors reach ~75–80% accuracy on three crowding classes and mean absolute errors of ~8–11 passengers only when real-time APC counts exist on the bus in question; APC is typically fitted to a minority of vehicles (20% in Stockholm) and systematically undercounts on crowded trips, and tap-in-only smart-card data (Baku's case) require destination inference whose exact-stop accuracy is around 66% (94% within one stop) and which fails when riders' last trips do not return home.

### Cited Findings
- Stockholm line 4 (2016 data, afternoon peak) [FIELD data, offline evaluation]: APC available on only "about 20% of all buses" (1,200 of 5,861 runs); "15 minutes before departure the average mean absolute error (MAE) is around 11 passengers with real time APC data; 5 minutes before departure the average MAE has dropped to ca 8 passengers. Meanwhile, crowding level accuracy increases from ca. 75% to more than 80%"; historical patterns alone "cannot explain well why some buses are highly crowded". — [KTH/DiVA paper](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1786851/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- APC counts "look reliable at low demand but systematically undercount passengers on crowded trips", with MAE/RMSE rising by load bucket; the authors recommend cross-verifying with AVL/smart-card boardings. — [arXiv 2605.00083 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00083)
- Nashville (WeGo) models: the ground truth is itself noisy APC data; "the major challenges in developing prediction models are the sparsity of data and the presence of noise in the data"; authors suggest publishing a crowdedness factor rather than absolute loads. — [Talusan et al., arXiv 2210.04989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04989v1); [Zulqarnain et al., NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10466155-addressing-apc-data-sparsity-predicting-occupancy-delay-transit-buses-multitask-learning-approach)
- Transferability: models "can provide reliable estimates of future data when trained with historical information if demand patterns are fairly stable". — [Wood, Yu & Gayah, IJTST 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijtst.2022.03.005)
- Low-data city example (Varanasi) [conference paper, low quality]: stop-level occupancy classification accuracy 0.54–0.66 with random forest/XGBoost. — [Urban Mobility India paper](https://www.urbanmobilityindia.in/Upload/Conference/077b46f4-00eb-4cb0-bde7-93888ec3da6f.pdf)
- Florida DOT review: "it remains a challenge to accurately estimate transit vehicle occupancy, especially in a real-time and cost-effective manner"; Wi-Fi probing hit a best R² of 0.84 but MAC randomisation breaks unique-device counting. — [FDOT BED30-977-03](https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/research/reports/fdot-bed30-977-03-rpt.pdf?sfvrsn=7dd7ed98_1)
- Tap-in-only destination inference: Shenzhen (smart card + GPS + cellular) exact-stop accuracy 65.82%, 93.67% within one stop — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10195948/1/Alighting%20location%20estimation%20from%20public%20transit%20data%20%20a%20case%20study%20of%20Shenzhen.pdf); South-East Queensland validation: the "last destination assumption shows a significant negative impact on the matching results", mean error cut from 806 m to 530 m with fixes — [Alsger et al., 2016](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/5965); Gatineau algorithm 79% within 400 m — [BAnQ record](https://collections.banq.qc.ca/document/s/t/o5s4FjLyfUHE0E6kBzbt0g)
- NYCT builds its journey-time KPI from "fare swipes on buses and off-board fare kiosks to specific bus stops, and infers destinations by riders' next boarding swipe", weighted by prior-month ridership — i.e., a month-lagged OD model, not real-time demand. — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- Even the CTA RL pilots had no real-time demand: loads "were estimated from historical averages". — [arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)

### Inferences
- Baku's 89% cashless tap-in gives boardings per stop only if each tap can be matched to a stop via GPS; where GPS is missing, the tap cannot be placed, so demand data vanish on exactly the buses that are already invisible to control. Alightings (and hence loads) must be inferred, with errors of several hundred metres or one stop.
- Without APC on most of the fleet, a "crowd forecast" is a historical-average product dressed as AI; the Stockholm result (bias not fixed by real-time AVL) implies it will under-predict the worst crowding and over-predict empty buses.
- Forecast skill collapses when demand patterns shift (network redesigns, new contracts, metro disruptions) — the moments a reserve-dispatch product is meant to help.

### Gaps
- No agency-published accuracy figures for deployed crowding predictions (e.g., Transit app, Google "crowdedness") were found; evidence is academic and offline.
- No study was found on tap-in-only inference accuracy for a system with many private operators and partial GPS coverage.

## Key Question 8 — AI-specific risks: over-promising, opacity, vendor lock-in, cancelled pilots, procurement/ethics (driver surveillance)

### Takeaway
The transit "AI" track record is thin on independent evaluation and thick on vendor and agency press claims (e.g., Dubai's ">68%" early-departure reduction with no baseline); the documented harms are driver surveillance disputes (Rochester 2024–26, Utah 2014, London 2009), proprietary CAD/AVL data lock-in (Fresno, San Francisco, World Bank guidance), and a string of on-demand/"smart" dispatch pilots shut down for cost per ride of US$17–124; headway-management pilots themselves have a history of quiet discontinuation (LA Metro Rapid) and undisclosed results (LA Line 16, WMATA Route 79 at the time of reporting).

### Cited Findings
- **Driver surveillance via AI telematics (Rochester, NY, Transdev/Lytx) [PRESS, FOIA-based, June 2026]:** ATU Local 1005 says the system "is invasive and incapable of perceiving the nuances of the decision-making required while operating city buses", alleges discipline and firings "without sufficient human oversight or transparency", struggles to obtain footage ("employees often see footage for the first time in a meeting"); a city director's email conceded "it does feel intrusive"; contracts with Transdev and Lytx were not obtainable under FOIA; a six-month trial now replaces "immediate AI-based suspensions with progressive discipline". — [Workday Magazine](https://workdaymagazine.org/foia-documents-show-intrusive-ai-system-is-monitoring-rochester-bus-drivers/)
- Earlier telematics disputes: Metroline Perivale (2009) in-cab device monitoring "up to 120 driving manoeuvres" with a traffic-light display — "the lights are very distracting and are just adding to drivers' stress", fear of use for discipline [ACTIVIST] — [Socialist Worker](https://socialistworker.co.uk/news/metroline-bus-drivers-anger-at-electronic-monitoring/); Utah Transit Authority installed cameras in 2014 despite a union grievance claiming a CBA violation — [KUER](https://www.kuer.org/business-labor/2014-05-16/uta-installs-cameras-on-buses-despite-union-objections); a Twin Cities ATU 1005 contract bars cameras that "directly monitor the bus driver" — [Workday Magazine](https://workdaymagazine.org/foia-documents-show-intrusive-ai-system-is-monitoring-rochester-bus-drivers/)
- **Contract incentives vs safety (London):** drivers' testimony that QIC bonuses drive "push, push, push" and that iBus headway management forces late finishes — [London Assembly transcript 2017](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/18186/download); the Assembly's related 2017 report (as summarised in search results) found TfL contracts "incentivise operators to meet punctuality, but not safety, targets" — [London Assembly report PDF (likely "Driven to Distraction")](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/40982/download)
- **Unverified agency AI claims:** Dubai RTA press release (8 June 2026) claims AI dashboards (built in-house on Dataiku) cut early departures "more than 68%" and saved ">13,000 tonnes" of CO₂, with no baseline period, sample, definition, method or independent evaluation stated. — [RTA](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/news-and-media/all-news/NewsDetails/ai-contributes-68percent-to-reducing-early-bus-departures)
- **Industry guidance silent on risks:** APTA's AI guidance suite (15 May 2026), based on a survey of 32 member agencies, presents only success examples and does not name opacity, bias, lock-in or workforce surveillance as risks; the only caution is a call for "the right governance structures". — [METRO Magazine](https://www.metro-magazine.com/news/apta-launches-ai-guidance-suite-for-public-transit)
- **Vendor-reported results (all [VENDOR]):** Swiftly claims WMATA OTP 67.0% → 73.2% after re-timing 34 routes — [Swiftly](https://goswift.ly/blog/wmata-improves-on-time-performance-by-6-2-systemwide-with-swiftlys-run-times); Oulu ">10%" OTP gains and two buses saved — [Swiftly](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/oulu-public-transport-authority-eliminated-the-need-for-two-buses-with-swiftly); Optibus/JTA one route 83% → 94–98% — [METRO Magazine](https://www.metro-magazine.com/news/jta-sees-on-time-performance-improvement-with-optibus-snapper-integration); Swiftly's own Cap Metro account concedes "dispatch alone" cannot eliminate bunching — [Swiftly](https://goswift.ly/blog/mind-the-gap-best-practices-in-headway-management). NYCT's 8-week Optibus "pick" pilot (2023) has no published outcome. — [Optibus blog](https://blog.optibus.com/new-york-city-transit-to-pilot-optibus-as-part-of-the-transit-tech-lab)
- **Vendor lock-in:** "AVL hardware providers often impose proprietary constraints on the data produced by their systems. They allow access to the processed data only through the interfaces the vendor provides" — [World Bank Connections note](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/pt/979511471004982975/pdf/107701-BRI-PUBLIC-conn-27-MARK-VC-KNOWLEDGENOTES.pdf); Fresno FAX: "the technology that FAX utilizes is proprietary and only accessible to its original vendor, Trapeze" — [City of Fresno legislation](https://agendas.fresno.gov/en/legislation/id19-11218-10280); SFMTA on Conduent: "any attempt to either maintain software or develop new features and enhancements will require services from Conduent", and self-maintenance would invalidate the warranty — [SFMTA board document](https://sfmta.com/media/40156/download); Riverside Transit's Trapeze CAD/AVL maintenance renewal ~US$4.56 million over five years — [RTA presentation, Dec 2023](https://www.riderta.com/sites/default/files/events/2023-12-05CAD-AVLMaintenanceSupportPresentation.pdf); TRB: lock-in "often arises where an incumbent vendor has significant control over the agency's ability to procure new systems" — [TRB TCIP note](https://trb.org/main/blurbs/180131.aspx)
- **Cancelled or failed "smart dispatch" pilots (2019–2025):** Gold Coast Transit (CA) ended its Go NOW microtransit pilot at US$124 per trip versus US$8 fixed-route — [GCTD board slides, Sept 2025](https://www.gctd.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Item-12-BOD-Slides-for-Go-NOW-2.pdf); Sacramento SmaRT Ride discontinued at "over $47" per passenger, ~5× fixed route — [CBS Sacramento](https://www.cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/sacramento-microtransit-smart-ride-ending); Park City ended microtransit over "unreliability, high cost and unsatisfactory service" — [Park Record](https://www.parkrecord.com/news/microtransit-services-discontinued-within-the-park-city-limits/); St. Pete Beach ended on-demand service at ~US$17 per trip — [St Pete Catalyst](https://stpetecatalyst.com/st-pete-beach-is-ending-its-on-demand-ride-service/); LA Metro Micro cost US$62.97 per trip in early 2023, later ~US$29 — [Streetsblog CA, June 2024](https://cal.streetsblog.org/2024/06/24/latest-on-metro-micro-still-few-riders-high-costs); Singapore cancelled planned autonomous-bus deployments on services 191 and 400 — [Land Transport Guru](https://landtransportguru.net/?p=155121)
- **Headway pilots with undisclosed or negative outcomes:** LA Metro Rapid early-2000s effort "later discontinued" — [LAist 2023](https://laist.com/news/transportation/can-buses-run-more-efficiently-without-a-schedule-metro-wants-to-test-it-out); LA Line 16 (July 2023, ~17,500 riders/day, 6-min headway, operator tablets) — contractor TMD reviewed 12 months of "end-of-line departure adherence, headway regularity, operator performance, and technical challenges" but findings were not found published — [TMD](https://tmdinc.net/la-metro-headway-based-operations-pilot/); WMATA "still in the process of analyzing the results" a year after launch — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- **Opacity/fragility of the RL approach itself:** CTA's RL model was trained with driver compliance "modeled as a uniform random process", needed hard "guardrails" capping holding and lateness, fell back to simple even-headway rules on bad data, and the authors call for retraining on real-world data. — [arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231v1)
- **Academic over-promise warnings:** hybrid holding methods "have outperformed standalone holding" but "need real-world testing" — [Rezazada et al. 2024](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/10327); "operators know their routes better than any algorithm or anyone else in the agency" (Berrebi, as summarised) — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- The procurement risk for Baku is a bundle: proprietary data access, model opacity, and KPIs defined by the vendor. The advisor's checklist should require open data export (GTFS-RT/SIRI), vendor-independent measurement of excess wait time, and a documented "manual override/driver judgment" policy.
- Telematics that feed a headway product can also score drivers; given the Rochester and Utah disputes, any Baku deployment across a state operator and private carriers needs explicit data-use limits agreed with drivers before launch, or it risks the Dublin outcome (drivers switching radios off).
- Agency-announced percentage improvements (Dubai 68%, vendor OTP gains) should be treated as marketing until a baseline, sample and method are published; none of the cited AI claims met that bar.

### Gaps
- No example was found of a specifically headway-control AI product being formally cancelled after procurement in 2020–2026; the discontinuations found are either older (LA Metro Rapid) or adjacent (microtransit, AV buses). The Kansas City Bridj case (US$1.3 million for 1,480 rides, 2016–17) appeared in a search summary but its primary source URL was not verified.
- Contract values for Rochester's Lytx deal and most CAD/AVL contracts were not public.

## Key Question 9 — Do simple non-AI fixes (bus lanes, all-door boarding, terminal dispatch discipline, schedule rewrites) outperform headway control per dollar?

### Takeaway
The only field experiment that ran holding alongside street and boarding measures (Stockholm 2014) attributed almost all of the 2-minute-per-passenger saving to bus lanes, stop removal and boarding changes and found holding had no significant travel-time effect; TriMet data show bunching is mostly born at the terminal (so dispatch discipline beats mid-route AI), all-door boarding cut Muni dwell time 38%, the 14th Street busway raised speeds up to 24% and ridership up to 30%, and realistic schedule rewrites repeatedly deliver 5–15 point OTP gains — none of which require driver compliance with real-time instructions.

### Cited Findings
- **Stockholm line 4 package (2014) [FIELD]:** average speed +6–9%; cycle time 8–10 minutes shorter, "could potentially help the operator to cut the fleet size by two buses, from 27 to 25"; headway CoV -28%; waiting time -35%; "Out of almost six minutes of observed travel time savings observed, one minute is attributed to the changes in boarding regime, whereas physical street measures and removed stops account for a reduction of almost five minutes. Headway based holding did not have a significant effect on average travel time"; per-passenger saving 2 min (10%), worth 9 million SEK (US$1.1 million) a year for weekday PM peaks alone, achieved "with inexpensive measures". — [West & Cats 2015](http://resolver.tudelft.nl/uuid:5454500a-1e84-4a3c-9759-e18d725da02e); [CASPT 2015](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/paper11.pdf)
- **Terminal discipline first:** "irregular departure headway at the initial stop is the key cause of bus bunching rather than downstream traffic conditions and passenger demand uncertainty... A bus departure headway control at the initial stop of high frequency service zone is highly recommended"; once bunched, pairs "are more likely to continue until the end of high frequency service zone". — [Feng & Figliozzi, TriMet Route 15](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1328&context=cengin_fac). Where many riders board at the origin, "the best strategy is to ensure that vehicles depart on time from the route origin rather than detain them en route". — [Abkowitz & Engelstein 1984](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/trr/1984/961/961-001.pdf). "Terminal performance is key – it is very difficult to catch up if a trip starts late." — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf). MBTA: buses on the same route leave terminals less than 1 minute apart "130-190 times" on a typical day. — [MBTA deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eSthnyd8bemsaI2ZWk3CorfMBJA9Rob8/htmlpresent)
- **All-door boarding (SFMTA, 2012–14) [FIELD]:** average dwell per customer 3.93 → 2.45 s (-38%), standard deviation 3.6 → 2.1 s, rear-door boardings 29% → 54%; systemwide speed only 8.41 → 8.56 mph, and SFMTA states that "To reduce travel times substantially, other strategies such exclusive transit lanes, transit signal priority and parking management must accompany All-Door Boarding." — [SFMTA All-Door Boarding Evaluation, Dec 2014](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/agendaitems/2014/12-2-14%20Item%2014%20All%20Door%20Boarding%20Report.pdf)
- **Busway (NYC 14th Street, from Oct 2019) [FIELD]:** NYC DOT (2026): "bus speeds increased by as much as 24 percent and ridership grew as much as 30 percent" — [NYC DOT press release](https://home4.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2026/kick-off-transformation-manhattan-14-street.shtml); early data: M14 ridership +19% weekdays (+25% AM peak), speeds +55% on the car-free segment, attributed to removing cars — [Streetsblog USA, Jan 2020](https://usa.streetsblog.org/2020/01/20/buses-work-best-on-car-free-streets-ny-mta-data-shows); City Hall: travel times improved 22–47% — [NYC Mayor's Office, Dec 2019](https://www.nyc.gov/office-of-the-mayor/news/625-19/mayor-de-blasio-releases-first-14th-street-busway-report)
- **Frequent-service branding and priority without headway management:** King County Metro's six RapidRide routes "provide 70 percent more rides per weekday than their replacement routes, and operate as much as 20 percent faster during peak hours" — "even without adopting headway management". — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)
- **Transit signal priority [FIELD]:** Virginia field/simulation work found maximum savings of 3–6% and that green-extension TSP "could increase transit travel times by about 2.5% during congested morning peaks" — [VTRC 06-CR6](https://vtrc.virginia.gov/reports/all-reports/06-cr6/); other field evaluations in the ITS JPO database report 4–15% savings (Chicago Western Avenue X49, Los Angeles) with travel-time variance roughly halved in Chicago (entries indexed at [ITS KRS 2007-b00345](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2007-b00345) and [ITS KRS 2012-b00814](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2012-b00814); exact entry mapping not verified). Cap Metro used its headway data chiefly to win "more aggressive TSP at certain intersections" [VENDOR]. — [Swiftly](https://goswift.ly/blog/mind-the-gap-best-practices-in-headway-management)
- **Schedule rewrites [mostly VENDOR/PRESS]:** WMATA 34 routes, OTP 67.0% → 73.2% — [Swiftly](https://goswift.ly/blog/wmata-improves-on-time-performance-by-6-2-systemwide-with-swiftlys-run-times); Valley Metro ~10% OTP gain and one bus saved by removing 20% slack — [Swiftly](https://www.goswift.ly/case-study/phoenix-valley-metro); JTA +14% (2015) — [Jacksonville Daily Record](https://www.jaxdailyrecord.com/news/2015/aug/07/route-changes-increased-jtas-time-performance-14-percent); Canberra 73% → 79% — [CityNews](https://citynews.com.au/?p=110222); Donostia delayed trips -59%/-72% after running-time increases with no new investment — [El Diario Vasco 2024](https://www.diariovasco.com/san-sebastian/dbus-buenos-cambios-seis-lineas-20240428065709-nt.html); TriMet's original AVL/CAD deployment improved run times only ~3% but final-stop on-time performance 9.4% — [ITS JPO](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2000-b00151)
- **Contract design as the lever (relevant to gross-cost contracts):** Singapore's EWT-based incentive framework cut excess wait 20% (45 services) and 15% (108 services) — [MOT Singapore](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/written-reply-by-minister-for-transport-khaw-boon-wan-to-parliamentary-question-on-bus-regularity-following-implementation-of-bus-service-reliability-framework/); London's QICs are what drivers say actually changes behaviour ("QICs are our bonus") — [London Assembly 2017](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/18186/download); in Santiago stricter regularity targets are estimated to have improved regularity by 16% (difference-in-differences on GPS data) — [Navarro, job-market paper](https://mnavarrosudy.github.io/files/research/navarro_jmp.pdf)
- **Unit-cost data points for simple fixes:** SEPTA Market Street bus-lane red paint ~US$785,000 (length not stated), epoxy "will last up to a decade" — [Philadelphia Inquirer, Mar 2023](https://www.inquirer.com/transportation/septa-bus-lanes-red-traffic-20230315.html); SFMTA says red paint improves lane compliance by about 50% — [SFMTA blog](https://www.sfmta.com/index%2Ephp/blog/everything-you-need-know-about-red-transit-lanes); agencies "have largely moved away from red paint" over durability — [NCHRP/TCRP red-lane synthesis 2026](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/29451/chapter/8); a full 2.2-mile SF transit-priority corridor was budgeted at US$67.1 million (bundled) — [SFMTA](https://sfmta.com/media/40156/download)
- The TransitCenter/SSE toolkit ranks reliability measures as: robust scheduling data, headway management or drop-back dispatching, appropriate metrics, and "capital investments such as bus lanes and transit signal priority"; "Headway management at the terminal and/or by operators during service cannot alone ensure reliability." — [SSE/TransitCenter 2019](https://transitcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/SSE_bus_reliability_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- The evidence ordering for Baku is: (1) fix terminal departures and layover (zero capital, but needs supervisors and schedule slack); (2) rewrite running times from GPS data (software-cheap, no driver instructions); (3) all-door boarding — already enabled by 89% cashless; (4) bus lanes/enforcement on the trunk corridors; and only then (5) real-time headway control on ≤10-minute corridors. Steps 1–4 produce the fleet savings (Stockholm: 2 of 27 buses) that a driver-short system needs; step 5 consumes slack.
- Per dollar, no study directly compares headway control with street measures, but the Stockholm decomposition (≈5 of 6 minutes from street/stops, ~0 from holding) and Cap Metro's conclusion that control "alone" fails are the closest field evidence, and both point the same way.
- A gross-cost contract with an excess-wait-time incentive (Singapore/London model) buys regularity from operators without the authority owning the control technology; the risk, documented in London, is that incentives translate into pressure on drivers unless schedules and breaks are protected.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed benefit–cost comparison of headway control versus bus lanes/boarding/schedule measures was found; per-mile bus-lane costs were not retrievable in comparable form.
- Red-paint and enforcement costs for a Baku-scale network would need local quotes; US figures are context-specific.
