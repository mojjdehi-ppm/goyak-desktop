# Baku buses: on-board technology, how dispatching actually works, and buses-per-route distribution

Research date: 2 Nov 2026. Sources are 2023–2026 unless marked **[older]**. Azerbaijani terms are kept next to English. "Confirmed" = stated in a cited source; "Inference" = my reading of the evidence.

Key actors: **AYNA** (Azərbaycan Yerüstü Nəqliyyat Agentliyi, Azerbaijan Land Transport Agency, under the Ministry of Digital Development and Transport; took over Baku's bus regulation from the former **BNA**, Bakı Nəqliyyat Agentliyi / Baku Transport Agency); **BakuBus MMC** (state operator, part of AZCON Holding since Nov 2024); **K Group MMC** (operator of the BakıKart fare system, local partner of Turkish vendor **Kentkart**); private carriers (Xaliq Faiqoğlu MMC, Azlənd Plaza MMC, Menloqq MMC, Transkontrol MMC, etc.).

## Q1. What does the on-board driver console / validator in Baku buses do today, and who supplies it?

### Takeaway
The validator and the driver-facing screen are one Kentkart-family system (Turkish vendor Kentkart, installed and operated locally by K Group MMC, the BakıKart operator). The unit is not just a fare reader: by design it carries GPS/GPRS, shows the driver "trip runtime information" for timekeeping, plays next-stop audio, feeds the LED/LCD passenger displays and can exchange messages with a dispatch centre. From 1 Jan 2026 every bus on a Baku regular route must by regulation carry GPS, an e-payment device, an on-board unit that streams movement/fare data to a "central dispatcher system", and an automatic passenger counter, all integrated with the "AYNA" information system in real time. No public source describes the exact Baku driver UI (the "+1:54 / 5 min" fields seen in the team's photo).

### Inferences
- The driver screen in the team's route-172 photo is almost certainly a Kentkart "Kent Tablet"/OBU-class device run by K Group (BakuBus has been on Kentkart OBUs since 2015; route 172 passed to BakuBus in 2023 per BakuBus's own 2023 review, see Q3). The "+1:54" with a green tag and the "5 min"/clock are consistent with Kentkart's advertised "trip runtime information … facilitates timekeeping for the drivers" — i.e., a schedule-adherence (early/late vs. the trip's timing points) figure and a time-to-next-timing-point/stop figure. This is an inference; no Baku-specific UI documentation was found.
- Because the OBU already streams GPS, speed, door/stop events and (soon) passenger counts to K Group's/AYNA's back end, the data needed for a headway-control tool exists centrally; what is missing publicly is any sign that the back end computes **headway** (inter-bus spacing) rather than **schedule deviation**, or that it pushes hold/speed-up advice to drivers.
- The Dec 2022 outage (buses could not enter service after a validator software push) suggests the validator/OBU is operationally mandatory for a bus to run, i.e., a natural "distribution channel" for any new driver-facing feature — but also a single vendor gateway (K Group) that a third-party tool would have to go through.

### Gaps
- No tender, manual, press tour or vendor case study describing the fields on the Baku driver screen (schedule deviation format, headway display, text messaging) was found. Searches in Azerbaijani, Russian and Turkish returned nothing Baku-specific.
- Could not confirm whether private carriers' buses use the same Kentkart OBU model as BakuBus or a cheaper validator-only configuration (the 2016 Xaliq Faiqoğlu rollout mentions validators plus a company monitoring centre, not OBUs).
- The UNESCAP deck lists an "NEC Project – public transport management" with "messages sent to the driver (informational and as a warning)" but gives no date, status or vendor detail beyond NEC — [UNESCAP PDF](https://www.unescap.org/sites/default/d8files/event-documents/Azerbaijan_2.pdf).

## Q2. How do AYNA, BakuBus and private carriers dispatch buses and correct intervals in practice? Does anyone send real-time instructions to drivers?

### Takeaway
Three layers exist: (1) AYNA's Monitoring Centre (formerly BNA's "Vahid Dispetçer Mərkəzi/Xidməti") watches GPS positions and intervals for all BakıKart-connected routes but acts on carriers ("daşıyıcıya tapşırıq/xəbərdarlıq"), not on drivers; (2) BakuBus's own "Onlayn Monitorinq Mərkəzi" plus route-level dispatchers and "team leaders" who regulate departures at terminals and talk to drivers; (3) private carriers' in-house dispatchers at terminals. All published interval control is schedule-based (planned interval vs. observed) and reactive (add buses, warn carriers, hold departures at the terminus). No source describes mid-route holding, speed guidance or short-turning instructions to drivers, and AYNA itself said in Sept 2025 it has "no mechanism to punish carriers" for quality until the gross-cost ("brutto") contracts arrive.

### Inferences
- Interval correction in Baku today is (a) planning-level (more buses, re-routing, bus lanes), (b) terminal-level (dispatchers hold/release departures at the terminus by phone/radio or in person), and (c) after-the-fact (AYNA warns carriers). There is no public evidence of en-route headway management (holding at mid-route stops, speed advice, short-turns) delivered to drivers through the OBU or otherwise.
- The regulatory KPI language is "interval" (hərəkət intervalı), not a stop-by-stop timetable, on most urban routes (AYNA's own route passports quote 6–8 min intervals). This makes a headway-based tool conceptually compatible with how AYNA already measures service — but the measurement is still of deviation from a planned interval, triggered by complaints or peak-hour snapshots.
- BakuBus is the only operator with a demonstrated closed loop (monitoring centre → dispatcher → terminal departure regulation). Private carriers appear to rely on a terminal dispatcher and AYNA's external warnings.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 "Vahid Dispetçer Xidməti" bulletins were found; the published peak-hour bulletins located are BNA-era (Dec 2022–Jan 2023). Whether AYNA still publishes them after absorbing BNA could not be verified.
- The functional scope of the 2020 ARIS LLC software (AZN 3.498m, "planning and managing route lines") is not described anywhere public; no evidence it includes real-time dispatch.
- No job description, training material or press tour of AYNA's Baku Monitoring Centre (staffing, tools, decision rules) was found; AYNA did not answer one media query on intervals (Oct 2026).
- No driver interviews describing what instructions they receive en route.

## Q3. BakuBus specifically: its control centre, vendors, modernisation and share of the fleet

### Takeaway
BakuBus runs ~1,337 buses on 83 routes (2025 figures, including Ganja and intercity lines) — roughly half of Baku's ~2,530 route buses. Its on-board stack is Kentkart/K Group (since 2015), and it operates an "Onlayn Monitorinq Mərkəzi"/"dispetçer mərkəzi" with route-level team leaders; no third-party AVL/ITS vendor (INIT, Trapeze, etc.) is named in any source.

### Cited Findings
- AZCON Holding portfolio page: BakuBus "current fleet 1,337 buses", "83 route lines in total (including Ganja city and intercity routes)", "557 thousand+ average daily passengers", figures for 2025 — [AZCON](https://azcon.gov.az/az/portfolio/bakubus-4).
- Baku total: 2,530 buses on route lines from 15 Sep 2026 (vs 1,780 in Sept 2022; 2,011 in 2023; 2,180 in 2024; 2,340 in 2025) — [Banker.az, 14 Sep 2026](https://banker.az/bakida-marsrut-avtobuslarinin-sayi-rekord-h%C9%99dd%C9%99-catdirilir-2-530-avtobus/).

## Q6. Legal/regulatory: must buses follow an approved timetable, and would deliberately holding a bus conflict with rules or contract KPIs? Penalties for "qrafikdən kənara çıxma"?

### Takeaway
Yes — every regular route has an approved **marşrut pasportu** (route passport) with a **hərəkət cədvəli** (timetable) that lists, per trip, the times of passing route points; carriers are contractually bound not to change the timetable/scheme and may revise it only with AYNA's consent, and repeated/gross schedule violations are grounds for unilateral contract termination. The Administrative Offences Code (Art. 322.0.1) fines "non-compliance with the established movement schedule and movement scheme" of regular-route buses (sanction set at 50 AZN by the Aug 2023 amendment). However, AYNA's own definition of urban service quality is the **interval** (passports quote "6–7 dəqiqə interval"), the new gross-cost contracts make "bus count and interval" the KPIs with penalty deductions, and operators already hold buses at termini under dispatcher regulation — so holding to even out spacing is legally sensitive only insofar as it is done outside the operator's dispatch authority or produces measurable "early/late vs. passport" deviations. AYNA stated in Sept 2025 it has no effective sanctioning mechanism for quality until brutto contracts spread.

### Cited Findings
**Primary law and rules**
- Law "Avtomobil nəqliyyatı haqqında" (On Motor Transport) defines regular passenger carriage as carriage on a set timetable (interval) and route; the executive body sets the city route network — [AZSTAND copy of the Law](https://azstand.gov.az/upload/files/556-IIIQ%20Avtomobil%20n%C9%99qliyyat%C4%B1%20haqq%C4%B1nda.pdf) (consolidated text date not verified).
- Rules on passenger and baggage carriage (Cabinet of Ministers; AYNA-hosted consolidated text): 4.3 — after a joint road-safety check, "the route passport is prepared and approved" by the relevant bodies (Baku: "müvafiq qurumlar"); 4.4 — "the average speed on each section is calculated, stop durations and trip time are developed ... movement timetables (hərəkət cədvəlləri) and route schemes are drawn up"; 4.7.5 — the passport states "the movement interval by season, day of week and time of day"; 4.8 — a trip-by-trip timetable is attached to the passport; 4.9.5 — the timetable shows "the time of the vehicle passing route points on each trip"; 4.9.6 — "time and place of inter-trip standing and of drivers' breaks"; 4.10 — timetables "are regularly monitored and regulated by the relevant bodies ... coordinated with other modes and overlapping routes" — [AYNA PDF of the Rules](https://ayna.gov.az/uploads/documents/vQ3Nfs2PELQhuRuUQLwPDMS7RNOXCpah39NP9iDH.pdf); also hosted at [Mincom](https://mincom.gov.az/storage/pages/650/303332ddb68db94267ac05a89108c60e.pdf).
- Tender rules and model contract (Cabinet Decision No. 52 of 3 Apr 2009, Annex 7 model contract): carrier must perform carriage "observing the work regime approved by the Customer" (2.2); "not change the approved movement scheme, work regime, movement timetable of buses ... not reduce the envisaged number of vehicles" (2.6); "fulfil the requirements of the Customer's authorised bodies performing organisation and control of carriers' activity" (2.10); "make a record of arrival and departure at the start, end and intermediate points envisaged in the timetable" (2.11); Customer may "control the Carrier's work and take measures" (5.1) and unilaterally terminate for "significant or repeated" breaches (5.3); clause 7: for "repeated or gross violation ... of the movement scheme, timetable, work regime" the contract may be terminated unilaterally; clause 13: the carrier may "temporarily revise bus timetables and the number of trips" when passenger flow changes sharply "with the Customer's consent"; 1.6: termination with ≥15 days' notice; contracts run 3–5 years — [Mincom PDF of Decision 52](https://mincom.gov.az/storage/pages/653/43ae63e8bf49c3dccdbeeabbbdcc4282.pdf); [e-qanun 16555](https://e-qanun.az/framework/16555). AYNA's 2026 tenders are still run under this decision — [AYNA, 3 Mar 2026](https://www.ayna.gov.az/az/news/muxtelif-istiqametlerde-muntezem-avtobus-marsrutlari-musabiqeye-cixarilir-1033).
- Tender tables themselves fix, per lot, the "required number of buses", reserve buses and operating hours (e.g., route 49: 30 buses + 2 reserve, 05:15–23:45) — [AYNA announcement 66](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-66); a footnote notes some routes "will operate on the basis of the schedules stated in the route passport" — [AYNA announcement 55](https://ayna.gov.az/az/media/announcements/musabiqe-elani-55).

**Administrative penalties**
- Code of Administrative Offences Art. 322.0.1: "non-compliance with the established movement timetable and movement scheme of buses operating on regular intra-city (intra-district), intercity (inter-district) and international routes" — text quoted in [an older consolidated PDF of the Code](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Azerbaijan/Azerbaijan-Code-on-Admin.-Offenses-native.pdf) (which shows fines of 200–500 AZN individuals / 800–1,200 officials / 4,000–6,000 legal entities) **[older version]**; a Milli Məclis draft shows 200 / 1,000 / 3,000 — [meclis.gov.az draft](https://www.meclis.gov.az/news-layihe.php?id=2103&lang=az&par=0); the law of 18 Aug 2023 amending Art. 322 replaced the sanction wording "forty to fifty manat" with "fifty manat" and added 322.0.11–322.0.14 (e.g., charter carriers running on regular-route timetables/stops) — [President.az, 18 Aug 2023](https://president.az/az/articles/view/60868). AYNA (Mar 2025) confirms the 50-AZN fine, in force from 1 Jan 2024, for charter carriers using regular-route timetables/stops — [AYNA, 6 Mar 2025](https://ayna.gov.az/az/news/sifarisli-sernisin-dasinmasi-ucun-formulyarlarin-istifadesi-ile-bagli-maariflendirici-tedbirler-kecirilir-695). Sources conflict on the current amount for 322.0.1; see Gaps.
- Organising regular passenger transport without a contract: 300 AZN (officials) / 1,200 AZN (legal entities) — [President.az amendment law](https://president.az/az/articles/view/60868) (via search summary).

**Enforcement reality and the shift to gross-cost KPIs**
- AYNA chairman (Sep 2025): "AYNA has no mechanism to discipline carriers for quality"; brutto contracts will bring "daily control" and "fines or termination" — [Report.az, 22 Sep 2025](https://report.az/infrastruktur/anar-rzayev-ayna-nin-elinde-dasiyicilari-tenbeh-elemek-mexanizmi-yoxdur).
- Presidential Decree No. 497 of 19 Sep 2025 "On some measures to improve regular passenger transport by motor transport" is the legal basis for the new vehicle requirements (clause 4.3) and the subsidy rules (clause 3.2.2) — [Cabinet Decision 250 text, AZERTAC PDF](https://azertag.az/store/manual/2026_07_31_6a6ca8f24476a.pdf); [FED.az](https://fed.az/az/neqliyyat/bakida-avtomobillerin-odenisli-hereket-zonalari-yaradilacaq-240817).
- Gross-cost subsidy rules (Cabinet Decision No. 250, 31 Jul 2026): payment to the carrier = monthly service value "minus the total amount of **dəbbə pulları** (contractual penalties) applied under the contract" (3.3); fares are paid into AYNA's account and AYNA pays carriers per km (3.4); service value is recalculated annually or on fuel-price shocks (3.8) — [AZERTAC PDF](https://azertag.az/store/manual/2026_07_31_6a6ca8f24476a.pdf). AYNA: "number of buses per route, movement interval, distance and other indicators are set in advance ... compliance with the schedule (qrafikə riayət) will be ensured" even in low-demand evening hours; "specially created software" will measure quality; first routes 130, 205, 211, 500, 503 — [AYNA](https://www.ayna.gov.az/az/news/brutto-muqavileye-kecidle-ictimai-neqliyyat-sahesinde-yeni-dovr-baslayir-1207); subsidies apply to CNG/electric buses in Baku etc. and are "not connected with fare regulation" — [Elchi.az, 5 Aug 2026](https://en.elchi.az/2026/08/important-change-regarding-buses/).
- Operators already hold buses at termini under dispatcher control and describe it as regulation "according to the set movement schedule" (BakuBus route 10, Sep 2026) — [Bakues.az](https://bakues.az/bakubus-10-nomreli-avtobusla-bagli-naraziliga-aydinliq-getirdi-video-yenilenib/).
- Passenger/regulator sensitivity to waiting: AYNA warned carriers whose buses "change their movement scheme at peak hours" (routes 202/45) — [Icma.az, 26 Nov 2025](https://icma.az/news/ayna-bezi-avtobuslarin-dasiyicilarina-xeberdarliq-edecek-1255230); passengers complain of buses "not moving until full" at termini — [Baku.ws, 12 Jun 2025](https://baku.ws/social/bakubus-dan-istifade-edenlerin-nezerine-avtobus-20-25-deqiqeden-gec-gelerse).

### Inferences
- Legal risk of a holding-based anti-bunching tool is low if (a) holds are issued or endorsed by the operator's dispatcher (the contract gives the carrier and its dispatch the right to regulate departures and, with AYNA consent, to revise timetables), and (b) holds are short and at termini or designated stops (the passport already provides for "inter-trip standing" at termini; mid-route dwell is not explicitly addressed). The risk rises if holds make buses measurably "late vs. passport times" at AYNA-monitored control points (4.9.5 timing points, Art. 322.0.1), or if gross-cost KPIs are later written as timetable adherence rather than interval regularity.
- Conversely, the brutto model's stated KPIs ("avtobus sayı, hərəkət intervalı") and AYNA's practice of describing service by interval make an interval-regularity metric the natural compliance measure — a headway tool could be positioned as a compliance aid rather than a conflict with the rules.
- Practical obstacle is institutional rather than legal: AYNA acts on carriers, not drivers; the tool would need to live inside an operator's dispatch (BakuBus) or inside K Group's OBU platform to reach drivers.

### Gaps
- The exact current fine under Art. 322.0.1 could not be confirmed from e-qanun (sources range from 50 AZN flat after the 2023 amendment to legacy tiered fines); the e-qanun pages were not retrievable (404/503) during research.
- The brutto model contract and its actual penalty schedule (what deviation triggers what deduction) are not public; AYNA statements only say penalties and "special software" exist.
- Whether AYNA's route passports for Baku urban routes include trip-by-trip timing points (4.9.5) or only intervals in practice could not be verified; media quotes always cite intervals.
