# AI/Adaptive Traffic Signal Control and Smart Pedestrian Crossings: State of the Art and Real-Deployment Results (2020–2026)

Scope note: Vendor-sourced figures are flagged **[VENDOR]**. Figures from city agencies/press releases without independent verification are flagged **[CITY]**. Peer-reviewed or independently evaluated figures are flagged **[INDEP]**. The hackathon idea under evaluation: a camera/AI pedestrian-only signal that (a) calls pedestrian green sooner when more people wait, (b) holds vehicle green when nobody waits, (c) gives immediate pedestrian green when no cars are present (late night), and (d) extends crossing time for elderly/disabled.

---

## Key Question 1: Existing pedestrian-adaptive crossings — what do they detect, what did they measure?

### Takeaway
Every individual feature in the hackathon idea already exists somewhere in production: demand-cancel and slow-walker extension (UK Puffin, since 1990s), camera intent detection with group-size-based green extension and mobility-aid recognition (Vienna/TU Graz, 21 sites since 2018, 2nd-gen 2024), continuous pedestrian green until a vehicle approaches (TfL Green Person Authority, 2021–, ~36+ sites), volumetric pedestrian counting that lengthens green with crowd size (TfL Pedestrian SCOOT), and card-tap extensions for elderly (Singapore Green Man+, 1,000+ crossings; Hong Kong 21 sites). The novel combination is *packaging all of them as a low-cost retrofit for pedestrian-only mid-block signals*; the measured benefits of the existing systems are modest but real (TfL: +13% pedestrian compliance, 1.3 pedestrian-hours saved/day/site; Shenzhen: −22% pedestrian wait, −71% wasted vehicle green).

### Cited Findings

**UK Puffin crossings (kerbside + on-crossing detectors)**
- Puffin crossings use kerbside presence detectors (pressure mat or pole-mounted above-ground) to cancel a pedestrian call if the waiting area is empty for a preset period after the button press, and on-crossing detectors (responding to pedestrians walking ≥0.5 m/s) to extend the all-red clearance period; a fault-monitor algorithm extends clearance to a preset maximum if the detector produces no output between cycles — [DfT Traffic Advisory Leaflet 1/02](https://tsrgd.co.uk/pdf/tal/2002/tal-1-02.pdf)
- Puffins remove the flashing amber/flashing green man period and use nearside pedestrian signals; the intent is to reduce delays to vehicles via call-cancel and improve security for older/disabled pedestrians via extension — [TfL Puffin behaviour report](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/puffin-behaviour-report.pdf)
- TRL accident study of 50 upgraded sites (1994–2007): conversion to Puffin reduced all accidents by 19%; at junctions, 26% for all accidents and 38% for pedestrian accidents (not statistically significant due to sample size); "a large number of sites where the cancel facility was not working although the kerbside detection appeared functional" — [TRL, Maxwell et al., The safety of Puffin crossings](https://trid.trb.org/view.aspx?id=924158) [INDEP]
- TfL London observational study: all-red extension activated in 20%–91% of pedestrian phases depending on site; call-cancel was used only once in 500 crossing events; 4 of 5 Puffin crossings had detector faults; on-crossing detectors failed for 1–3% of crossing events — [TfL Research Summary No. 8](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/research-summary-no-8-puffin-crossings.pdf) [INDEP]
- Same study: up to 49% of pedestrians crossed without pressing the button; longer waits (UTC-linked sites) led to significantly more red-man crossing; "insufficient evidence ... to determine whether Puffin crossings were safer than Pelican crossings or whether they perform better in terms of reduced delays" — [TfL Puffin behaviour report](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/puffin-behaviour-report.pdf) [INDEP]

**TfL Green Person Authority (GPA) — pedestrian green by default until a vehicle is detected**
- Installed at 18 crossings in May 2021; the signal shows continuous pedestrian green until a vehicle is detected approaching, then switches to red for pedestrians to let the vehicle through — [TfL press release, Feb 2022](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/media/press-releases/2022/february/new-tfl-data-shows-success-of-innovative-pedestrian-priority-traffic-signals) [CITY]
- Results: average site showed pedestrian green for an extra 56 min/day; total time saved by all pedestrians at the average crossing = 1.3 hours/day; pedestrian compliance with signals increased 13%; driver compliance unchanged; largest bus journey-time increase 9 s, general traffic 11 s; several sites improved vehicle journey times — [TfL press release, Feb 2022](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/media/press-releases/2022/february/new-tfl-data-shows-success-of-innovative-pedestrian-priority-traffic-signals) [CITY]
- As of Oct 2023: 12 of the original 18 sites still operating GPA (others being reconfigured "to enhance the vehicle detection system"), 9 further sites added, 15 more planned; sites selected for high pedestrian / low traffic flows — [London Assembly MQT 2023/3657](https://www.london.gov.uk/who-we-are/what-london-assembly-does/questions-mayor/find-an-answer/green-person-authority-pedestrian-crossings-trial-0) [CITY]

**TfL Pedestrian SCOOT (volumetric pedestrian counting)**
- Pedestrian SCOOT, trialled from 2014 at Balham and Tooting Bec, uses video to count waiting pedestrians and extends the green-man invitation when many are waiting; TfL also developed "call cancel" to drop the pedestrian stage if the button-presser crosses early or walks away — [TfL press release, Mar 2014](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/media/press-releases/2014/march/tfl-to-launch-worldleading-trials-of-intelligent-pedestrian-technology-to-make-crossing-the-road-easier-and-safer)
- TRL modified the SCOOT kernel so each pedestrian stage is optimised individually on measured demand (previously demand level was set by timetable at 5-minute intervals); claimed benefits include a reducible minimum invitation period and lower cycle time; SCOOT network-wide in London delivers an average 12.5% delay reduction and 4.6% fewer stops — [TRL Pedestrian SCOOT project page](https://www.trl.co.uk/projects/pedestrian-scoot-system) [VENDOR/INDEP mix]
- 2020s Lane Rental trial at Highbury Corner with a FLIR pedestrian quantity detector: detector density estimate within 10% of ground truth >96% of the time over 24 h (TOPAS compliant); monitoring confirmed it altered pedestrian green times based on demand; TfL concluded it will be written into contracts for deployment "as and when other suitable locations are identified" — [TfL Lane Rental publication](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-ped-scoot-trial-lane-rental-industry-publication.pdf) [CITY]
- TfL Pedestrian Countdown (PCaTS) trial: 54% of pedestrians crossed within 5 s of arrival, 70% within 15 s, ~85% within 30 s regardless of signal shown; "first person wait times" at trial sites were 40–72 s; countdown cut the share "feeling rushed" (Balham 45% → 7%) — [TfL PCaTS trial results](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/PCaTS-Note-3-PCaTS-Trial-Results-Report.pdf) [INDEP]

**Vienna / TU Graz camera-based intent-detecting pedestrian signals (closest analogue to the hackathon idea)**
- Published method: "fully automated, vision-based traffic light for pedestrians" using short- and long-term trajectory cues to predict which pedestrians intend to cross; long-term evaluation in a European capital showed it can replace push buttons — [Ertler et al., AVSS 2018](https://doi.org/10.1109/avss.2018.8639112) [INDEP]
- Detection zone 8×5 m vs 2×3 m for standard industrial detectors; intent estimated in 1 s, reliable after 2 s; registers demand 3–4 s before a person would press the button; green can be extended for large groups; demand cancelled if people leave the waiting area; only geometric information is used, images never leave the camera — [TU Graz news, May 2019](https://www.tugraz.at/en/news/article/denkende-fussgaengerampeln-neues-system-der-tu-graz-erkennt-kreuzungswunsch-automatisch0)
- Acceptance test criterion set by MA 33: at most 50 failures per 10,000 pedestrians; target cost ≤ €5,000 per signal, "the same as a push-button pedestrian signal" — [ORF Wien, Nov 2017](https://wien.orf.at/v2/news/stories/2879578/)
- "Teaching the system not to respond to pedestrians without the intention to cross ... proved to be the biggest challenge"; suitable sites are those with high irregular pedestrian volumes and heavy traffic (schools, museums) — [EU Urban Mobility Observatory, 2019](https://urban-mobility-observatory.transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/vienna-introduce-smart-traffic-lights-2019-06-27_en)
- Status Nov 2024: 21 intelligent lights in use in Vienna since 2018; second generation tested at 4 crossings; recognises wheelchairs, rollators, crutches, strollers and can extend green; compute rose from 0.5 TFLOPS (2018) to 100–300 TFLOPS; camera covers ~30 m² of waiting area; images processed locally and deleted within 50 ms; intent prediction 99% accurate, mobility-aid recognition ≥85%; fail-safe: on classification error green is still requested at standard duration; model trained on staged campus footage for data-protection reasons — [EurekAlert / TU Graz, Nov 2024](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1066452) [INDEP]
- Vienna's aim is to gradually replace ~200 push-button lights; "the system does not work at every intersection, some are simply too busy" — [BR24, Dec 2024](https://www.br.de/nachrichten/wissen/warten-ade-fussgaengerampeln-schalten-automatisch-auf-gruen,UWBJpFY)

**Shenzhen "full pedestrian+vehicle sensing" intersection (2024) — numbers directly comparable to the hackathon idea**
- Shenzhen traffic police's first intersection with both vehicle and pedestrian detectors (Shenwan 5th Rd / Baishi 4th Rd): pedestrian green length set by number of people waiting; vehicle green adjusted to pedestrian demand. Measured 9:00–17:00: average pedestrian wait fell from 65.7 s to 53.8 s (−22.1%); 10:00–11:00 average "wasted" vehicle green fell from 23.6 s to 6.9 s (−70.8%) — [CNR Shenzhen, Sept 2024](https://sz.cnr.cn/rdzt/xzsclsgcjt/zyjtkjcxxty/20240926/t20240926_526918285.shtml) [CITY]

**Hong Kong Transport Department**
- 2017–18 pilot: video pedestrian detection at 5 junctions (HK$900,000 total ≈ HK$180k/site) automatically calls pedestrian green when someone is in the waiting zone and cancels it if they leave; preliminary review "can effectively reduce the waiting time" — [HK LegCo reply, Jan 2018](https://www.tlb.gov.hk/eng/psp/pressreleases/transport/land/2018/20180124a.htm) [CITY]
- 2021 pilot completed at 5 isolated junctions; 2024 AI "real-time adaptive traffic signal system" at 8 linked junctions in Tung Chung distinguishes waiting pedestrians from passers-by; vehicle delay cut ~5–10% (2–4 s per 40 s wait), ~240 vehicle-hours/day saved across 200,000–220,000 daily vehicles; rollout to ~50 isolated junctions 2026–2027 — [HK TLB blog, Jul 2026](https://www.tlb.gov.hk/eng/blog/20260711.html) [CITY]
- Octopus-card "Smart Device" extends flashing-green for over-65s and disabled at 21 locations, placed at non-busy junctions "to avoid serious traffic congestion" — [HK TD](https://www.td.gov.hk/en/transport_in_hong_kong/pedestrians/pedestrian_crossing_facilities/smart_device/index.html)
- HK studied pedestrian countdown timers twice (2006, 2018) and found "no material improvement and benefit to pedestrian safety" because more people were still crossing when flashing green ended — [HK LegCo, May 2022](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202205/18/P2022051800246.htm) [INDEP]

**Singapore Green Man+ (card-tap extension)**
- Introduced 2009; gives seniors/disabled 3–13 s extra depending on crossing width via concession-card tap; >1,000 crossings equipped, +1,500 by Q4 2027 (total >2,500); highest utilisation near MRT stations; LTA needs per-site surveys to decide how long green can be extended — [Straits Times, Nov 2023](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/transport/seniors-to-get-longer-green-man-time-at-half-the-crossings-in-all-housing-estates-by-2027) [CITY]
- Singapore green-man timing: steady green 6–10 s, remainder computed from crossing distance at ~1.0 m/s — [Singapore Parliament](https://telescope.gov.sg/transcript/26174)
- Pilot study (198 crossings, 52 subjects): pedestrians aged ≥70 would struggle at up to 14.6% of crossings even starting on steady green, needing 6–8 s extra; starting at flashing onset, those ≥70 fail at >50% of crossings; most crossings need up to 10 s longer flashing time — [Kong et al., J Am Geriatr Soc 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jgs.12344) [INDEP]

**Netherlands (Tilburg / Rijswijk CrossWalk app, Dynniq)**
- Tilburg 2017: 10 trial users; app has four preset extension levels by mobility; sensor confirms user is waiting when button pressed; works via GPS and the signal controller software, no new hardware — [The Guardian, Jul 2017](https://www.theguardian.com/cities/2017/jul/12/dutch-app-elderly-hack-pedestrian-crossings)
- Giving all pedestrians longer green "was not an option" for Tilburg because it would obstruct car traffic toward the centre — hence individual targeting — [Mobiliteit.nl, May 2017](https://www.mobiliteit.nl/innovatie/2017/05/11/voetgangersapp-helpt-ouderen-veilig-steken/)
- Rijswijk Dec 2019 pilot at 2 junctions for school groups and community-centre visitors, "to be evaluated at a later moment" — [Omroep West](https://www.omroepwest.nl/nieuws/3971667/in-rijswijk-zet-je-met-een-app-het-verkeerslicht-langer-op-groen)

**Starling Crossing (London)**
- A 2017 temporary full-scale prototype in South London: LED road surface with neural-network cameras tracking pedestrians, cyclists, vehicles; crossing appears only when someone approaches at quiet times, widens for crowds; spun out as Starling Technologies in 2020 — [Umbrellium](https://umbrellium.co.uk/projects/starling-crossing/). No measured performance results published.

**South Korea (AI auto-extension, 2025)**
- Seoul Jongno-gu installed an AI "pedestrian time auto-extension system" at 2 crossings that detects vulnerable pedestrians and extends walk up to 6 s — [Jongno-gu press release](https://www.jongno.go.kr/mayor/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?bbsId=BBSMSTR_000000001618&menuId=388338&menuNo=388338&nttId=250420) [CITY]
- Seoul Jung-gu installed AI-camera auto-extension at 2 school crossings (Sept 2025) — [Asia Today](https://www.asiatoday.co.kr/kn/view.php?key=20250907010003691) [CITY]; commercial product (NeoTraffic) interfaces to the field controller board — [NeoTraffic](https://www.neotraffic.co.kr/products/ai-pedestrian-extension) [VENDOR]
- Research motivation: Korean elderly walk at 0.54 m/s vs the 1.0 m/s timing assumption — [crosswalk-cctv project](https://github.com/rashiedomar/crosswalk-cctv)

**Shenzhen solar smart crosswalk (unsignalised)**: AI pedestrian detection triggers flashing ground lights and warnings; no signal control — [Shenzhen Government, Nov 2021](https://www.sz.gov.cn/en_szgov/news/latest/content/post_9372138.html)

### Inferences
- The hackathon idea is not novel feature-by-feature; its defensible pitch is (1) a cheap retrofit for pedestrian-only signals that are currently fixed-time or push-button, (2) combining count-based call timing, demand cancel, night "instant green", and vulnerable-user extension in one edge device, and (3) local evidence generation (before/after wait times and compliance) — Vienna and TfL show cities accept the concept but roll it out slowly and site-selectively.
- The strongest citable comparables are Shenzhen 2024 (−22% ped wait, −71% wasted vehicle green) and TfL GPA (+13% compliance, 1.3 ped-hours/day saved, ≤11 s vehicle impact).
- Vienna's experience predicts the hardest engineering problem: rejecting passers-by (false calls), and the operational need for a fail-safe (if unsure, request green at standard duration).

### Gaps
- No quantified before/after wait-time or compliance numbers found for Vienna beyond "reduces waiting time considerably in some cases".
- No published evaluation found for the Tilburg/Rijswijk CrossWalk app or Starling Crossing.
- TfL's full GPA trial report (with per-site methodology) was not fetched; only press-release and Assembly-answer numbers are cited.

## Key Question 2: Adaptive signal systems and AI — what has been achieved in real cities?

### Takeaway
Classic adaptive systems (SCOOT/SCATS/ACS-Lite) deliver median travel-time gains of ~5–12% and stop reductions of ~15–28% in US field tests, with some deployments making things worse; the NCHRP meta-review of 85 studies puts average benefits at 7.8% (stops) with a 3.4% *increase* in side-street delay. Headline AI numbers (Surtrac −25% travel time / −40% wait; Google Green Light "up to 30% fewer stops"; NoTraffic −46%) are mostly vendor- or developer-measured against outdated baselines; the most credible independent AI figure is Boston/INRIX for Green Light: −13.5% delay, −20% stops on retimed intersections. Deep RL has essentially one small real-world deployment (Lemgo, ~10% travel-time cut, single intersection) and reviews through 2025 state no multi-agent RL has been deployed anywhere.

### Cited Findings

**Baselines for conventional adaptive control (SCOOT, SCATS, ACS-Lite etc.)**
- FHWA: "On average ASCT improves travel time by more than 10 percent. In areas with particularly outdated signal timing, improvements can be 50 percent or more." — [FHWA EDC-1 ASCT](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/innovation/everydaycounts/edc-1/asct.cfm)
- FHWA evaluation of field tests (median % improvement): SCOOT travel time 7% (range −15 to 29), stops 15%, delay 11%; SCATS travel time 12% (0–21), stops 25%, delay 17%; ACS Lite travel time 5%, stops 28%, delay 18%; "there were many instances when a technology made conditions worse or kept them the same" — [FHWA HRT-17-007](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/randt/evaluations/17007/17007.pdf) [INDEP]
- Literature review: SCOOT travel time −5% to −29%, delays −2% to −28%, stops −17% to −32%; SCATS travel time 0 to −20%, delays +3% to −19% — [ITS JPO deployment evaluation, Zhao & Tian 2014](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2014-b00921) [INDEP]
- NCHRP 20-07 Task 414 database of 85 evaluation studies: average benefits range from 7.8% (number of stops) to 85% (split failures); side-street delay increased 3.4%; transit travel time −2.8%; fuel −0.3% to −7%; emissions −0.1% to −9.8%; crashes −35.1%; "in some instances ATCSs failed to outperform previous type of control" — [NCHRP 20-07/414](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/nchrp/docs/NCHRP20-07_Task414FinalReport.pdf) [INDEP]
- Florida DOT 8-corridor before/after: average travel-time reduction 9.36%; major-street throughput +6.96%, major-street queues −15.57%, minor-street queues +16.98%; B/C 12.8 with safety, 5.4 without — [ITS JPO 2021-b01579](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2021-b01579) [INDEP]
- Siemens SCOOT Ann Arbor (StreetLight data): weekday travel time −12% (236→207 s), weekend −21% — [Siemens case study](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:4396089f-53e9-4c66-b968-c63a11f1b04c/ann-arbor-adaptive-traffic-control-case-study.pdf) [VENDOR]
- Monterey, CA SCOOT at 41 intersections (Jan 2023): travel time −16%, delay −30%, stops −40%, speed +27% — [Save California Streets](https://savecaliforniastreets.org/city-of-monterey-adaptive-traffic-control-system/) [CITY]
- Istanbul ATAK (ISBAK, in-house GA+fuzzy): isolated junction cycle time −10%, saturation −4%; 5-junction 2.6 km corridor travel time −15% (plate-matching before/after) — [Gündoğan et al., JTLE 2014](https://www.jtle.net/uploadfile/2014/0604/20140604034552236.pdf) [CITY/INDEP]; IMM reports delay −15–30%, travel time ~−20%, fuel ~−15%, CO₂ −18% — [MedUrbanTools](https://medurbantools.com/portfolio_page/traffic-management-istanbul-metropolitan-municipality/) [CITY]

**Surtrac (Pittsburgh, CMU → Rapid Flow Technologies)**
- 9-intersection East Liberty pilot, before (Mar 2012) vs after (Jun 2012) drive-through runs on 12 routes: travel time −25.8%, speed +34%, stops −31.3%, wait time −40.6%, emissions −21.5%; baseline was coordinated-actuated plans at peaks and actuated free mode off-peak; June volumes ~5% higher — [Smith et al., CMU-RI-TR-12-20 / TRB 2013](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/pub_files/2013/1/13-0315.pdf) [INDEP—developer-measured]
- Expanded to 50 intersections, with USDOT ATCMTD/PennDOT funding for 150 more; commercial deployments in Atlanta, Portland ME, Needham and Quincy MA — [USDOT UTC page](https://www.transportation.gov/utc/surtrac-people-upgrading-surtrac-pittsburgh-deployment-incorporate-pedestrian-friendly)
- Independent VISSIM simulation for Burlington VT found more modest gains: travel time −6–11%, delay −14–21%, stops −27–32%, emissions −10–14% — [CCRPC Adaptive Signal Study](https://studiesandreports.ccrpcvt.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/CCRPC-Adaptive-Signal-Study-Final-Report.pdf) [INDEP]

**Google Project Green Light**
- Mechanism: AI model on Google Maps driving trends infers cycle length/splits, recommends timing tweaks to city engineers who implement in ~5 minutes; "up to 30% reduction in stops and up to 10% reduction in emissions at intersections"; 70 intersections in 12 cities (Oct 2023), 18 cities on 4 continents (May 2025); footnote: emissions modelled with a single vehicle type, "not yet adjusted for local vehicle mix", averaged from coordinated intersections — [Google blog Oct 2023](https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/sustainability/google-ai-reduce-greenhouse-emissions-project-greenlight/), [Google blog May 2025](https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/sustainability/project-green-light-boston-expansion/) [VENDOR]
- Boston (INRIX-evaluated, 114 intersections): average delay −13.5% (up to −24%), unnecessary stops −20% average; best sites −21% delay/−33% stops (Atlantic Ave & Richmond St), −24%/−34% (Beacham St & Maffa Way); ~4,000 gallons fuel/year per recommendation; changes are typically a few seconds (e.g., +7 s and +3 s green) — [Boston.gov May 2025](https://www.boston.gov/news/mayor-wu-announces-expansion-project-green-light-signal-optimization-program), [Axios Boston](https://www.axios.com/local/boston/2025/07/15/project-greenlight-google-street-lights-traffic) [CITY/INDEP]
- Brazil (Sept 2025): stops −9%, fuel −15% at 83 intersections, ~100 t GHG/month; "data and claims have not been verified by an independent third-party" — [Google Sustainability](https://sustainability.google/stories/transforming-mobility-in-brazil-through-google-mobility-solutions/) [VENDOR]
- Buenos Aires: 14% fewer stops on one corridor, 2,339 h and 6,987 L fuel/year (city figures) — [Context/TRF May 2025](https://www.context.news/ai/ai-helps-tackle-traffic-carbon-emissions-in-buenos-aires) [CITY]
- Criticism: Henry Liu (U. Michigan): "It's all dependent on the baseline"; most Green Light test sites were not running adaptive control; sites were chosen to avoid bus/bike lanes and high-use pedestrian crossings; Seattle reverted one recommendation; Transport for Greater Manchester often ignored recommendations because signals are deliberately set for bus priority or to discourage residential through-traffic — [Scientific American Aug 2024](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/googles-project-green-light-uses-ai-to-take-on-city-traffic/) [INDEP]; only ~2% of US transport emissions come from idling/queuing — [heise Aug 2024](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Traffic-control-how-Google-wants-to-make-traffic-lights-smarter-with-AI-9838704.html)

**NoTraffic (camera/radar edge AI, NVIDIA-based)**
- Maricopa County AZ 1-week pilot at 1 intersection: vehicle delay 29.5→13.7 s (−46%), cross-traffic 26.8→12.4 s (−54%), pedestrian wait 42.2→33.1 s (−22%); results extrapolated to 170 intersections; ITS JPO notes "details on study methodology within the source document were limited" — [ITS JPO 2025-b02021](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2025-b02021) [VENDOR]
- Phoenix Glendale Ave: control delay −10%, pedestrian delay −17%, PM peak delay −29%, red-light running −70% — [NoTraffic](https://www.notraffic.com/resources/phoenix-az-a-leap-forward-in-traffic-safety/) [VENDOR]; Tucson 15 intersections: control delay −48.5%, pedestrian delay "up to 80%" — [NoTraffic](https://www.notraffic.com/resources/from-gridlock-to-green-lights-in-downtown-tucson/) [VENDOR]; 80+ intersections in Tucson, 200+ agencies in 35 states — [NVIDIA blog Jan 2025](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/notraffic-reduces-road-delays-carbon-emissions/) [VENDOR]

**Alibaba City Brain**
- Hangzhou Xiaoshan district 104 junctions: traffic speed +15% in first year (company figure); Hangzhou fell from 5th to 57th most congested Chinese city — [WIRED 2018](https://www.wired.com/story/alibaba-city-brain-artificial-intelligence-china-kuala-lumpur/), [CNN 2019](https://www.cnn.com/2019/01/15/tech/alibaba-city-brain-hangzhou) [VENDOR]
- Alibaba's own paper: Hangzhou ambulance response −50%, average travel time −15.3%; Shanghai travel time −8%, congestion index −15% — [Zhang et al., IET Smart Cities 2019](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/iet-smc.2019.0034) [VENDOR]
- Kuala Lumpur pilot (500 CCTV, 300 signals): "travel times could be reduced by 12% with proper implementation" (Alibaba Cloud statement) — [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/article/tech-alibaba-clouds-city-brain-could-reduce-kl-travel-time-12) [VENDOR]; critics note it "will do little" when drivers routinely ignore signals at rush hour — [Malaysian Insight](https://www.themalaysianinsight.com/s/38459?parent_id=8298); privacy/surveillance concerns — [WIRED 2018](https://www.wired.com/story/alibaba-city-brain-artificial-intelligence-china-kuala-lumpur/)

**Reinforcement learning — research vs deployment**
- Lemgo, Germany (Fraunhofer IOSB-INA, KI4LSA, 2020–22): DRL agent trained in simulation then run live at one real intersection via edge computer in the cabinet with camera+radar sensing; "approximately 10% reduction in travel time for vehicles" observed in the field; simulations showed 15–20% less noise/emissions and up to 20% fewer stops; described as "first time worldwide" RL controlled a real intersection — [Fraunhofer IOSB-INA Sept 2022](https://www.iosb-ina.fraunhofer.de/de/aktuelles_news/2022/KI4LSA-Projektabschluss.html) [INDEP—developer-measured]; NRW parliament evidence calls it a proof-of-concept whose results "are not fully transferable" — [Landtag NRW](https://www.landtag.nrw.de/portal/WWW/dokumentenarchiv/Dokument/MMST18-2144.pdf)
- 2025 review: "to date, no multi-agent traffic signal control application has been deployed in real-world environments" — [Michailidis et al., Infrastructures 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2412-3811/10/5/114) [INDEP]
- 2024 industry-oriented paper (Alibaba/Chinese authors): "there is no real-world deployment of an actual RL-based TSC to date"; real controllers only expose flow data, support cyclic phases, and require monotone cycle-flow relationships, which most RL methods ignore — [GuideLight, arXiv 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2407.10811) [INDEP]
- Offline-RL paper using real Zhuzhou intersection data: existing RL methods "hardly have any successful deployments" due to idealised simulators and unrealistic fine-grained observations — [D2TSC, arXiv 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.15920) [INDEP]
- Parameter-level RL (optimising engineer-designed control programme parameters rather than phases) showed ~20–23% performance-index improvement in simulation of a real German intersection; authors stress this preserves "all the mandatory certainties and guarantees" that direct phase control loses — [Meess et al., J. Simulation 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17477778.2024.2364715) [INDEP]
- RL benchmarks (CityFlow Hangzhou 4×4, Jinan 3×4, New York) still dominate; e.g., HAMH-PPO claims 48.7% over fixed-time and 31.4% over MaxPressure — [arXiv 2503.07678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07678) [INDEP—simulation only]
- The one large-scale RL field success in traffic is not signals: MARL variable speed limits on 67 controllers over 17 miles of I-24 (Nashville) since Mar 2024, with safety guards overriding RL ~2% of the time — [Zhang et al., arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01017) [INDEP]

### Inferences
- For the hackathon pitch, cite conservative ranges: ~10% travel-time improvement (FHWA average), 13.5% delay / 20% stops (Boston/INRIX), and −22% pedestrian wait (Shenzhen; NoTraffic Maricopa). Reserve Surtrac's 25/40% and Green Light's "up to 30%" for "best case vs poor baseline".
- A hackathon prototype should *not* be framed as RL; a rule-based/optimisation controller with a safety layer matches what is actually deployed, and reviewers will know RL has no production track record at intersections.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed evaluation of NoTraffic or Alibaba City Brain claims was found.
- No post-2022 Surtrac independent evaluation at the 50-intersection scale was found.

## Key Question 3: Pedestrian behaviour, minimum timings, standards (MUTCD, UK, GOST/CIS), and legal/safety constraints on changing vehicle green

### Takeaway
Compliance degrades sharply once pedestrian wait exceeds roughly 30–40 s (18% violate below 30 s vs 38% at 40–60 s in German data; 98% compliance at 30 s minimum vehicle green in a Florida mid-block study, falling at 60/120 s). Standards fix the hard constraints a pedestrian-adaptive controller must respect: walk interval ≥7 s (≥4 s allowed) and clearance at 3.5 ft/s (1.07 m/s) in MUTCD; green man 4–7 s by crossing length and 7 s minimum vehicle green in UK guidance; in GOST R 52289 (Russia/CIS, which Azerbaijan's practice closely mirrors) a mandatory 3 s yellow, ≤2 s red+yellow, mandatory 3 s flashing green on pedestrian signals, and pedestrian clearance computed at ~1.2–1.3 m/s with 3 s start-up. Vehicle green cannot be cut below its minimum green, and the change interval must still clear the dilemma zone — so "immediate pedestrian green" is only legal once minimum green and the full change interval have elapsed.

### Cited Findings

**Compliance vs waiting time**
- "Studies have shown that when pedestrian wait-times are below 30 seconds, compliance is significantly better"; Florida mid-block study: at 30 s minimum vehicle green 98% waited for walk; compliance fell and centre-line trapping rose as minimum green increased — [City of Portland half-signals report](https://trec.pdx.edu/sites/default/files/Bussey_Half%20Signals%20Report%20FINAL.pdf) [INDEP]
- Van Houten et al.: pedestrians willing to wait 30 s; 60 s and 120 s timings produced significantly more illegal crossings; German data: 38% crossed against the signal at 40–60 s waits vs 18% below 30 s — [McKernan, UConn thesis](https://digitalcommons.lib.uconn.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1916&context=gs_theses) [INDEP]
- NZTA review: after ~20–30 s frustration grows disproportionately; London study: 30–40% annoyed at 6–22 s delay, >70% annoyed above 26 s; German HBS recommends avoiding cycle times >90 s; VRU-TOO sensor trial (Leeds) reduced pedestrian–vehicle conflicts 18%; Spanish site: share waiting >30 s fell from 28% to 18% and signal-violation conflicts fell 51% — [NZTA Research Report 440](https://www.nzta.govt.nz/assets/resources/research/reports/440/docs/440.pdf) [INDEP]
- TRL/TfL: pedestrians more likely to comply if female, older, mobility impaired, waiting <30 s, heavy traffic, or others waiting; "only the waiting time is dependent upon the traffic signals" — [TRL PPR507](https://www.trl.co.uk/uploads/trl/documents/PPR507.pdf) [INDEP]
- Utah study of 5,589 crossings: 22% began crossing on steady Don't Walk; violations more likely overnight and with shorter waits/no other pedestrians; Brosseau et al. found significantly more violations where red phase >56 s — [Singleton, Utah DOT/ROSA P](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/68345/dot_68345_DS1.pdf) [INDEP]
- China hazard-duration model: violation propensity rises with waiting duration; ~50% still comply after 50 s; ~10% are high-risk violators regardless — [Guo et al., Traffic Inj Prev 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/15389588.2010.518652) [INDEP]
- Puffin study: "if the waiting time is longer than 40 seconds, the number of pedestrians crossing against the signal increases greatly" (Canadian review) but some authors find no link — [TfL Puffin behaviour report](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/puffin-behaviour-report.pdf)

**US MUTCD (11th ed., effective Jan 18, 2024)**
- Walk interval should be ≥7 s (4 s permitted if volumes allow); pedestrian clearance at 3.5 ft/s (1.07 m/s), slower where slow walkers/wheelchairs routinely cross; up to 4 ft/s permitted where an extended-press button or passive detection provides slower pedestrians more time; "passive pedestrian detection may also be used to automatically adjust the pedestrian clearance time based on the pedestrian's actual walking speed"; buffer ≥2 s steady Don't Walk before conflicting release; total walk+clearance should allow 3 ft/s from 6 ft behind curb; yellow ≥3 s; LPI ≥3 s — [MUTCD 11th ed. §4I.06](https://buildspec.codes/codes/mutcd/4i.06), [Federal Register final rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-12-19/html/2023-27178.htm)

**UK**
- LTN 2/95 (Pelican/Puffin): vehicle minimum green normally 7 s (6–15 s range), vehicle max 40 s (up to 60 s); 3 s amber; green man 4 s (≤7.5 m), 5 s (≤10.5 m), 6 s (≤12.5 m), 7 s (>12.5 m), +2 s where many disabled users — [DfT LTN 2/95](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/330214/ltn-2-95_pedestrian-crossings.pdf)
- Traffic Signs Manual Ch. 6: minimum green for traffic phases normally 7 s (5 s "with caution"); minimum green "cannot be overridden by demands from vehicles, manual control devices, central computers, or linked controllers"; add 2 s safety buffer to clearance — [DfT TSM Chapter 6](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/851465/dft-traffic-signs-manual-chapter-6.pdf)

**Russia/CIS GOST (framework Azerbaijan practice most closely resembles)**
- GOST R 52289-2019 §7.5.2: sequence red → red+yellow (≤2 s) → green → yellow (3 s in all cases) → red; if the computed intermediate interval exceeds 3 s the red is lengthened; §7.5.3: 3 s green flashing (1 Hz) before green ends — mandatory for pedestrian signals P.1/P.2; countdown displays permitted; audible signals per GOST R ISO 23600 where visually impaired pedestrians regularly cross; §7.5.4: flashing yellow allowed when flows fall below 50% of warrant thresholds, but not where there is a diagonal crossing or a pedestrian-safety warrant — [ConsultantPlus, GOST R 52289-2019 §7.5 (as amended 2025)](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_348566/1a86b3cbce7898eb0ce04fa13a3df96493aaa6e1/); older 2004 text — [ConsultantPlus §7.4](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_136094/478a21029c84ad58ccd908c21bc86d2149e83bd2/)
- Russian Mintrans methodology: pedestrian minimum green G_p = 3.2 s start-up + L/S_p (+ crowd term), with S_p = 1.2 m/s (15th-percentile speed), adjustable for district demographics; yellow 3 s; start-up lost time 3–4 s where pedestrians have not cleared — [Mintrans methodological recommendations](https://www.mintrans.gov.ru/file/404539)
- Russian research argues the fixed 3 s change interval does not guarantee pedestrian safety when vehicles legally complete their crossing as pedestrian green starts, and proposes direction-specific change intervals — [Gorodokin et al., Transp. Res. Procedia 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trpro.2018.12.067), [Shepelev et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trpro.2018.12.102) [INDEP]
- Pedestrian phase formula with 3 s start delay, speeds 0.5–1.5 m/s, 1 m between rows; a 22 m, 6-lane crossing with 3 rows needs 23 s — [Shepelev et al., CyberLeninka](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/raschet-prodolzhitelnosti-takta-raboty-svetofornogo-obekta-razreshayuschego-vyhod-peshehodov-na-proezzhuyu-chast)
- Astana set green-flashing to 4 s from 2019 "based on the analysis of foreign practice" — [Zhandarbekova et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3233/atde240039)

### Inferences
- Design targets for the prototype: hold pedestrian wait below ~30 s (and never above ~40–60 s); respect ≥7 s vehicle minimum green (5 s absolute floor), 3 s yellow + computed all-red, ≥4–7 s green man, clearance at ≤1.2 m/s (≤1.0 m/s where elderly), plus flashing-green 3 s per GOST; extension logic for slow walkers should add time to clearance (as MUTCD passive detection allows), not shorten it.
- "Immediate green when no cars" is lawful only as: terminate vehicle green at min-green expiry + full change interval; a camera-based "no vehicle approaching" check is exactly what TfL GPA does, and its vehicle detection needed upgrading at 6 of 18 sites.
- Call-cancel (Puffin) reduces wasted vehicle red; TfL found it was rarely triggered in practice and detector faults were common — plan health monitoring and a fail-safe default (serve the call).

### Gaps
- No Azerbaijan-specific signal-timing standard (AZS) text was found; the inference that Baku follows GOST-style parameters is based on CIS practice, not a cited Azerbaijani document.
- No study found that isolates compliance behaviour at *pedestrian-only mid-block* signals in CIS cities.

## Key Question 4: Low-cost implementation — edge devices, accuracy, privacy, cost vs loops, controller interfacing

### Takeaway
Raspberry Pi/Jetson + YOLO prototypes count waiting pedestrians at ~90–95% accuracy (one Pi 4/YOLOv8 study: 92.7%; Jetson Nano at ~6–9 FPS with lightweight models; Jetson Orin Nano 50 FPS); commercial passive pedestrian detectors cost $1,500–3,000 each (≈$30k/intersection for 8+), while Vienna's target was ≤€5,000 per pedestrian signal including camera. Inductive loops cost ~$18–20k per intersection with ~10% annual failure; video detection ~$22–42k with lower maintenance. Interfacing is the easy part: every controller type (NEMA TS1/TS2, 170/2070, UK TOPAS) sees a detector as a binary on/off contact input, so an edge box can present "pedestrian demand", "cancel", and "extend" as relay closures. Privacy precedent: process locally, delete frames within ~50 ms, store only counts/classes.

### Cited Findings
- Raspberry Pi 4 + camera + custom YOLOv8 for pedestrian-count-based crossing control: average count error 7.33% (92.67% accuracy) over 20 samples; 3 s dwell filter to ignore passers-by; red timing stepped by count (1–3 people 15 s, 4–7 → 20 s, >7 → 30 s); errors driven by occlusion — [ICISS 2024, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3700706.3700717) [INDEP]
- Jetson Nano: YOLOv8-nano 57 ms vs YOLOv10-nano 40 ms per 320×160 frame (≈17–25 FPS), 517–563 ms at 1280×640 — [Univ. Genoa comparison](https://unige.iris.cineca.it/retrieve/94c1f773-63a3-41c4-a508-325b4b57806d/Yolov8-v10%20-%20Last%20Version.pdf); SSD-MobileNet pedestrian/sign detection 8.7 FPS, 98.45% accuracy — [Sarvajcz et al., MDPI 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/4/1440); Jetson Orin Nano lightweight YOLOv8 variant 50 FPS — [Wang et al., MDPI 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/app14209445) [INDEP]
- Chinese thesis building an embedded pedestrian-crossing-demand detector on Jetson Nano: pruned YOLOv4 went from 0.8 to 5.7 FPS, accuracy +4.3%, custom dataset for rain/snow/night — [Globethesis 2023](https://www.globethesis.com/?t=2568306788958759)
- Pyeongtaek, Korea edge-AI smart intersections (3 sites): compressed YOLOX ran 29.5 FPS on-site with 93.64% accuracy after 97.8% model size reduction; signal coordination tuned with real-time counts, stops −7.7% in simulation — [Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1155/2024/8999086) [INDEP]
- FDOT automated pedestrian detection evaluation: best system detected pedestrians 94% of the time and placed a call 90%; removed the call 97% of the time when pedestrians left early; detector spec: 12–42 V, 3 W, dry-contact outputs, NEMA TS2 shock/vibration, IP67, −40 to +60 °C — [FDOT/USF report](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/64442/dot_64442_DS1.pdf) [INDEP]; later FDOT review notes camera systems degrade in fog/heavy rain and cost more — [FDOT BED25-977-14](https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/research/reports/fdot-bed25-977-14-rpt.pdf?sfvrsn=ccc8707f_3)
- Commercial passive pedestrian detectors: $1,500–3,000 per detector, 8+ per 4-leg intersection → ~$30,000/intersection — [Alameda CTC guidance 2020](https://www.alamedactc.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Appendix-E_Traffic_Signal_Technology_Implementation_Guidance_20200824.pdf)
- Vienna target: ≤€5,000 per camera pedestrian signal, equal to a push-button signal — [ORF Wien 2017](https://wien.orf.at/v2/news/stories/2879578/); Hong Kong 5-site video pilot HK$900,000 — [HK LegCo 2018](https://www.tlb.gov.hk/eng/psp/pressreleases/transport/land/2018/20180124a.htm)
- Vehicle detection 10-year life-cycle cost per intersection (Oregon): inductive loop $20,400; magnetometers $33,451; hybrid video+radar $56,400; thermal $72,311; video stop-bar $77,255; radar $85,469 — [ITS JPO 2021-sc00500](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2021-sc00500) [INDEP]; LA County: loops $18,600 initial + $10k 10-yr maintenance vs VIDS $22,500 + $4k; loop failure rate ~10%/yr — [LA DPW I-710 detection analysis](http://ladpw.org/TNL/ITS/i710/files/reports/Del%202.5.1.4/Del%202.5.1.4%20-%20System%20Detection%20Tech%20Analysis%20-%20Final.pdf) [INDEP]
- Adaptive software 10-year life-cycle cost for 10 intersections: $191k–$350k vs $140k baseline — [ITS JPO 2021-sc00500](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2021-sc00500)
- Controller interfacing: "Each detector input to a controller, regardless of detector technology, appears to the controller as simply on or off – a binary input. This is true in all cabinet types, including Model 332, NEMA TS1, NEMA TS2, and the ITS cabinet"; controllers poll inputs every 0.1 s — [LA DPW detection analysis](http://ladpw.org/TNL/ITS/i710/files/reports/Del%202.5.1.4/Del%202.5.1.4%20-%20System%20Detection%20Tech%20Analysis%20-%20Final.pdf); detectors are assigned to call, extend, or count for a phase — [FDOT detection design guide 2022](https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/traffic/cmtp/stamp/informational-guide-design-of-traffic-signal-detection-technologies_september-2022.pdf)
- NoTraffic architecture as a template: camera → Wi-Fi → on-site AI box hard-wired to the existing controller, LTE to cloud — [Design News 2021](https://www.designnews.com/automation/time-wasted-at-traffic-lights-is-astounding-you-could-get-some-of-it-back) [VENDOR]; Korean products connect "via the field controller board" — [NeoTraffic](https://www.neotraffic.co.kr/products/ai-pedestrian-extension) [VENDOR]
- Privacy precedent (Vienna): images processed locally, deleted within 50 ms, only counts/categories retained; training data captured on campus, not on public streets — [EurekAlert 2024](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1066452); Lemgo RL used "anonymous, GDPR-compliant sensor data" — [Fraunhofer 2022](https://www.iosb-ina.fraunhofer.de/de/aktuelles_news/2022/KI4LSA-Projektabschluss.html)

### Inferences
- A Pi 5 / Jetson Orin Nano-class box with a wide-angle camera over the waiting area, outputting dry-contact "call / cancel / extend" to the existing pedestrian-signal controller, is technically sufficient and mirrors Vienna/HK/FDOT architectures; the bill of materials is plausibly in the low hundreds of USD vs $1.5–3k per commercial detector.
- Count accuracy of ~90–93% is enough for group-size and presence logic but not for safety-critical clearance decisions, so clearance extension should be fail-safe (extend by default when uncertain).
- Baku's NİİM already centrally controls ~150–152 signal controllers, so an integration path via controller detector inputs (not replacement) is the realistic route.

### Gaps
- No published cost figure for a Vienna-style camera unit as actually procured, only the 2017 ≤€5,000 target.
- No source found on the specific controller models/protocols used by Baku's NİİM.

## Key Question 5: Known failures and criticisms of adaptive/AI signals

### Takeaway
Documented failures cluster around four causes: (1) inflated claims from comparing against stale baselines (FHWA says reports of 90% stop reductions reflect poor prior timings; signal timing degrades ~3%/yr so late "after" studies flatter results); (2) sensor failure in glare/fog leading to abandonment (Ada County, ID scrapped a $600k camera-based system in 2015); (3) pedestrian/equity harm when systems count only vehicles (Seattle Mercer St: 3-minute pedestrian waits, skipped walk phases, with no pedestrian metrics in the evaluation); and (4) side-street penalties (NCHRP: +3.4% side-street delay; FDOT: +17% minor-street queues). Detector maintenance is a recurring weak point even for simple Puffin crossings (4 of 5 sites faulty).

### Cited Findings
- FHWA: "Systems that were compared against poor existing timings get high marks ... failures of ASCT in [oversaturated] situations, in particular when they have been oversold to eliminate such congestion, tend to be notable"; before/after gaps of months bias results; signal timing degrades ~3%/yr; percentages "amplify differences in small numbers" — [FHWA-HOP-13-031](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop13031/fhwahop13031.pdf) [INDEP]
- Ada County Highway District (Boise) scrapped a Rhythm Engineering camera-based adaptive system across 22 sensors ($600k federal funds) because sun glare and fog defeated detection and side streets were not served; reverted to time-of-day plans and sought a refund — [KTVB 2015](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/achd-scraps-adaptive-traffic-light-program/277-22736840)
- Seattle Mercer St adaptive corridor: walk phases cut short, skipped for 2+ cycles, pedestrians waiting >3 minutes; westbound car travel times got worse, eastbound improved 18 s (AM) / 2.7 min (PM); SDOT's evaluation included no pedestrian measures; SDOT later agreed to stop skipping walk phases and include pedestrian delay metrics — [The Urbanist 2017](https://www.theurbanist.org/adaptive-signal-system-kicks-pedestrians-curb/)
- Seattle Pedestrian Advisory Board: adaptive signals counting only vehicles weight a 30-passenger bus the same as a single car and "completely ignore" pedestrians, who are 31% of users at Denny & Westlake — [SPAB letter 2018](https://www.seattle.gov/documents/Departments/SeattlePedestrianAdvisoryBoard/SPAB%20Adaptive%20Signals%20Letter%20Oct%202018.pdf); advocates asked for no new adaptive signals until the technology "can measure and mitigate delays to people walking"; induced-demand concern for the $10M Denny Way project — [The Urbanist 2019](https://old.theurbanist.org/2019/12/05/the-10-million-plan-for-adaptive-signals-on-denny-way/)
- Des Moines adaptive corridor: "small improvements in travel rate ... but the overall variability increased"; high initial and maintenance cost, labour-intensive tuning, agencies cap cycle lengths — [Iowa DOT RB09-016](http://publications.iowa.gov/29973/1/Performance-Based%20Operations%20Assessment%20of%20Adaptive%20Control%20RB09-016.pdf) [INDEP]
- Almaty: 93 "smart" intersections installed 2021 (of 580); promised citywide AI by end-2023, then slipped; throughput +20% on some intersections and doubled on Timiryazev St, but "the result on the overall picture of 9-point congestion was not particularly noticeable — everything is levelled by the growth in the number of cars"; in-pavement sensors are often destroyed by road crews and the system fails during power outages — [Time.kz, Jan 2024](https://time.kz/articles/territory/2024/01/15/umnik-gollandskij) [CITY]
- Green Light: optimises only private-vehicle stops, avoids complex multimodal sites, recommendations ignored in Manchester where bus priority is intended — [Scientific American 2024](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/googles-project-green-light-uses-ai-to-take-on-city-traffic/); German Yunex AI signals in Essenbach and Hamm "still quite annoying for drivers" — [heise 2024](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Traffic-control-how-Google-wants-to-make-traffic-lights-smarter-with-AI-9838704.html)
- Puffin: detector faults at 4 of 5 London sites; cancel facility non-functional at many sites nationally — [TfL Research Summary 8](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/research-summary-no-8-puffin-crossings.pdf), [TRL safety study](https://trid.trb.org/view.aspx?id=924158)
- Smoothing car flow may induce driving: "Measures taken so that cars don't have to stop typically have the opposite effect: they make people more interested in driving" — [Context/TRF 2025](https://www.context.news/ai/ai-helps-tackle-traffic-carbon-emissions-in-buenos-aires)

### Inferences
- A pedestrian-first adaptive crossing avoids the main equity criticism levelled at vehicle-only adaptive systems, which is a genuine differentiator for the pitch — provided the evaluation plan measures pedestrian delay and compliance, not just vehicle flow.
- Weather/glare robustness and detector health monitoring (fault → safe default) should be explicit design requirements, since both have sunk real deployments.

### Gaps
- No systematic review of pedestrian impacts of adaptive systems beyond the Seattle case and NCHRP side-street statistics was found.

### Inferences
- Baku's pilot is vehicle-flow oriented; the one pedestrian-only signal in it (school No. 115) is the natural comparison point for a pedestrian-first hackathon prototype, and NİİM's own messaging ("long red on an empty road at night") shows demand for the "immediate green when no cars" feature.
- Regional numbers (Astana +18% speed, Istanbul −15% travel time) are all city-sourced; Almaty's candid "not noticeable at network level" is a useful counterweight when setting expectations.

### Gaps
- No results yet from Baku's Dec 2025 pilot; vendor/foreign partner not named in sources found.
- No measured outcomes found for Tbilisi's smart signals or Tashkent's 2024 pilot.
- No pedestrian-adaptive crossing deployment with published results found anywhere in the Caucasus/Central Asia.
