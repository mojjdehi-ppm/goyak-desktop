# Bus Bunching: Mechanism, Proven Control Strategies, Deployments, AI Approaches, Metrics and Demo Simulation (as of 2024–2026)

Scope note: This brief covers why bunching happens, which control strategies work (with measured numbers), which cities deployed them, how AI/ML has been applied (including real pilots), and how a hackathon team with GPS/IoT bus data could build an anti-bunching tool. Vendor-sourced claims are marked **[VENDOR CLAIM]**. Simulation-only results are marked **[SIMULATION]**; field/real-world results are marked **[FIELD]**.

## Key Question 1: Mechanism — why fixed timetables fail under traffic, the positive-feedback loop, and the effect of fleet shortage and traffic

### Takeaway
Bunching is an inherent instability first characterized by Newell & Potts (1964): a bus that falls behind meets more waiting passengers, dwells longer, and falls further behind, while the bus behind it finds fewer passengers and speeds up — a positive feedback loop that no fixed timetable can hold without large amounts of schedule slack, which itself slows service. Traffic variability and missing trips (fleet/driver shortages) are the two dominant disturbance sources that seed the loop.

### Cited Findings
- "It has been known for nearly 50 years (Newell and Potts, 1964) that if buses are left to their own devices, they cannot stay on schedule. The reason is that a lagging bus has to collect more passengers, and therefore tends to fall further behind. And the reverse is also true. So there is a positive feedback loop that leads to undesirable bunching. Transit agencies typically deal with this problem by inserting slack into their schedules... Slack, however, reduces the buses' commercial speed... this medicine (slack) is sometimes worse than the illness." — [Daganzo & Pilachowski 2011, Transportation Research Part B](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261510000858)
- Newell & Potts (1964) "showed that the equations of bus motion exhibit a positive feedback loop; i.e., that if a bus is delayed so that its headway increases then it is delayed even more... this feedback loop leads to unstable platoon travel, and ultimately to bunching." Schedule-based control "usually require[s] too much slack to be effective. For this reason, the slack time provided in real systems is usually smaller, or nil, and reliability suffers." — [Argote-Cabanero, Daganzo & Lynn, "Dynamic Control of Complex Transit Systems" (UC Berkeley)](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6j16889k/qt6j16889k_noSplash_c53214284cd5f0a420be45fe2676deca.pdf)
- "Bus schedules cannot be easily maintained on busy lines with short headways... Although transit agencies build slack into their schedules to alleviate this problem – if necessary holding buses at control points to stay on schedule – their attempts often fail because practical amounts of slack cannot prevent large localized disruptions from spreading system-wide." — [Daganzo 2009, TR Part B 43(10)](https://its.berkeley.edu/publications/headway-based-approach-eliminate-bus-bunching-systematic-analysis-and-comparisons)
- Two root causes: "(i) the variability in travel time between stops and (ii) variations in passenger demand. These factors lead to an increase in bus headway variance and a consequent worsening of both the magnitude and variability of average waiting times." Under capacity constraints, "passengers waiting at a bus stop might not be able to board the first arriving bus (especially after long intervals), and have to wait for the next one." — [Delgado, Muñoz & Giesen 2012, TR Part B](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261512000653)
- TfL's own explanation: "Bus 1 is delayed by an unexpected incident. Just behind is bus 2 - which also gets delayed. Behind them, bus 3 runs without delay. After a while, buses running on time start to catch up with the delayed buses. The buses closest to the incident get delayed as they are picking up more and more passengers... At the same time, the buses running behind have fewer passengers to pick up." — [TfL Bus routes & borough reports](https://tfl.gov.uk/forms/14144.aspx)
- Schedule control is "Useful for low frequency (<5 buses/hour) lines without congestion: not the reality of major urban cities"; cons include "Not useful for high frequency or congested lines" and "Excessive slack in schedules." — [Giesen, "Avoiding Bus Bunching: From Theory to Practice" (PSU TREC seminar)](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1104&context=trec_seminar)
- Traffic as the fundamental issue in a real pilot: Capital Metro (Austin) held departures to the second for two weeks: "It definitely helped, but after about three or four miles, things started to break down in a major way... traffic was the fundamental issue. During the pilot, vehicle run-times changed dynamically and randomly with traffic." — [Swiftly / Capital Metro webinar write-up, 2019](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/mind-the-gap-best-practices-in-headway-management) **[VENDOR-HOSTED, agency quotes]**
- Critical-gap collapse: "Schedule-based or headway-based control schemes... cannot prevent buses from losing ground to the buses they follow when disruptions increase the gaps separating them beyond a critical value. (Following buses are then overwhelmed with passengers and cannot process their work quick enough to catch up.)" — [Daganzo & Pilachowski 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261510000858)
- Fleet/driver shortage link: In Chicago route 81 (Oct 2022), "15 to 25 percent of the daily scheduled trips were canceled" due to absenteeism, producing gaps that control had to fill. — [Rodriguez et al. 2025, arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231)
- APTA Feb 2022 survey of 117 agencies: 71% had to cut service or delay increases due to worker shortfalls; APTA Oct 2022 survey: 96% of 190 agencies reported workforce shortage, bus drivers the highest-vacancy position. — [TransitCenter, "Bus Operators in Crisis" 2022](https://transitcenter.org/publication/bus-operators-in-crisis/); [Stateline 2023](https://stateline.org/2023/01/31/ghost-buses-haunt-transit-agencies-and-frustrate-riders/)
- NYC MTA 2024: "80% of the lost trips this year can be pinned on operator vacancies and availability." — [The City, May 2024](https://www.thecityreporter.nyc/2024/05/16/express-bus-canceled-mta-drivers/)
- "When rides are dropped or delayed, buses bunch up, arriving at stops at the same time" — TransitMatters recommended headway management in response. — [GBH 2024](https://www.wgbh.org/news/local/2024-09-06/ghosted-by-the-bus-why-some-mbta-buses-never-arrive)
- Review of 60 years of research: bunching "can spread through the network from one stop and route to others... Hybrid methods combining holding with other measures have outperformed standalone holding methods but need real-world testing." — [Rezazada et al. 2024, Transport Reviews](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01441647.2024.2313969)
- London's network EWT has been "under pressure since 2022-23" with TfL citing "traffic levels and delays" and "mechanical issues affecting vehicle availability" as causes of longer waits. — [TfL Buses performance data](https://tfl.gov.uk/corporate/publications-and-reports/buses-performance-data); [TfL Q4 2023/24 report](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/customer-service-and-operational-performance-report-quarter-4-2023-24-acc.pdf)

### Inferences
- A city with a fleet shortage and heavy traffic is in the worst quadrant: missing trips create large initial gaps (supra-critical in Daganzo's terms) and traffic variability continually re-seeds them. This is exactly the regime where schedule adherence fails and where headway-based control gives the most benefit per dollar — because it extracts regular service from whatever fleet is actually on the road.
- Adding schedule slack to "fix" bunching consumes bus-hours (lower commercial speed → more buses needed for the same frequency), which is unaffordable when the fleet is short. Dynamic control is the substitute for slack.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the share of bunching attributable to traffic vs. demand vs. missing trips for a specific city; it is route-specific.

## Key Question 2: Operational strategies with measured results

### Takeaway
Headway-based holding (at terminals and a few en-route control points) is the most repeatedly field-validated strategy, delivering roughly 5–26% reductions in (excess) waiting time in real pilots even with 35–57% driver compliance; cooperative speed guidance and TSP are the theoretically strongest (because they do not slow buses down) but have mostly simulation evidence plus a few field tests; boarding-time measures (all-door boarding, off-board payment) cut dwell per passenger by 38–68% and are cheap "upstream" fixes that reduce the feedback gain.

### Cited Findings

**Headway-based vs. schedule-based control**
- **[FIELD]** Washington DC WMATA routes 70/79 headway-control experiment: "Overall, a reduction of 26% in passenger excess waiting time was attained, which implies annual time savings that translate into US$1 million. Even though the field experiment implementation was far from ideal..." — [Soza-Parra, Cats et al. 2019, TRR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0361198119845369)
- **[FIELD]** Stockholm RETT3 (autumn 2012, headway-based driver display): "Service regularity has improved even though the implementation conditions were far from ideal. The coefficient of variation of the headway was much more stable along the line. The extra waiting time passengers experienced due to reliability problems decreased overall by 5-10% with a decrease of up to 45% at some stops." Drawbacks: "several system functionality problems and persistent timetable constraints." — [Cats, RETT3 final report (TRID)](https://trid.trb.org/View/1375940)
- **[FIELD]** Stockholm trials (2011 line 1; 2012 lines 1 & 2), ~100 drivers, in-bus screen showing position relative to bus ahead: "more even distance between the buses and fewer fully-loaded buses. The drivers became less stressed and the time that passengers had to wait for the bus every day was cut by ten percent." The regularity-based strategy "is now included in the long-term contracts that SL has with its operators." — [KTH impact case](https://www.kth.se/en/forskning/sarskilda-forskningssatsningar/strategiska-forskningsomraden/impactcases/regularity-based-bus-services-stockholm-1.935863)
- **[FIELD]** Stockholm line 4 package pilot (17 Mar–19 Jun 2014; 65,000 pax/day, 4-min headway): stop consolidation (4 of ~30 stops removed per direction), third-door boarding, +3 km bus lanes, real-time headway-based holding, parking removal → "service that is 7% faster on average yielding a 10% decrease in passenger in-vehicle times... 8 minutes shorter cycle time... improved regularity and less bunching leading to a 25% reduction in passengers' waiting times due to irregularity... each passenger saved 2 minutes which is 10% of journey time." — [Cats et al., CASPT 2015](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/paper11.pdf)
- **[FIELD]** Chicago CTA pilots (2022–2023, RL-based decision support with even-headway fallback): Route 81 terminal holding (Oct 2022, with 15–25% of trips cancelled): AM wait time −8.7% (7.25→6.62 min), PM −19.9% (8.28→6.63 min); PM 90th-pct load −15.5%. Route 66 en-route holding (Nov 2023, ~15,000 rides/day): wait time −5.8% (5.13→4.83 min), 90th-pct load 60.0→56.7, with 1.6 fewer trips/day than baseline. Driver compliance was 35% (route 81) and 56.9% (route 66). "despite the lower compliance, the performance benefits from control are significant." — [Rodriguez et al. 2025, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231)
- **[FIELD]** MBTA Green Line terminal headway-based dispatching with tablets: "When optimized departure times were adhered to, peak period headway variability was reduced by 40%. The average wait was shortened by 15% (30 sec), and the 90th percentile wait was shortened by 21% (90 sec)." — [Fabian, Sánchez-Martínez & Attanucci 2018, TRR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0361198118794534)
- **[FIELD]** Georgia Tech trolley (Bartholdi & Eisenstein, 2012): self-equalizing headway rule at two terminals, using NextBus GPS predictions: "Our scheme entirely avoided severe bunching: The smallest headway under our scheme was 2 minutes 28 seconds, compared to only 1 second under scheduled service on a comparable day. Similarly, the largest headway under our scheme was 14 minutes 59 seconds, but 19 minutes 45 seconds under scheduled service." After removing one of three buses, "headways of successive arrivals reequalized at about 17 minutes." System "began full-time use at Georgia Tech in August 2013." — [Bartholdi & Eisenstein, TR Part B 2012 (PDF)](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/papers/Bartholdi-Eisenstein-2011.pdf); [project page](https://www.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/buses/buses.html)
- **[FIELD]** Transantiago line 210 pilot (from March 2014): "40-50% decrease in fines despite a 25% compliance rate." Transmilenio (Bogotá) C84 pilot: cycle time with control 63.4 min (SD 5.6) vs 69.5 min (SD 11.8) without. Dispatch irregularity at terminals was the main driver of downstream irregularity (end-point CV ≈ 0.69×dispatch CV + 0.30, R²=0.87). — [Giesen et al., CASPT 2015 talk](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/talk49.pdf)
- **[FIELD]** Santiago pilot: "on the days the system was implemented less bus bunching was observed, which translated in fewer headway-irregularity fines. Moreover lower passenger fare evasion was observed"; validations (demand) "increased by 20% during the pilot plans." — [Lizana, Muñoz, Giesen, Delgado 2014, Procedia CS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2014.05.440); [Giesen TREC seminar](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1104&context=trec_seminar)
- **[FIELD]** Donostia (Dbus, Spain) multi-line control via cheap on-board tablets (Via Analytics app): ~22% improvement in schedule-deviation metric at α=0.9 over an already >95% on-time system; median driver violated holding orders 13.8% of the time (355 drivers, 5 months); when α was dropped to 0.5 without retraining, median violation rose to 42.1%. GPS on tablets malfunctioned 1.73% of the time. — [Argote-Cabanero, Daganzo & Lynn (UC Berkeley)](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6j16889k/qt6j16889k_noSplash_c53214284cd5f0a420be45fe2676deca.pdf)
- **[FIELD]** Community Transit SWIFT BRT (Everett, WA): moved from pure headway management to "hybrid" operations; bunching 4.17% (baseline) → 3.28% (hybrid, no holds) → 4.02% (hybrid with holds); severely bunched 0.99% → 0.77% → 0.74%; "Sticking with a headway based approach was right decision." — [TRB BRT Conference 2018 presentation](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/Conferences/2018/BRT/DMurphy.pdf)
- **[FIELD, early]** Portland Tri-Met (2001) supervisor-directed holding/short-turning/swaps: headway-ratio variance −3.8% overall, −15.8% at the control point (not statistically significant); passenger-load variance −16%. — [Journal of Public Transportation 4(1), 2001](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/jpt/vol4/iss1/6)
- **[SIMULATION]** Headway-based holding "better mitigate[s] irregularity along the line, especially when there are disruptions. However, schedule-based control strategies are currently easier to implement... the performance of both strategies is generally equal in regular, undisrupted situations." (Dutch regional line) — [TRR 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0361198119845893)
- **[SIMULATION]** Delgado et al. rolling-horizon holding (HRT) and holding+boarding limits (HBLRT): "Excess waiting savings of up to 77% with low variability"; boarding limits "only beneficial under high frequency and high demand." Share of passengers waiting >10 min: 3.58% (no control) → 0.95% (threshold) → 0.04% (HRT). — [Delgado, Muñoz, Giesen 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261512000653); [CASPT 2015 talk](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/talk49.pdf)
- **[SIMULATION]** Driver heterogeneity: "an easy way of improving headway regularity is to stratify drivers by line according to historical driving speed, regardless of whether or not a control action is applied." — [TR Part A vol. 174 (PUC Chile)](https://www.ing.uc.cl/publicaciones/improving-the-performance-of-headway-control-tools-by-using-individual-driving-speed-data/)

**Speed guidance / driver advisory (cooperative control)**
- **[THEORY+SIMULATION]** Daganzo & Pilachowski 2011: two-way-looking speed control ("as if successive bus pairs were connected by springs") "yield[s] regular headways with faster bus travel than existing control methods. Its simple and decentralized logic automatically compensates for traffic disruptions and inaccurate bus driver actions. Its hardware and data requirements are minimal." — [TR Part B 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261510000858)
- **[SIMULATION, Barcelona H6, 19.3 km, 5-min headway, 21 buses]** Speed-modification strategy reduced total cost 18–26% and headway CV 41–62% vs. slack-based baseline; adding traffic-light priority for delayed buses reduced total system cost by 40% and achieved CV<0.21 (LOS A), whereas even 6 min of terminal slack left CV>0.8 (LOS F). — [Mension (TMB) & Estrada (UPC), AET 2016](https://aetransport.org/public/downloads/Bv7HG/4816-57cd5cc05c897.pdf); [Estrada et al. 2016, TR Part C 71](https://upcommons.upc.edu/entities/publication/06c534f6-54a5-4287-853c-da31de7df579/full)
- **[SIMULATION, Nanchang line 245]** Real-time speed + dwell + load control: headway stability σh=0.05, average travel time ~34% lower than multi-point headway control; timetable control with more control points raised travel time ~36%. — [Sustainability 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/13/7870)
- **[FIELD, test track]** Bus GLOSA (speed advisory for signals) with 30 drivers: 22.1% fuel and 6.1% travel-time savings vs. uninformed driving. — [Chen & Rakha 2022, Energies](https://doi.org/10.3390/en15041491)
- **[SIMULATION, Luxembourg]** Hybrid holding + driver advisory system "additional benefits compared to traditional holding in terms of regularity while similar performance to strong transit signal priority." — [Laskaris et al. 2019, MT-ITS](https://doi.org/10.1109/mtits.2019.8883372)

**Stop-skipping / short-turning / boarding limits**
- Definitions and trade-offs: stop skipping "represents a transformation from regular to limited service in real time... at the expense of increased wait time for persons at stops that have been passed by and passengers who are forced to alight early"; short-turning "primarily benefits passengers traveling in the opposite direction." — [JPT 2001](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/jpt/vol4/iss1/6)
- **[SIMULATION]** Cooperative holding + stop-skipping via MARL (DDQN-HA): 12–14% wait-time reduction vs no control; only 3.2% better than simple even-headway holding on mean wait, 6.5% better on reliability buffer time. — [OSTI / DOE paper](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2577182)
- **[FIELD]** Dubai RTA uses "optimisation of short-turning operations" as one of its AI dashboard applications (2026). — [Dubai Media Office, June 2026](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/june/08-06/rta-deploys-ai-to-enhance-real-time-bus-operations-and-efficiency)
- **[FIELD]** Shenzhen Bus Group uses OD data to design "large-stop express" (大站快车) variants on ~10 routes, claimed to save 21% travel time for 70% of passengers. — [Shenzhen SASAC 2023](https://gzw.sz.gov.cn/gkmlpt/content/10/10487/post_10487228.html) **[OPERATOR CLAIM]**

**Transit signal priority (TSP) and bus lanes**
- TSP travel-time savings in North America "ranged from 2 to 18 percent, with typical reductions of 8 to 12 percent"; LA Metro BRT corridors −7.5%; Chicago Cermak Rd −15%; bus delay at TSP signals reduced 15–80%. — [USDOT ITS KRS / TCRP synthesis](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2013-b00847)
- King County Metro Rainier Ave TSP (2000): "TSP reduced bus travel time variability by 35 percent," signal-related stops −50%, ~5 s saved per intersection. LA: "Buses were instructed to slow down or speed up to avoid interference with other buses en-route." — [USDOT ITS KRS](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2007-b00345)
- TransLink (Vancouver) 2023 Bus Speed & Reliability Report: bus priority projects improved speeds 5–35%; bus/BAT lanes up to 15%; queue jumps ~15%; signal upgrades up to 20%; RapidBus routes R2/R3/R4 saved 24–35% of run+dwell time; "Most projects yield a return-on-investment within 10 years—many within two years." — [TransLink 2023 BSR Report Part 4](https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/plans-and-projects/bus-projects/bus-speed-and-reliability/tl-2023-bsr-report_20240415_part4.pdf)
- SF Van Ness BRT (2025 report): "Bus travel is now up to 36% faster... Transit is 45% more reliable." — [SFMTA 2025](https://www.sfmta.com/index%2ephp/blog/new-report-van-ness-brt-earns-high-marks-travel-time-and-safety-improvements)
- SCAG synthesis: TSP saves 8–12% of travel time; bus lanes 20–60 s/mile, 12–30% change in travel-time CV; TSP reliability improvement "up to 40% (TransLink)." — [SCAG Regional Dedicated Transit Lanes Study](https://iff.scag.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2024-05/3038_scag-rdtlstransitpriority_final.pdf)

**Boarding-time reduction**
- **[FIELD]** SFMTA all-door boarding (systemwide from 1 July 2012; proof-of-payment, cash still accepted at front door): dwell per boarding/alighting on two-door buses 3.93→2.45 s (−38%); rear-door boarding 29%→54%; "All-Door Boarding also has improved dwell time consistency and lowered variability, an important factor in helping reduce vehicle bunches and gaps"; fare evasion 9.5%→7.9% (2009–2014); system speed 8.41→8.56 mph. — [SFMTA All-Door Boarding Evaluation 2014](https://www.sfmta.com/sites/default/files/agendaitems/2014/12-2-14%20Item%2014%20All%20Door%20Boarding%20Report.pdf); [NAP summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/29448/chapter/4)
- **[FIELD]** NYC SBS Bx41 (2013; off-board payment + all-door + bus lanes): dwell per boarding 3.52→1.12 s (−68%); "wait assessment improved by 8.9 percent, on-time performance by 27.9 percent, running time by 15 percent, and bus bunching by 63 percent." — [Weinzimmer, SBS Bx41 study](https://dweinz.com/assets/pdfs/SBS-Bx41-Study-Final-Public.pdf)
- NYC SBS routes: total time stopped at stops −28% to −40%; dwell per passenger −42% to −51%; ridership +6% to +30% (Bx12, M15, Bx41, B44). — [NACTO "Better Boarding, Better Buses" 2017](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/NACTO_Better-Buses_Boarding.pdf)
- **[FIELD]** Montreal all-door boarding pilot: 5% passenger-activity-time savings at regular stops, ~19% at metro-station stops. — [El-Geneidy et al. 2017, TR Part A](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2017.03.005)
- Per-passenger boarding times: dip cards ~4.2 s, exact change 3.6–4.3 s, prepayment 2.25–2.75 s; low-floor buses cut boarding ~20%. — [Weinzimmer, citing TCRP](https://dweinz.com/assets/pdfs/SBS-Bx41-Study-Final-Public.pdf)

### Inferences
- Rank by evidence × feasibility for a fleet-short, traffic-heavy city with no new buses: (1) headway-based terminal dispatching + 1–2 en-route control points (software + tablets only; 5–26% EWT reduction in field pilots); (2) all-door boarding / off-board or tap validation at all doors (policy change; cuts dwell 38–68% and reduces the feedback gain); (3) targeted TSP at the few intersections where GPS data shows run-time variance originates (8–12% travel time, up to 35–40% variability); (4) bus lanes where politically feasible. Speed guidance is cheap to add once drivers have a display but has thin field evidence.
- Terminal dispatch regularity is the highest-leverage single control point (Transmilenio: downstream CV tracks dispatch CV with R²=0.87; Capital Metro and CTA route 81 both targeted it) and requires no driver compliance en route — only a supervisor or an app at the terminus.
- Because headway control "is robust to missing trips" (CTA route 66 improved with 1.6 fewer trips/day; Georgia Tech re-equalized after removing a bus), it directly converts a fleet-shortage problem (random gaps) into a manageable one (even, slightly longer headways).

### Gaps
- Few field studies isolate stop-skipping or short-turning benefits with before/after numbers; evidence is mostly simulation or bundled in BRT packages.
- No field evidence found for pure driver speed-advisory headway control at scale (Stockholm's displays included speed-up/slow-down hints but results are reported as combined).

## Key Question 3: Deployments and measured improvements in headway regularity / EWT

### Takeaway
The best-documented city-scale programs are Singapore's Bus Service Reliability Framework (EWT-based incentives/penalties; −15% to −25% average additional wait time across 108–292 services, 2016–2017) and London's iBus/QSI regime (network EWT ~0.95–1.19 min, worsening since 2022 due to traffic and vehicle availability). Santiago, Stockholm, Washington DC, Chicago, Boston, Donostia and Atlanta have route-level pilots with 5–26% waiting-time gains; Dubai and Shenzhen report AI-driven operations improvements but mostly in punctuality/cancellation metrics rather than headway regularity. Nothing substantive was found for Barcelona TMB field results, Abu Dhabi, Moscow or Tbilisi headway control.

### Cited Findings

**Singapore LTA — Bus Service Reliability Framework (BSRF)**
- BSRF trial started Feb 2014 with 22 high-frequency services, "modelled on service quality standards applied in cities such as London"; operators earn up to S$6,000 per 0.1 min EWT reduction and are fined up to S$4,000 per 0.1 min deterioration. — [TODAY, June 2014](https://www.todayonline.com/singapore/bus-reliability-study-shows-encouraging-early-signs-lta)
- Operator measures during the no-penalty start-up (Feb–May 2014): "recruited and trained more service controllers to monitor and regulate en-route bus service performance. Bus captains were trained to work with service controllers more closely to regulate bus speeds. Operators also enhanced their bus management systems..." Result: fewer bunching instances and reduced EWT on 12 of 15 trial services. — [Singapore Parliament PQ (Telescope)](https://telescope.gov.sg/transcript/12006)
- Final trial assessment (Dec 2015–Aug 2016, 45 services): "20 per cent reduction in average additional wait times"; S$3.4M incentives paid; "From September [2016], BSRF has been implemented for 108 services, and a 15 per cent reduction in average additional wait times has been observed." — [Straits Times, Mar 2017](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/transport/sbs-transit-and-smrt-earn-34-million-in-incentives-for-improving-bus-reliability)
- Sept 2016–July 2017: "The average additional wait time for 292 high frequency bus services has reduced by 25 per cent"; all services now under BSRF; all services have peak headways ≤15 min. — [Straits Times, Sept 2017](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/transport/bus-services-more-reliable-and-less-crowded-now-lta)
- BSRF is now embedded in the Bus Contracting Model (Sept 2016): "reliability of a bus service is assessed using either the Excess Wait Time or On Time Adherence metric... bus captains adjust their travelling speed to maintain regular intervals between buses." — [LTA Bus Contracting Model](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/who_we_are/our_work/public_transport_system/bus/bus_contracting_model.html)
- 2017–2025: share of services meeting performance baselines 94.0% (2017), 99.2% (2018), 98.4, 100.0, 98.1, 97.4, 97.0, 84.9 (2024), 90.5% (2025); reliability incentives/penalties ranged −0.1% to +5.9% of contracted fees; LTA considering releasing disaggregated reliability data by early 2027. — [Singapore MOT, Oct 2026](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/breakdown-of-bus-operator-key-performance-indicators--incentive-payments-and-penalties-since-introduction-of-bus-contracting-model/)
- Caveat from academics: trial results "came from a small sample"; passengers "do not measure waiting times at the level of seconds." — [TODAY, April 2015](https://www.todayonline.com/singapore/sbs-and-smrt-earn-over-s1m-incentives-improving-bus-services)

**London TfL — iBus and Quality of Service Indicators**
- TfL high-frequency routes (≥5 buses/h) are measured by EWT; "we aim to ensure buses run at evenly spaced intervals and do not bunch." Historically EWT came from ~40,000 survey hours/year with >100 surveyors (2006). — [Currie et al. 2012 ATRF](https://australasiantransportresearchforum.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2012_Currie_Douglas_Kearns.pdf)

## Key Question 4: AI/ML approaches — RL holding control, arrival-time prediction, AI scheduling — and demonstrated benefits

### Takeaway
Reinforcement learning for holding control has moved from simulation (typical claims: 20–60% headway-variance reduction vs. no control, but only a few percent over a well-tuned even-headway rule) to a real two-route pilot in Chicago (2022–23) that wrapped RL in guardrails with a classical even-headway fallback; ML arrival-time prediction (GBDT, LSTM/GNN) halves error vs. schedule-delay baselines and is the enabling input for both control and passenger information; AI scheduling vendors (Optibus, Swiftly) report 10–13 point OTP gains and ~10% peak-vehicle savings (vendor claims).

### Cited Findings

**Reinforcement learning for holding (and real pilots)**
- **[FIELD]** CTA pilots: "A decision support system was developed for a bus holding strategy based on a reinforcement learning approach... models are pre-trained in simulated operational conditions that are based on empirical data sources. For robust deployments, guardrails are built around the model's recommended holding actions to enforce service plan constraints, such as limits on lateness and excessive holding times. Also, an even-headway strategy similar to classical methods is formulated and used as an additional source of holding recommendations, as an alternative in case the RL model fails." Results: wait −8.7% (AM) / −19.9% (PM) on route 81; −5.7% on route 66; hundreds of interventions via a supervisor app. — [Rodriguez et al. 2025, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231)
- **[SIMULATION, open-source]** Systematic RL evaluation (IEEE T-ITS 2025): "simpler state representations (e.g., considering only forward and backward spacings between adjacent buses) may outperform more complex ones"; "discrete holding time achieve comparable results to their continuous counterparts"; "multi-agent methods can slightly improve performance, their computational costs can outweigh benefits"; "RL approaches demonstrate strong generalizability across diverse routes and fleet sizes." — [Xu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/tits.2025.3545642)
- **[SIMULATION]** DRL dynamic holding: headway variation −61.9% vs no control, −61.0% vs schedule-based, −38.0% vs headway-based; average waiting time −28.4%/−26.5%/−23.6%. — [Journal of Transportation Systems Engineering and IT, 2024](http://www.tseit.org.cn/EN/Y2024/V24/I5/173)
- **[SIMULATION, 50 replications, Gainesville routes 35/37]** V2X + SAC RL "BOCS": 65–71% headway-deviation reduction, 29% waiting-time reduction, 51% overcrowding reduction vs robust headway controllers; 128 ms decision latency. — [Alobeidyeen 2026, Intelligent Transportation Infrastructure](https://academic.oup.com/iti/article/8422771)
- **[SIMULATION, SUMO, 12 Changsha lines, 3.37M logged transitions]** Offline RL (CQL, BC, RE-SAC) and offline-to-online (WSRL, RLPD): WSRL best return; mean passenger wait 301±4 s; "no single controller was best on every passenger and service outcome." — [Zhang et al. 2026, Transportation Safety and Environment](https://academic.oup.com/tse/advance-article/doi/10.1093/tse/tdag059/8812242)
- **[SIMULATION]** LLM-enhanced RL (GPT-4 reward design) for holding reduced average travel time by only ~3.14% vs no-holding. — [Yu, Wang, Ma 2024, arXiv 2410.10212](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10212v2.pdf)
- **[SIMULATION]** MARL holding + stop-skipping beat even-headway (EH) holding by only 1.5–3.2% on mean wait. — [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2577182)
- Multi-agent DRL holding framework with joint-action tracker tested on a "real-world transit simulator." — [Wang & Sun 2020, TR Part C](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0968090X20305763)
- **[SIMULATION]** Time-window (10-min) joint holding optimisation vs event-based one-headway rule on Stockholm line 1 in SUMO: 0–17% lower waiting-time variance, ~5% better in-vehicle time; degrades when link travel-time estimates have ≥50% noise. — [Gkiotsalitis & Cats 2019, Transportmetrica B](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21680566.2019.1606743)

**Arrival-time prediction**
- **[ACADEMIC]** GNN+LSTM on GTFS + real-time positions (Belo Horizonte, 246M records): MAE ≈13.1 s / MAPE 1.53% vs ≈26.3–26.7 s for mean-delay baselines (per stop, per hour). Notes Belo Horizonte real-time data lacked GTFS-RT identifier matching. — [ACM AI-ML Systems 2024/25](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3703412.3703417)
- **[VENDOR CLAIM]** Swiftly: CTtransit "24% more accurate ETAs over previous system; 67% reduction in predictions that would cause passengers to miss their bus"; MBTA "up to 30% more accurate"; DCTA prediction accuracy +~10%. — [Swiftly CTtransit case](https://www.goswift.ly/case-study/ct-transit); [Swiftly MBTA](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/how-the-mbta-is-using-swiftly-apis-to-expand-their-operations-toolkit); [Swiftly DCTA 2025](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/dcta-otp-case-study)
- **[VENDOR CLAIM]** Huawei Cloud/Shenzhen Bus: GBDT spatiotemporal model, predictions "accurate to less than 2 minutes." — [Huawei](https://www.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/cases/szbs.html)
- Early GPS-based prediction (Blacksburg VA): algorithm using time-check-stop dwell had 2.0 min mean deviation vs 3.6 min for schedule-only. — [USDOT](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/5137/dot_5137_DS1.pdf)

**AI scheduling / headway analytics products**
- **[VENDOR CLAIM]** Swiftly Headways module (2019): classifies departures as bunched/expected/gapped by route, stop and time of day with customisable thresholds; used to decide "Where would Transit Signal Priority have the biggest impact." — [Swiftly](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/think-like-your-riders-introducing-the-swiftly-headways-module)
- **[VENDOR CLAIM]** Swiftly Onboard App (operator tablet guidance): MDOT MTA pilot — buses "25% more likely to be on time," early departures −37%; texting early operators cut early arrivals 20%. - **[VENDOR CLAIM]** Optibus Predictive Runtimes (Jacksonville JTA, Fall 2024 data): OTP 83.4%→94% (up to 98% peak). Grupo Ruiz: OTP 97%, vehicle efficiency +2%. TST (Portugal): peak vehicle requirement 340→~307 (−10%) via timetable optimisation. — [Optibus JTA 2025](https://optibus.com/case/how-jacksonville-transportation-authority-boosted-on-time-performance-by-13-with-optibus-and-snapper-services/); [Optibus Grupo Ruiz](https://optibus.com/case/improving-service-punctuality-for-passengers-using-optimization-algorithms/); [Optibus TST](https://optibus.com/case/transportes-sul-do-tejo-achieves-operational-excellence-using-optibus-timetable-optimization/)
- Via Analytics built the tablet app used in the Donostia multi-line control deployment (see KQ2). — [Argote-Cabanero et al.](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6j16889k/qt6j16889k_noSplash_c53214284cd5f0a420be45fe2676deca.pdf)

### Inferences
- The marginal gain of RL over a well-implemented even-headway rule is small (single-digit %) in most simulations; the big gain (20–60%) is from *any* headway-based control vs. none. For a hackathon, the credible, defensible architecture is: classical two-way-looking headway rule as the baseline/fallback + an ML layer for (a) arrival/travel-time prediction and (b) tuning hold times — exactly the CTA guardrail design.
- ML prediction quality is the real differentiator for both control and passenger information; off-the-shelf gradient boosting on link travel times by time-of-day/day-of-week, updated with live positions, is sufficient to beat schedule baselines by ~2x.

### Gaps
- No published pilot of fully autonomous RL control (without human supervisor in the loop) was found.
- Vendor OTP figures are not headway-regularity figures; no vendor publishes EWT/CV before-after for a headway-management product.

## Key Question 5: Driver-facing vs dispatcher-facing vs passenger-facing tools — highest impact for low cost

### Takeaway
Dispatcher/supervisor-facing terminal dispatching is the cheapest high-impact layer (no driver behaviour change en route; 15–40% headway-variance reductions when adhered to); driver-facing displays add en-route control but compliance is the binding constraint (35–57% in Chicago, median 14–42% violations in Donostia, 25% in Santiago) and must be designed with simple, infrequent instructions and training; passenger-facing real-time information does not fix bunching but cuts perceived waits ~13–30% and actual waits ~2 min, and lets riders self-balance between a crowded leader and an empty follower.

### Cited Findings
- Capital Metro: "Dispatch alone can't eliminate bunching and gapping"; terminal control decayed after 3–4 miles; drivers "trained for years to keep to a schedule, found it difficult to change their mindset." — [Swiftly/Metro 2019](https://www.metro-magazine.com/blogposts/mind-the-gap-best-practices-in-bus-headway-management)
- CTA: compliance 35% at terminal (lower the further the instruction deviated from schedule) vs 56.9% en-route "attributed to the presence of a supervisor to enforce the holding time"; drivers left early at stops just before signals; compliance "related to the mentality of schedule adherence and seniority." — [arXiv 2509.08231](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08231)
- Phillips et al. 2015: quantifies "effects of driver non-compliance and communication system failure" on real-time holding; CTA cites it as showing regularity gains "even with lower compliance levels." — [Phillips et al. 2015, TR Part A](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transa/v78y2015icp463-472.html)
- Donostia: larger/more variable holds "could stress drivers and reduce acceptance"; after training, median violation 13.8%; with more aggressive control and no retraining, 42.1%. — [Argote-Cabanero et al.](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6j16889k/qt6j16889k_noSplash_c53214284cd5f0a420be45fe2676deca.pdf)
- Georgia Tech drivers "liked not having to check the clock constantly... freed them to concentrate on safe driving." — [Bartholdi & Eisenstein](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/papers/Bartholdi-Eisenstein-2011.pdf)
- Stockholm: drivers "became less stressed"; but recurrent technical problems "made it very difficult for drivers to follow and trust the system." — [KTH](https://www.kth.se/en/forskning/sarskilda-forskningssatsningar/strategiska-forskningsomraden/impactcases/regularity-based-bus-services-stockholm-1.935863); [RETT3](https://trid.trb.org/View/1375940)
- Singapore operators' first moves were hiring service controllers and training captains "to regulate bus speeds" with controllers. — [Telescope PQ](https://telescope.gov.sg/transcript/12006)
- MBTA Green Line: terminal personnel with tablets showing even-headway departure times; benefits "if operational changes are accompanied by improved supervision." — [TRR 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0361198118794534)
- MBTA Skate: 80% of bus inspectors adopted the tablet tool. — [Swiftly](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/how-the-mbta-is-using-swiftly-apis-to-expand-their-operations-toolkit) **[VENDOR CLAIM]**
- Passenger-facing RTI (Seattle OneBusAway): users report average wait 7.5 vs 9.9 min (−30%); RTI "decreases the perceived wait time by 0.7 min (about 13%)"; RTI users "wait almost 2 min less" in measured terms. — [Watkins et al. 2011, TR Part A](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856411001030)
- Tampa controlled experiment: usual wait fell 1.79 min (16%) for RTI group vs 0.21 min control; anxiety/frustration fell; no change in trip frequency. — [Brakewood, Barbeau, Watkins](https://candacebrakewood.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/brakewood-barbeau-watkins-preprint-version-of-paper.pdf)
- NYC natural experiment: RTI associated with ~118 extra unlinked trips per route per weekday, mostly on largest routes. — [Brakewood et al. 2015, TR Part C](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0968090X15000297)
- Seoul exposes on-bus crowding in apps so travellers can "wait for a less crowded bus." — [World Bank](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/fe16467f3f9165aadf561473d0cd7c91-0400012022/original/Final-LUTP-Seoul-Smart-Green-Case-Study-ENG.pdf)
- Review: "User-centric approaches, such as encouraging and incentivising passengers to choose less crowded vehicles or wait, have demonstrated some potential in alleviating service bunching." — [Rezazada et al. 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01441647.2024.2313969)
- Ghost buses: when trips are dropped and the real-time feed is not updated, apps show phantom buses; a feed that reflects cancellations is prerequisite for trusted passenger info. — [Stateline 2023](https://stateline.org/2023/01/31/ghost-buses-haunt-transit-agencies-and-frustrate-riders/); [GBH 2024](https://www.wgbh.org/news/local/2024-09-06/ghosted-by-the-bus-why-some-mbta-buses-never-arrive)

### Inferences
- Priority order for a low-budget city: (1) dispatcher/terminal tool (one tablet per terminus, one rule, immediate effect, no en-route compliance issue); (2) simple driver display with a single colour-coded instruction (hold X s / drive normally / ease off) — the fewer and smaller the holds, the higher the compliance; (3) passenger-facing "next two buses + crowding" to let riders self-sort. All three can share one backend (position ingestion → headway state → predictions).
- KPIs and contracts must change together with tools: drivers and supervisors evaluated on schedule adherence will ignore headway instructions (Santiago confusion, Capital Metro mindset, CTA seniority effect).

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison found that isolates cost-effectiveness of driver vs dispatcher tools on the same route.

## Key Question 6: Metrics — EWT, headway CV, bunching index — definitions and typical targets

### Takeaway
EWT = AWT − SWT, where SWT = half the scheduled headway and AWT = E[H]/2 · (1 + CV²) under random passenger arrivals; headway CV (TCQSM "headway adherence") grades LOS from <0.21 (clockwork) to ≥0.75 (most buses bunched); bunching is commonly a headway <25% of scheduled (NYCT/SFMTA/CTA) or <50% (Capital Metro), gaps >150% or >scheduled+5 min. Typical big-city network EWT is ~1 min (London 0.95–1.2 min); bunching rates 3–11% and gaps 4–18% of intervals.

### Cited Findings
- Mean wait for random arrivals: E[W] = E[H]/2 + Var(H)/(2E[H]) = 0.5·E[H]·(1+CV²); ideal (scheduled) wait uses the scheduled headway CV, and "Wexcess = W − Wideal." — [Furth & Muller 2006](http://www1.coe.neu.edu/~pfurth/Furth%20papers/2006%20Service%20reliability%20&%20hidden%20waiting%20time,%20Furth%20&%20Muller%20TRR.pdf); [Giesen CASPT 2015](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/talk49.pdf)
- Worked example: scheduled headway 10 min, observed mean 10.4 min, SD 4.46 → CV 0.43 → AWT = 0.5·10.4·(1+0.43²) = 6.14 min; SWT = 5.0; EWT = 1.14 min. — [Metro Transit reliability measures assessment (UMN)](https://cts-d10resmod-prd.oit.umn.edu/pdf/contract-14i072.pdf)
- TfL definitions: SWT, AWT, EWT ("difference between SWT and AWT... key indicator of good performance"), AWT:SWT ratio ("1.5 would mean passengers waited 50% longer than intended"), and % chance of waiting <10 / 10–20 / 20–30 / >30 min; high-frequency = ≥5 buses/h (≤12-min headway). — [TfL](https://tfl.gov.uk/forms/14144.aspx); [Currie et al. 2012](https://australasiantransportresearchforum.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2012_Currie_Douglas_Kearns.pdf)
- TCQSM headway adherence (CV of headway deviations / mean scheduled headway) LOS table: 0.00–0.21 "Service provided like clockwork"; 0.22–0.30 "Vehicles slightly off headway"; 0.31–0.39 "often off headway"; 0.40–0.52 "Irregular headways, with some bunching"; 0.53–0.74 "Frequent bunching"; ≥0.75 "Most vehicles bunched." — [UMN report](https://cts-d10resmod-prd.oit.umn.edu/pdf/contract-14i072.pdf); [Currie et al. 2012](https://australasiantransportresearchforum.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2012_Currie_Douglas_Kearns.pdf)
- Bunching: "A bus service is considered as bunching when the actual headway is less than 25% of the scheduled headway (NYCT and SFMTA)"; measure = % of trips bunched by route/direction/period. Gap thresholds e.g. 20% and 40% over scheduled headway (Metro Transit analysis). — [UMN report](https://cts-d10resmod-prd.oit.umn.edu/pdf/contract-14i072.pdf)
- Capital Metro: on-time if observed headway is 50–150% of scheduled; below = bunched, above = gapped. — [Metro Magazine 2019](https://www.metro-magazine.com/blogposts/mind-the-gap-best-practices-in-bus-headway-management)
- SFMTA: bunch = <2 min between buses; gap = scheduled headway + 5 min; 2024 target ">86% of arrivals evenly spaced." CTA bunching = headway <25% of scheduled (Active Trans analysis); CTA dashboard reports "bunched intervals" and "big gaps intervals" (3.9% / 5.0% Sept 2024). — [SFMTA 2012](https://archives.sfmta.com/cms/cmta/documents/11-9-12PAGStrategicPlanProgressReport.pdf); [SFMTA 2024](https://www.sfmta.com/media/40032/download?inline=); [UIC](https://equity82.digital.uic.edu/freq_rel.php); [Active Trans](https://activetransportation.org/map/report-card-methodology/)
- MBTA (Tribone et al. 2014) passenger-weighted: % of passengers waiting less than published headway / less than a "big gap" (lesser of 1.5× or +3 min) / less than 2× headway. — [UMN report](https://cts-d10resmod-prd.oit.umn.edu/pdf/contract-14i072.pdf)
- Singapore: EWT and On Time Adherence (−2/+5 min); incentives per 0.1 min of EWT. — [Straits Times 2017](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/transport/sbs-transit-and-smrt-earn-34-million-in-incentives-for-improving-bus-reliability)
- Transantiago: ICR (regularity) fines when headways exceed a threshold; ICF for frequency. — [Giesen CASPT 2015](http://www.rotterdam2015.caspt.org/proceedings/talk49.pdf)
- 2010 International Bus Benchmarking Group compared four regularity metrics (EWT, standard deviation, Wait Assessment, Service Regularity) across 12 operators. — [Mension & Estrada AET 2016](https://aetransport.org/public/downloads/Bv7HG/4816-57cd5cc05c897.pdf)
- Typical values: London network EWT 0.95–1.2 min (2017–2025); CTA 2.8–3.9% bunched / 4.4–5.0% big gaps (2023–24); SF Rapid 8% bunched / 15–18% gapped (2012); Chicago ward median 11% bunched (2018, independent). — sources above.

### Inferences
- For a hackathon dashboard, compute per route/direction/hour: headway CV, EWT (minutes), % bunched (<25% or <50% of scheduled), % gapped (>150% or >sched+5 min), and the TfL-style probability-of-waiting bands. EWT is the metric that translates directly into passenger-minutes and money (Singapore pays per 0.1 min; WMATA valued a 26% EWT cut at US$1M/yr).
- Reasonable targets: CV ≤0.3 (LOS B) on controlled corridors; bunching ≤5%; EWT ≤1.0 min.

### Gaps
- No universal "bunching index" exists; thresholds vary by agency (2 min, 25%, 50%), so results across cities are not directly comparable.

## Key Question 7: How to simulate this for a demo — open-source simulators, agent-based line sims, GTFS-realtime

### Takeaway
A single-line, event-based agent simulation (Poisson passenger arrivals, dwell = a + b·boardings, stochastic link times, a 120-s perturbation) reproduces bunching in minutes; several open-source Python testbeds (EXACT, RL-BusBunching, buskit, Plugboard example) already include holding controllers and RL baselines; SUMO's gtfs2pt.py imports a GTFS feed to run buses on a real OSM network; GTFS-realtime VehiclePositions parsed with gtfs-realtime-bindings gives live headways for a real-data demo.

### Cited Findings
- **EXACT** (Python, open source, companion to "An Extensible Python Open-Source Simulation Platform for Developing and Benchmarking Bus Holding Strategies"): real datasets (Chengdu Route 3; Guangzhou BRT corridor), built-in controllers: Xuan et al. 2011 simple control, Daganzo 2009 forward headway control, Wang & Sun 2021 event-graph RL, Alesiani & Gkiotsalitis 2018 target-headway RL; configured via config.yaml, run with `python main.py`. — [GitHub: chaojing-li/exact-experimental-adaptive-control-testbed-for-transit](https://github.com/chaojing-li/exact-experimental-adaptive-control-testbed-for-transit)
- **RL-BusBunching** (Xu): DQN/Double DQN/Dueling DQN/DDPG/PPO agents choosing holding time; simulator classes bus/stop/link/line/passenger; line input file with per-stop `link_length`, `lambda` (pax/min), `mean_tt`, `cv_tt`; logs headway variation, waiting, holding to wandb. — [GitHub: zhandongxu/RL-BusBunching](https://github.com/zhandongxu/RL-BusBunching)
- **buskit** (NYC SIRI/GTFS): pip-installable toolkit for streaming bus positions, historical analysis, and simulating holding/skipping strategies; dashboard to spot unstable service. — [GitHub: ywnch/buskit](https://github.com/ywnch/buskit)
- **BusBunchingRL**: PPO/TRPO (Stable-Baselines3) on a SUMO route via TraCI; actions Hold / Skip / Proceed; options for traffic on/off and buses starting bunched or evenly spaced. — [GitHub: josephgrech01/BusBunchingRL](https://github.com/josephgrech01/BusBunchingRL)
- **bus-holding-control**: DRL terminal dispatching trained on Xiamen BRT Line 2 smart-card data (June 2018); compares against uniform/schedule/manual dispatching. — [GitHub: bonny2016/bus-holding-control](https://github.com/bonny2016/bus-holding-control)
- **BusEnv** (UFBA): PettingZoo/RLlib multi-agent bus environment from Salvador (Brazil) real data; actions WAIT/MOVE; reward includes headway regularity and occupancy balance. — [GitHub: LabIA-UFBA/BusEnv](https://github.com/LabIA-UFBA/BusEnv)
- **Plugboard bus-bunching example** (event-driven): 15 stops at 400–600 m, bus speed 8 m/s, 6 buses, capacity 80, Poisson arrivals, 10-s ticks, 120-s perturbation at stop 3; a dispatch component holds the trailing bus when headway falls below threshold; "reduced headway variability (CV) by over 50% and excess passenger wait time by over 75%." — [Plugboard docs](https://docs.plugboard.dev/latest/examples/demos/transport/002_bus_terminal/bus-terminal/)
- Georgia Tech self-coordinating rule (two-parameter, uses only ETA of the following bus at each terminal): new headway = average of own and follower's headway; the project page hosted a live applet where clicking removes a bus and the rest re-equalise. — [Bartholdi project page](https://www.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/buses/buses.html)
- Delgado et al. simulation design: "event based and stochastic simulator. Each event is triggered every time a bus reaches a stop. Bus loads, travel times and position, as well as the number of passengers waiting at each stop are updated." Giesen's scenario: 30 stops, 29 signals, 72-s cycle, 2-min headway, 30 runs × 2 h with 15-min warm-up. — [Delgado et al. 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261512000653); [Giesen TREC](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1104&context=trec_seminar)
- **SUMO**: `python tools/import/gtfs/gtfs2pt.py -n osm.net.xml.gz --gtfs <feed.zip> --date YYYYMMDD --modes bus` produces `pt_vtypes.xml`, `gtfs_publictransport.add.xml` (routes/stops) and `gtfs_publictransport.rou.xml` (vehicles); needs routes/stops/stop_times/trips/calendar(_dates).txt; optional OSM ptLines for shapes. — [SUMO GTFS tutorial](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Tutorials/GTFS.html); [gtfs2pt docs](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Tools/Import/GTFS.html)
- Gkiotsalitis & Cats replicated Stockholm line 1 in SUMO from OSM and calibrated traffic flows/signal cycles with SciPy COBYLA to minimise RMSE between actual and simulated stop arrival times. — [Transportmetrica B 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21680566.2019.1606743)
- **MATSim**: large-scale agent-based transport toolbox (Java), with example project and code examples repos. — [GitHub: matsim-org/matsim-libs](https://github.com/matsim-org/matsim-libs)
- **GTFS-realtime**: protobuf `FeedMessage` → entities with `vehicle` (VehiclePosition: lat/lon, bearing, speed, `current_status`, `occupancy_status`, `timestamp`, `vehicle.id`, `trip.trip_id/route_id/direction_id`); use `gtfs-realtime-bindings` (`from google.transit import gtfs_realtime_pb2`; `feed.ParseFromString(response.content)`); key state by `vehicle.id` not `trip_id`; derive speed/bearing from successive fixes; project to metric CRS before distance math. — [MobilityData/gtfs-realtime-bindings](https://github.com/MobilityData/gtfs-realtime-bindings); [transit-data.com guide](https://www.transit-data.com/gtfs-realtime-integration/tracking-vehicle-positions-in-realtime/)
- GTFS-RT feed types: Trip updates, Service alerts, Vehicle positions. — [ACM 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3703412.3703417)
- Bartholdi & Eisenstein used NextBus GPS positions polled every 15 s for ETAs; real headway calculations can be done from stop geofences of 30–50 m radius (as Wialon/NimBus does in Baku). — [Bartholdi PDF](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/papers/Bartholdi-Eisenstein-2011.pdf); [GPS.az](https://gps.az/en/industries/transportation/public-transport/) **[VENDOR]**

### Inferences
- Minimal hackathon plan: (1) ingest GPS (GTFS-RT or raw CSV) → snap to route shape → stop-arrival events via geofence → headways per stop; (2) compute KPIs (CV, EWT, % bunched/gapped) and a time–space trajectory plot (the visual every paper uses); (3) run an event-based line simulator calibrated with observed link-time mean/CV and boarding rates; (4) implement no-control vs. terminal even-headway dispatch vs. two-way-looking en-route holding (Daganzo/Bartholdi rules) vs. optional RL (EXACT or RL-BusBunching); (5) show driver/dispatcher UI with a single hold instruction and the KPI delta; (6) stress tests: remove a bus (fleet shortage) and inject a traffic delay — demonstrate re-equalisation.
- Use the CTA guardrail pattern (max hold, max schedule deviation, fallback rule) in the demo to make the "AI" credible to operators.

### Gaps
- No public GTFS-RT feed for Baku was found; AYNA's real-time app was in beta as of Nov 2025, so a team may need operator GPS exports or synthetic data calibrated to Baku link speeds.
