# AI urban-mobility hackathon benchmarks, "better ideas" for Baku, and prototype guidance (2022–2026)

Context for the report writer: target is the youth hackathon "İnsanyönümlü şəhərlər" (Human-centred cities), co-organised by the Azerbaijan Youth Foundation and the National AI Centre within the "Year of Urban Planning and Architecture", November 2026. Confirmed format: Stage I with 150 participants presenting ideas/projects for the urban environment; Stage II = 10 finalist teams in a "Startup Demo Day" format with AI/tech expert mentoring; 3 most promising projects awarded. Registration deadline 08.11.2026. The initiative is explicitly tied to the "AI Strategy of the Republic of Azerbaijan 2025–2028" — [Edumap announcement, 30 Oct 2026](https://edumap.az/i-nsanyonumlu-seherler-hakatonu-kecirilecek). Note: no published judging rubric or challenge tracks were found for this specific event (see Gaps).

## Key Question 1 — Which AI mobility projects won or impressed at hackathons / startup competitions (2022–2026), and why?

### Takeaway
Winners cluster around five patterns: (1) real-time transit information built on open or scraped data (bus tracking, arrival prediction, occupancy); (2) computer vision that protects buses (bus-lane/bus-stop blocking detection, stop inspections); (3) demand-responsive / dynamic routing concepts; (4) matching/sharing (carpooling); and (5) analysis-heavy "research" entries (congestion pricing, coverage heatmaps). In Baku specifically, TransitHack 2024 rewarded a carpooling algorithm, an all-in-one mobility app, a congestion-pricing bottleneck study and a flood early-warning system; the Baku Metro hackathon (May 2026) rewarded passenger-flow forecasting / digital-twin concepts built on the operator's own data.

### Inferences
- The projects that win are rarely "smart traffic lights"; they are either (a) rider-facing information products on real data, or (b) operator-facing tools (enforcement, inspection, forecasting) with an obvious buyer. Baku's own TransitHack confirms that research/policy entries (congestion pricing study) can win alongside code.
- The Baku Metro hackathon shows local operators (AZCON/Baku Metropolitan) are now willing to share operational data and run pilots — a credible "pilot pathway" to cite at Demo Day.
- The 2026 Baku open-data bus-network paper demonstrates that a public-data-only analytics pipeline can produce defensible, quantified claims about Baku in weeks — a template for a hackathon team.

## Key Question 2 — What do judges reward, and what makes AI "real" vs buzzword?

### Takeaway
Published rubrics converge: working live demo (20–30%), quality/necessity of the AI (15–25%), clear problem and measurable impact (20–25%), feasibility/business model (15–25%), presentation (10–15%). Azerbaijani demo days add explicit weight to team capability, MVP readiness, market potential and commercialisation. "Real" AI means the team can name the model, its cost, its failure modes and how outputs are validated; a chatbot "bolted on" scores low.

### Cited Findings
- ITKAN AI Hackathon rubric: Problem & impact 20%, Originality 15%, Working demo 25%, Quality of AI use 25%, Presentation 15%. "Excellent" AI use = "AI does work that would be hard without it; the team can explain which model, why, roughly what it costs, where it fails, how they check its output, and how they protect user data"; weak = "AI is bolted on and adds nothing". Demo must run live on judges' own input; "admitting something is faked costs a little; hiding it and being found out costs a lot" — [ITKAN judging](https://www.itkan.one/I-Hackathon/judging).
- IEEE SmartAIthon 2026 (smart cities AI): Innovation 25, Technical implementation 25, Impact 20, Feasibility 15, Presentation 15 — [SmartAIthon](https://smartaithon.netlify.app/judging). The Devpost "AI Autonomous Smart City Hackathon 2026" uses the identical 25/25/20/15/15 split and requires GitHub repo, 2–5 min demo video, and explanation of the AI model — [Devpost](https://ai-smart-city-hackathon-2026.devpost.com/).
- Hack4SDG (GenAI): five equal 20% criteria — problem framing with evidence, prototype (teams without a technical prototype "heavily penalised"), appropriate technical component ("why is GenAI particularly suited… how does it improve on non-AI alternatives"), feasibility & impact (business model, metrics), sustainability & ethics (bias, privacy) — [Hack4SDG](https://www.hack4sdg.com/judging-criteria/).
- Mediterranean Smart Cities Hackathon 2025 rubric: Functionality of prototype 30/100 is the single largest item — [Med-SC2](https://med-sc.org/mscc2025/hackathon.php).
- DNHacks 2026: Technical execution 50, Problem/impact 25, Feasibility/deployment 25; "Best Use of AI": is AI fundamental or removable; a "simple wrapper around an existing model should generally score lower"; reliability/evaluation 25 — [DNHacks](https://portal.dnhacks.org/e/2026/judging).
- Azerbaijan "Startup Azerbaijan" Demo Day criteria: financial attractiveness, team's market knowledge, clarity of business plan, potential to lead the market, whether the team "could create a working version", team experience/motivation, promotion skills, investment plan, expected market lifetime — [startup.az](https://startup.az/menu/telebler-142).
- Startup School 3 Demo Day (Nov 2025, CAERC, jury incl. IRIA, Caucasus Ventures, SABAH.HUB, Yeni Fikir): criteria "innovation, market potential, team capabilities, and MVP readiness"; pitch stage adds development prospects, investment potential, commercialisation — [StartupSchool.az](https://startupschool.az/en/xeber/9/Jury-Panel-announced-for-the-Startup-School-3-finale); [ereforms.gov.az](https://ereforms.gov.az/en/media/xeberler/demo-day-basladi-finala-cixan-30-layihe-sergide-numayis-olunur-1958).
- PASHA Hackathon 6.0 criteria: innovation/creativity; user convenience & engagement; technical feasibility & sustainability (integration into existing infrastructure/business processes); commercialisation potential; team & presentation — [PASHA Hackathon](https://www.pashahackathon.az/criteria).
- Istanbul İBB/İETT hackathon jury criteria: applicability, UX and technical compatibility, social benefit and sustainability, design, innovation, presentation — [Tech Istanbul](https://tech.istanbul/v2/en/inovasyon-maratonunda-kazananlar-belli-oldu/).
- Azerbaijan's "Startup" certificate (SMBDA; 3-year income-tax exemption) requires an innovative, competitive product not identical to one already certified; Expert Council includes Ministry of Digital Development and Transport — [smb.gov.az](https://smb.gov.az/en/all-news/who-can-get-a-startup-certificate-and-how).

### Inferences
- For the Baku Demo Day format, expect a blend: hackathon-style technical/impact scoring in Stage I and startup-style (team, market, MVP, who pays) scoring in Stage II. Teams should prepare both a live demo and a one-slide "who pays / pilot partner" answer.
- Treat "AI" as a component with an evaluation: show baseline vs model (e.g., naive vs ML arrival prediction), error metrics, and a failure case. Judges in multiple rubrics penalise LLM wrappers.

### Gaps
- No rubric published for the Youth Foundation / National AI Centre hackathon itself; weights above are proxies.

## Key Question 3 — Evidence for alternative ideas beyond smart traffic lights and anti-bunching

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced, Baku-relevant alternatives are: bus-lane/bus-stop blocking detection (NYC ACE: +5% speed on average, up to +30%, −20% collisions; Dubai: −83% violations, +20% on-time), congestion/bottleneck pricing (London −15–18% traffic, +30% speeds, +40% bus use; Singapore −7% sustained), HOV/carpool policy (Jakarta: removing HOV raised delays 39–85%), big-data route design (Seoul Owl Bus), feeder integration (Jakarta +0.71% corridor ridership per integrated feeder route), school-run timing (school runs raise local congestion probability by 4.5 pp; morning drop-off 8.35 pp), and AI incident detection (Singapore anomaly detection AUC 0.95; Istanbul AI4LIFE cut detection time). Demand-responsive microtransit works at the margin (Via: cost/ride $8–19, 30% travel-time saving vs infrequent buses) but has high failure rates — pitch it as metro-feeder, not city-wide. Parking-guidance should NOT be pitched on "30% of traffic is cruising"; that figure is contested.

### Cited Findings

**A. Bus-lane / bus-stop violation detection (camera AI on buses or crowd-sourced)**
- NYC MTA ACE (ex-ABLE): routes with automated enforcement see bus speeds +5% on average on top of bus-lane gains, some corridors up to +30%; −20% collisions; −5–10% emissions; −40% bus stops blocked; only ~10% of violators re-offend; >1,600 buses equipped, 54 routes, 560 route-miles, 980,000 daily riders — [MTA press release](https://www.mta.info/press-release/automated-camera-enforcement-fines-begin-friday-feb-6-three-brooklyn-and-manhattan); [MTA June 2024](https://www.mta.info/press-release/mta-announces-bus-lane-camera-enforcement-expanded-include-new-violations).
- 14th Street busway M14: +24% travel-time improvement, +14% weekday riders, −42% collisions after bus-mounted cameras — [Streetsblog NYC](https://nyc.streetsblog.org/2023/04/25/smile-bus-lane-cameras-reduce-collisions-speed-up-commutes-according-to-mta-stats).
- Caveat: an independent analysis of 15–16 NYC routes found no observable speed impact two months after citations began; B26 speeds stayed ~6.1 mph — ["How Much Faster Are We Moving"](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/87d8897e-0526-4b64-8769-54eb32823548/How-Much-Faster-Are-We-Moving%2520-%2520Final.pdf). NAP/TCRP synthesis also notes MTA's +5% figure may reflect a warning-only period — [National Academies, Effects of Strategies to Enforce Bus Lanes](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/29526/chapter/2).
- NYC program mechanics useful for a prototype: ABLE issues a violation only when two buses observe the same vehicle at the same GPS location ≥5 min apart (to allow quick drop-offs); 990 cameras incl. 619 on-bus; budgeted MTA ABLE operating cost $2.7 M (2022) and $3.6 M (2023); fines $50 escalating to $250 — [NYC DOT 2024 report](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/bus-lane-camera-report.pdf).
- Earlier NYC SBS before/after: camera enforcement on Bx12, M15, M34 raised speeds 19.3%, ridership 10.6%, perceived reliability 55.3%; London on-board/stationary cameras: bus-lane speeds +5% (2004–05) and 12.6% faster than outside lanes by 2007; San Francisco TOLE cut delays 3–20% — [FTA/Virginia Tech final report](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/62781/dot_62781_DS1.pdf); [ITS KRS SF TOLE](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2020-b01514).
- Dubai RTA automated bus-lane monitoring (Phase III): −83% vehicles violating bus lanes, +20% bus on-time arrival; RTA's EC3 centre processes 75 M mobility data entries/day — [RTA Al Masar 132](https://www.rta.ae/links/magazine/masar/Al_Masar_132_Eng.pdf).
- Baku relevance: ~114 km of bus lanes on ~40 streets exist; bus speeds reportedly rose from 18 to 22 km/h by Nov 2024 — [Mincom roundtable Jan 2026](https://mincom.gov.az/en/media-en/news/roundtable-on-the-2025-state-program-for-the-improvement-of-transport-infrastructure-in-baku-and-surrounding-areas); [engrXiv preprint citing Report.az](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/8632/14011/12213). Baku's ITS centre already runs 100 illegal-parking control cameras and 60 CCTV — [UNESCAP Azerbaijan ITS deck](https://www.unescap.org/sites/default/d8files/event-documents/Azerbaijan_2.pdf).

**B. Congestion / bottleneck pricing (simulation entry)**
- London: after year one, traffic circulating in zone −15%, entering −18%; delays −25%, speeds +30%; bus use +40% — [ITS KRS](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2010-b00651). When the Western Extension was removed, traffic into the zone rose 8%, circulating +6%, speeds −3% — [NZ MoT review](https://www.transport.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/Report/DArtagnan-appendices-to-be-released.pdf).
- Singapore ERP: −7% average traffic volume sustained; −4% to −17% by charging point — same NZ review.
- Evidence review of 12 causal studies: LCC improved traffic, cut accidents (also in adjacent areas), raised housing values ~5%; Milan reduced congestion and pollution; retail prices fell 19% in Singapore ERP zones after 2010 rate hike; "improved public transport is an important policy complement" — [What Works Centre](https://whatworksgrowth.org/wp-content/uploads/Congestion_charging_-_Evidence_review.pdf).
- Meta-study lessons: exemptions (taxis, EVs) backfire; the first charge causes the biggest drop, later increases are marginal; no city priced alone — [ITS KRS meta-study](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2020-l00981). Safety: London crashes −35%/month; Stockholm −3.6%/yr — [PMC scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8545360/).
- Local precedent: a Baku TransitHack 2024 winning entry already modelled congestion pricing for a major Baku bottleneck (see KQ1).

**C. HOV / commuter carpooling and ride-matching**
- Jakarta: unexpected lifting of the "3-in-1" HOV policy raised delays 39–45% (morning) and 69–85% (evening); morning speeds fell 28→20 km/h; spillovers worsened traffic on unrestricted roads and times — [NBER w23295 (Hanna, Kreindler, Olken)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23295/w23295.pdf).
- France commuter carpooling: Karos AI matching (predicts trips with ~80% accuracy); national carpooling plan lifted monthly short-distance carpool trips from 224k (Jan 2022) to 780k (Jan 2023), 40,000 daily trips a year after launch; IDF Mobilités paid €2 per trip in a 13-month experiment; Karos reached 3.8 M trips in Île-de-France; 700,000 users in 2023 — [Drive Sweden deck](https://www.drivesweden.net/sites/default/files/2024-01/2-karos-mobility-presentation-20240123_drive-sweden.pptx.pdf); [Karos/Siemens white paper](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:d75b4eb3-a113-47e8-8237-af7b6b3f6323/Karos-whitepaper-Paris.pdf); [Tools of Change](https://toolsofchange.com/en/case-studies/detail/765/); [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/211425-commuting-becomes-faster-cheaper-cleaner-and-more-social).

**D. Big-data route design (Seoul Owl Bus) and metro-feeder optimisation**
- Seoul Owl Bus: route design from 3 billion KT mobile call logs (1 month) and 5 M taxi trips; 7 draft routes analysed, 5 revised; pilot carried 218,212 passengers in 3 months (175/day per bus, 65 more than regular buses); satisfaction 80–82 vs 74 for regular buses; by 2016 ~7,900–9,900 passengers/night, ~2.3 M fewer car trips/yr, ~500 t CO2/yr, ₩14.1 bn benefits, taxi refusal −8.9% — [Development Asia](https://www.development.asia/case-study/using-big-data-design-night-bus-routes); [Seoul Solution](https://seoulsolution.kr/en/content/7595).
- Jakarta JakLingko/Mikrotrans feeders: each Mikrotrans route integrated with a Transjakarta corridor raised corridor ridership by 0.71% (≈213 new daily passengers on a 30,000/day corridor) 2018–19; fleet +186% and routes +138% in 2019; integration judged cheaper than new BRT corridors — [Dharmawan 2022](https://doi.org/10.25104/mtm.v20i1.2016); [ITDP Jakarta case](https://itdp.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/MOBILIZE-Jakarta-Case-Study-11.16.21_pages.pdf).
- Baku context: ADB says metro carries ~630,000/day, expansion ($2.6 bn; ADB $850 M loan under preparation) is justified by "heavy traffic congestion"; the consultant found weak Baku–Absheron integration and 17% of residents with limited access; new network launched Jan 2026 with 8 routes, ~2,800 buses expected — [ADB 59411-001](https://www.adb.org/projects/59411-001/main); [engrXiv preprint](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/8632/14011/12213). AYNA reports taxi-to-metro trips fell sharply after taxi reforms and average trip length rose 8.5→10 km — [AYNA activities](https://ayna.gov.az/az/activities/mobillik-ve-elcatanliq-23).

**E. Demand-responsive microtransit (Via, Shotl, Citymapper) — mixed evidence**
- Seattle "Via to Transit": average trip 19.4 min vs 27.3 min if diverted to existing buses (+30%); no advantage for trips <1.5 miles; cost-effective in generalised cost ($5,278 vs $6,729) — [ITS KRS 2023](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2023-b01746).
- UTA (Salt Lake) Via pilot Q1: cost per rider fell $26.91→$15.54 as utilisation rose 1.3→2.3 riders/vehicle-hour; wait 9–12 min; target $13.08 — [UTA Q1 report](https://rideuta.com/-/media/Files/About-UTA/Reports/Via/Final_UTA_microtransit_pilot_Q1_report.pdf). BCG: Via services 3.6–4.3 passengers/vehicle-hour, $8–11 per ride, farebox 20% in West Sacramento — [BCG 2019](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2019/on-demand-transit-can-unlock-urban-mobility).
- Rimini Shuttlemare (Shotl; SCEWC 2025 finalist): >1,000 passengers/week rising to 2,200; 9-min average wait; 60% of users would otherwise have driven/moped — [Shotl](https://shotl.com/case-studies/rimini); [Comune di Rimini](https://www.comune.rimini.it/novita/notizie/lo-shuttlemare-comodo-puntuale-soddisfacente). Local critics: €1.64 M spent 2021–25; €417k in 2025 for ~50,000 riders ≈ €8.34/ride — [Riminiduepuntozero](https://www.riminiduepuntozero.it/shuttlemare-la-resa-dei-costi-in-5-anni-spesi-piu-di-16-milioni-di-euro-soldi-pubblici-che-potevano-essere-investiti-nei-parcheggi/).
- Failure rates: of 120 DRT schemes over 40 years, 50% lasted <7 years, 40% <3 years; recent tech microtransit 50% failed within 2 years; many-to-many designs failed 72% vs 44% for many-to-few; ICT-era cost/vehicle-hour ~$101, double the previous era — [Currie & Fournier, Why most DRT/micro-transits fail](https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/bitstream/handle/2123/27188/Why%20most%20DRT%3AMicro-Transits%20fail.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1). Citymapper shut its London SmartRide/Ride service in 2019 — [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/6/12/18662577/citymapper-london-transport-app-shut-down-smartride-ride-to-focus-on-pass).
- UNECE's Azerbaijan action plan (2024–25) includes assessing on-demand shuttle services in rural areas and extending GPS live tracking to the top-10 bus providers — [UNECE Action Plan for Azerbaijan](https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2025-08/2503622_E_PDF_WEB.pdf).

**F. School-run staggering / timing**
- Beijing (5-min speed data, triple-difference): school runs raise the probability of local congestion by 4.5 pp; 8.35 pp at drop-off vs 2.96 pp at pick-up, implying staggered hours help; annual congestion cost ≈ US$148 M — [Regional Science & Urban Economics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016604622030291X). Another Beijing study: congestion index 20% lower on workdays during school holidays — cited in [arXiv 2608.18785](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18785).
- Bangkok agent-based model: removing school trips raises 7 a.m. speeds 8.1%; staggered school schedules +6.9% speed at 7 a.m.; flexible work hours only +1.9% — [Sustainability 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/23/16244).
- Jakarta 2009: 30-min earlier school start promised up to −14% morning congestion but was widely criticised ("roads still jammed"); staggered working hours study forecast −4.5–8% peak flattening — [ITDP](https://itdp.org/2009/01/07/finding-a-way-out-of-jakartas-jams/); [EASTS Jakarta TDM evaluation](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/eastpro/2009/0/2009_0_15/_pdf/-char/en).

**G. Parking guidance / cruising**
- Shoup's "30% of traffic is cruising" is a straight average of 16 studies (1927 onward) in places with underpriced, overcrowded curb parking (range 8–74%; search 3.5–13.9 min); Shoup himself calls the generalised meme "apocryphal" — [Shoup, Pricing curb parking](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0xc27671/qt0xc27671.pdf). Modern estimates: <1% in San Francisco, 15% in central Stuttgart, 36 s average search in the Netherlands — [Millard-Ball et al. 2020](https://millardball.its.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/22/2022/06/Millard-Ball_Hampshire_Weinberger_2020_Curious_lack_of_cruising_for_parking_preprint.pdf); [Hampshire & Shoup 2018](https://doi.org/10.3828/jtep.2018.52.3.184).
- SFpark (2011–12) price changes reduced average occupancy but did not consistently increase availability or reduce cruising — [Pierce & Shoup critique in TRP](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0739885914000067).
- Baku has an "Azparking" app and 100 illegal-parking cameras, so curb-management data exists in principle — [AYNA](https://ayna.gov.az/az/activities/mobillik-ve-elcatanliq-23); [UNESCAP deck](https://www.unescap.org/sites/default/d8files/event-documents/Azerbaijan_2.pdf).

**H. AI incident detection from cameras**
- Singapore AI digital-twin framework: anomaly incident detection ROC-AUC 0.9526, F1 85.4%; weather detection 86.2% — [ICECET 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/icecet61485.2024.10698642). LTA predictive coordination: 7–12% reduction in peak commuting delays, faster incident clearance via EMAS — [JOIT 2025](https://doi.org/10.61453/joit.v2025no12).
- Advanced (ML, continuous-recording) cameras in a Chinese metropolis cut accidents 8.3% near equipped intersections vs conventional cameras — [Management Science 2025](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.00575).
- Moscow ITS (2,000+ cameras, 3,700 detectors, 6,000 signals): average speed 45→51 km/h (2010–16); TomTom rank fell from 1st to 13th — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.org/industries/travel-logistics-and-infrastructure/our-insights/building-smart-transport-in-moscow).
- Baku: the MIA Intelligent Transport Management Centre (NİİM) recorded 384 crashes in 3 months (2024) within camera coverage, concentrated on Ziya Bünyadov, Heydar Aliyev, Babek, Tbilisi avenues and Baku–Sumgayit, Airport highways; ~500 broken-down vehicles and 400+ crashes in 5 months of another year; operators relay to police manually — [Baku.TV](https://baku.tv/cemiyyet/bakida-3-ayda-384-yol-neqliyyat-hadisesi-bas-verib-niim); [Oxu.az](https://oxu.az/ru/obshestvo/umnye-svetofory-novye-kamery-kakie-novshestva-dlya-spaseniya-ot-probok-poyavyatsya-na-dorogah-v-baku). NİİM is building a 3D map/navigation app (6,235 km filmed) that will use phones as speed sensors and later show camera feeds — [Tech.az 2023](https://tech.az/en/posts/a-new-application-is-being-developed-to-prevent-traffic-jams-in-baku-3551).

**I. Digital twins / network redesign**
- AYNA has built a digital twin of Baku; 60+ mobility initiatives reviewed; 20+ investment projects simulated on it; the new bus network was simulated on the twin — [AYNA](https://ayna.gov.az/az/activities/mobillik-ve-elcatanliq-23); [APA 2024](https://en.apa.az/climate-news/160-new-electric-buses-will-be-brought-to-baku-by-land-transport-agency-442481).
- Barcelona superblocks: design assumed >20% city-wide traffic "evaporation"; only three pilots implemented; evaluations show benefits on pedestrianised streets but rebound on surrounding streets; authors argue superblocks need a city-wide measure like a congestion charge — [Benavides et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41841033).

### Inferences
- For Baku, "AI that protects the existing 114 km of bus lanes" (bus-mounted or crowd-sourced violation detection + hotspot analytics) has the strongest evidence-to-cost ratio and an obvious owner (AYNA/BakuBus/NİİM).
- A congestion-pricing or school-run simulation entry is cheap to prototype with SUMO/MATSim and OSM, and has precedent in winning a Baku hackathon; it should be framed as a decision-support twin, not a policy demand.
- Microtransit should be positioned narrowly (many-to-few metro feeders for Absheron settlements) and benchmarked honestly against cost/ride (~$8–19) and failure statistics.
- Parking-guidance pitches must replace the 30% meme with a measured local hotspot (e.g., GPS-trace or camera-based search-time estimate).

### Gaps
- No quantified result found for Dubai or Moscow *AI incident detection* specifically (Dubai figures are bus-lane monitoring; Moscow figures are aggregate ITS).
- No published before/after evaluation of Baku's 114 km bus lanes beyond the 18→22 km/h press figure.
- Helsinki digital-twin traffic results not researched within call budget.

## Key Question 4 — Data and tools for a 1–2 week prototype in Baku

### Takeaway
Baku has more usable public transport data than teams assume: AYNA's map API publishes routes, stops, stop sequences and shapes (scraped snapshot and GTFS exports already on GitHub/Zenodo); BakuBus daily boardings per route and metro station entries are on opendata.az; GoMap offers a public-transport routing API; Yandex Maps shows live traffic for Baku. There is no official GTFS or GTFS-realtime feed, no public vehicle positions and no public OD data. TomTom/INRIX indices do not include Baku, so congestion baselines must come from Numbeo-style surveys, Yandex/Google traffic layers, or the team's own YOLO counts on video.

### Cited Findings
- AYNA "Map AYNA" (map.ayna.gov.az) publishes bus routes, stops, carriers, tariffs and a "give feedback" function; updated May 2025 — [AYNA news](https://www.ayna.gov.az/az/news/avtobuslarla-bagli-melumatlar-map-aynada-739); [map.ayna.gov.az](https://map.ayna.gov.az/).
- The AYNA map API (map-api.ayna.gov.az) exposes 208 routes, 3,841 stops and 398 route shapes; scraped snapshot (6 May 2026, github.com/samirmehd1yev/best_route_finding) and a full reproducible pipeline + GTFS exports of today's network (github.com/ismatsamadov/route_efficiency, Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23604370). Other inputs used: BakuBus daily boardings per route (Jan–Sep 2026) and metro daily station entries/coordinates on opendata.az (CC BY), Kontur H3 population grid, OSM destinations via Overpass. Timetables, vehicle positions and OD flows are not public — [engrXiv preprint](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/8632/14011/12213).
- GoMap (Azerbaijani map provider) API: route planning, geocoding, `getTrafficTilesByCoord` (traffic tiles), and `getGraphHopperBusRoute` returning multimodal bus/metro/walk itineraries with stops and GeoJSON geometry; requires a personal GUID key — [GoMap API doc](https://gomap.az/info/api/extra_api/API_AZ.pdf).
- Yandex Maps provides a live traffic layer for Azerbaijan (yandex.az/maps/traffic) — [Yandex.az](https://yandex.az/maps/traffic/); Yandex Router API covers "Russia, the CIS and Turkey" with public-transport routing and traffic forecasts; pricing from $1,625/yr for 250k requests — [Yandex Router API](https://yandex.com/maps-api/products/router-api).
- TomTom Traffic APIs claim global coverage with 30-second updates, including Junction Analytics and O/D Analysis APIs — [TomTom](https://www.tomtom.com/products/traffic-apis/). But Baku is in neither the TomTom Traffic Index (500 cities) nor INRIX Scorecard (900 cities); the only public congestion figure is Numbeo's crowdsourced index (Traffic Index 149.9, 38-min average one-way trip in the 2025 table; 146.05 / 37.89 min / 14.16 km as of Jan 2026) — [Numbeo 2025](https://www.numbeo.com/traffic/rankings.jsp?title=2025); [geyin.az analysis](https://geyin.az/en/blog/post/in-a-big-city-time-is-the-real-currency-traffic-shopping-and/).
- Baku ITS inventory (MIA NİİM): 150 signal controllers, 344 traffic-flow/counting sensors, 60 CCTV, 100 illegal-parking cameras, 60 VMS, 243 bus info terminals, 27 speed gates; cashless payment on 1,847 of 2,100 buses + metro; centre manages 2,000 buses — [UNESCAP deck](https://www.unescap.org/sites/default/d8files/event-documents/Azerbaijan_2.pdf); [President.az 2011](https://president.az/en/articles/view/4048). Public access to these camera feeds was not found (see Gaps).
- SUMO has official tutorials for OSM import (osmWebWizard), public transport from OSM, GTFS import, taxi/DRT services, OD traffic and parking search — [SUMO tutorials](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Tutorials/index.html). CityFlow is a faster multi-agent RL traffic simulator (benchmarked vs SUMO) with a Python interface — [CityFlow GitHub](https://github.com/cityflow-project/CityFlow/). UrbanFlow-AI shows an end-to-end OSM → SUMO → TraCI AI signal controller with 3D web viewer, including buses and stops — [GitHub](https://github.com/TheAndreyZakharov/UrbanFlow-AI).
- YOLOv8 vehicle counting on public traffic cameras is a solved template: TfL JamCams pipeline (frame grab every ~5 s, YOLOv8, per-camera counts on a map) — [Quix demo](https://github.com/quixio/computer-vision-demo/); Ho Chi Minh City public cameras → YOLOv8 → Kafka/Spark → Streamlit dashboard — [GitHub](https://github.laiyagushi.com/thuan-hm/Traffic-and-Weather-Data-Streaming-Pipeline); YOLOv8s + ByteTrack + counting lines + anomaly alerts + React dashboard with a synthetic "demo mode" that needs no camera — [GitHub](https://github.com/salvirezwan/Traffic-Video-Analytics-Project); Roboflow InferencePipeline guide using NYC DOT public webcams — [Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/blog/video-stream-analysis). Licensing note: YOLO-World/Ultralytics models are AGPL-3.0 for self-hosting — same Roboflow post.
- A hackathon-grade YOLOv8 + SUMO + Streamlit adaptive-signal project with four virtual CCTV cameras illustrates a demo architecture (camera → detection → lane counts → controller → SUMO → dashboard) — [AI-Traffic-System GitHub](https://github.com/termux37/AI-Traffic-System).
- Judges' bar for AI: explain model, cost, failure modes and validation (see KQ2 ITKAN rubric).

### Inferences
- A credible 1–2 week stack for Baku: AYNA map API/GTFS export + opendata.az boardings + OSM/Kontur for network and demand; SUMO (or the preprint's Python pipeline) for scenario simulation; Yandex/GoMap traffic tiles for live congestion overlays; YOLOv8 on the team's own phone video (or any publicly reachable stream) for counting/violation demos; Streamlit or React for the dashboard; an LLM only as an explanation layer over computed results.
- Because vehicle positions aren't public, anti-bunching or ETA products are hard to validate in Baku; network-design, enforcement-analytics, feeder-design and pricing-simulation entries are easier to make "real".
- Positioning data gaps as a product feature (e.g., "we generate the GTFS/GTFS-RT Baku lacks") aligns with the UNECE action plan item on extending live GPS tracking.

### Gaps
- No public Baku traffic-camera streams or APIs were found; teams should assume they must record their own footage or request NİİM/AYNA data via the organisers.
- Google Maps traffic/Routes API coverage quality in Azerbaijan was not verified.
- GTFS-realtime for Baku: none found.

## Key Question 5 — Business/sustainability angle: who pays, cost benchmarks, pilot pathway

### Takeaway
The realistic payers are AYNA (bus network, bus lanes, Azparking, digital twin owner), Baku Metropolitan CJSC (already running hackathon→pilot pipelines), the MIA's NİİM (cameras/incidents) and the State Program for Baku transport 2025–2030 backed by ADB ($850 M–$1 bn metro loan), EIB Global (metro TA) and potentially EBRD (e-bus fleets; EBRD says it is "highly interested" in Azerbaijan e-buses but Baku is not an EBRD Green City — Ganja is). Cost anchors from abroad: NYC bus-mounted enforcement ~$2.7–3.6 M/yr operating for 450–1,000 buses; Barcelona pilots €97–100k each; Rimini on-demand ~€8/ride; Via $8–19/ride.

### Cited Findings
- State Program on the Improvement of Transport Infrastructure in Baku City and Surrounding Areas 2025–2030 approved 30 Jan 2025; ADB preparing an $850 M OCR loan for the $2.6 bn metro expansion (20 km, 9 stations, 300 cars; +25 M passengers/yr by cutting headway 2:00→1:45) — [ADB 59411-001](https://www.adb.org/projects/59411-001/main); ADB $700k TA for metro decarbonisation and "smart operation" — [ADB TAR](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/project-documents/59165/59165-001-tar-en.pdf).
- EIB Global MoU (COP29, Nov 2024) for technical assistance on Baku metro upgrade; micromobility lanes ~32 km — [EC Enlargement](https://enlargement.ec.europa.eu/news/cop29-eib-global-support-azerbaijan-planning-baku-metro-upgrade-2024-11-14_en).
- EBRD: Ganja (not Baku) is the Azerbaijani EBRD Green City (joined 2020; GCAP projects in lighting and waste) — [EBRD Green Cities Ganja](https://www.ebrdgreencities.com/our-cities/cities/ganja). EBRD lent €6 M (2018) to private Baku operator Khaliq Faiqoglu for 35 Euro-6 buses — [EBRD](https://www.ebrd.com/home/news-and-events/news/2018/ebrd-supports-modernisation-of-private-bus-operator-in-azerbaijan.html). EBRD Green Cities head (May 2026): "highly interested" in financing e-bus fleets, depots and charging in Azerbaijan; ready to lend directly to cities/utilities — [Report.az May 2026](https://report.az/en/finance/ebrd-ready-to-finance-projects-to-develop-electric-bus-fleets-in-azerbaijan); [Report.az Oct 2025](https://report.az/en/finance/ebrd-ready-to-lend-directly-to-cities-and-utilities-in-azerbaijan).
- World Bank Baku Urban Mobility Policy Note (2018) frames priorities: integrated multimodal system for Greater Baku, public and non-motorised transport, congestion management, financial sustainability — [World Bank](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/963521580136216965-0080022020/original/BakuUrbanMobilityPolicyNoteJune2018.pdf). Baku has ~4.8 M motorised trips/day; bus system ~1.6 M passengers/day — [ITF/OECD 2020](https://doi.org/10.1787/64b18b6a-en).
- Bus sector economics: COVID cut bus journeys ~70% vs 2019 (to ~300 M) and ~70% of routes were unprofitable; UNECE plan calls for a subsidy model and consolidation of operators to 5–10 by 2030, gross-cost contracts — [UNECE Action Plan](https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2025-08/2503622_E_PDF_WEB.pdf). Daily buses in service rose 1,720 (2022) → ~2,300 (2026); 395 new electric/CNG buses; cashless buses 1,000→1,800; ridership +67.3 M over four years — [Mincom Jan 2026](https://mincom.gov.az/en/media-en/news/roundtable-on-the-2025-state-program-for-the-improvement-of-transport-infrastructure-in-baku-and-surrounding-areas).
- Operators as buyers: Baku Metropolitan moved hackathon winners into pilot testing and internships (venture-clienting model) — [SUP.VC](https://sup.vc/metro-hackathon). Barcelona's venture-client model paid €97,200 (80% of cost) and €100,000 (50%) for 6–16-month pilots — [Barcelona Digital City](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/digital/en/news/news/turning-bus-stops-into-data-centres-and-cameras-with-ai-to-improve-bus-lane-circulation-the-two-winning-projects-by-barcelona-innova-mobility-lab-1415817).
- Cost anchors: NYC ABLE budgeted operating costs $2.715 M (2022) / $3.61 M (2023); fine revenue goes to a transit fund — [NYC DOT](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/bus-lane-camera-report.pdf). Rimini on-demand €417k/yr for ~50k rides — [Riminiduepuntozero](https://www.riminiduepuntozero.it/shuttlemare-la-resa-dei-costi-in-5-anni-spesi-piu-di-16-milioni-di-euro-soldi-pubblici-che-potevano-essere-investiti-nei-parcheggi/). Via $8–19 per ride — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2019/on-demand-transit-can-unlock-urban-mobility); [UTA](https://rideuta.com/-/media/Files/About-UTA/Reports/Via/Final_UTA_microtransit_pilot_Q1_report.pdf).
- Startup certificate (3-year tax exemption) is available for innovative micro/small businesses — [smb.gov.az](https://smb.gov.az/en/all-news/who-can-get-a-startup-certificate-and-how).

### Inferences
- The most convincing Demo Day "who pays" story: a pilot sold to AYNA/BakuBus or Baku Metropolitan under the State Program 2025–2030, with IFI programs (ADB/EIB/EBRD) as scale-up finance and fine revenue (for enforcement analytics) as a self-funding mechanism.
- "Cost per bus" (camera + edge compute) and "cost per intersection" benchmarks were not found for Baku; teams should derive from NYC (≈$3.6 M/yr for ~1,000 buses ≈ $3.6k/bus/yr operating) and flag it as an estimate.

### Gaps
- No Baku-specific per-intersection or per-bus procurement costs were found.
- Whether Baku intends to join EBRD Green Cities was not confirmed; only Ganja is listed.
